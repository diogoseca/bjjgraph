<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Keep a useful upper-body frame before the shoulder connection closes. Prevent the upper arm from separating from the torso while Jersey control is forming. Clear the arm before the high leg closes around the shoulder. If the entry stalls, maintain your base above the remaining Z-Guard. If the high connection opens fully, recover posture inside the ordinary closed guard.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Z-Guard]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Z Guard to Carni?</p>

- Begin underneath in Z-Guard with a stable knee-shield connection and access to the opponent's shoulder.
- Use the Jersey intermediate taught with this entry rather than treating knee-shield contact as a completed Carni.
- Use the frame and hip angle to give the high leg a clear path around the head without pulling on a fixed knee.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Z Guard to Carni?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: If the high connection opens fully, recover posture inside the ordinary closed guard.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Prevent the upper arm from separating from the torso while Jersey control is forming.
