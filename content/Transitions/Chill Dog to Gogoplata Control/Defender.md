<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Recognize the cleared-neck angle before the shin enters. Keep a useful inside frame while the enclosure is forming. Protect neck alignment without pulling against the raised leg. If the new enclosure fails, recognize retained Chill Dog control. If the high connection clears, recover posture inside ordinary guard.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Rubber Guard/Chill Dog]] (Bottom)

</section>

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Chill Dog to Gogoplata Control?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: If the high connection clears, recover posture inside ordinary guard.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Keep a useful inside frame while the enclosure is forming.
