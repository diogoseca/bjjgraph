<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The bottom player faces the opponent in a high two-leg clinch with the target arm still inside. Opening enough of the second-leg reinforcement to pummel is a prerequisite. New York is defined by arm isolation, not a collar grip.

Keep the first high leg and hand connected while adjusting the second leg. Open the reinforcing leg as needed without losing all posture control. Work through the opening near the hand rather than forcing a tightly clamped elbow straight outward.

Guide the wrist and forearm through the available space without bending individual fingers. Cover the cleared arm and close the knee hug as the hand reaches the mat. Once the arm is isolated, maintain that new control instead of repeatedly treating Zombie as a separate leg entanglement.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

Use hip movement to keep the knee close as the cleared arm is secured with the New York overhook. The isolated arm can support later submission entries, but guard retention comes first if the top player withdraws posture and the high connection opens.
