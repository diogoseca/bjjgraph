<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Keep an upper-body frame while the passer changes the angle. Track the passer's hips with your knees without holding a twisting leg entanglement. Recover inside knee space before the passer clears the last hook. If the weave stays connected, resist the angle change with frames rather than forcing the knee. If the weave releases, retain a half-guard leg connection and prevent chest consolidation.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Leg Weave]] (Top)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Backstep from Leg Weave?</p>

- You are the top passer. The arm connecting the opponent's legs defines the weave, not a leg threaded through a trap.
- Control the upper body while keeping your weight supported; do not hang on the opponent's neck.
- Before backstepping, make sure the moving leg and knee can turn without a retained hook fixing the lower leg.


</section>

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Backstep from Leg Weave?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: If the weave releases, retain a half-guard leg connection and prevent chest consolidation.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Track the passer's hips with your knees without holding a twisting leg entanglement.
