<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The bottom player is already facing the opponent in Zombie, with a high rubber-guard leg connection. The player begins facing the opponent in rubber guard; the reset does not free a lockdown on the opponent's leg.

The bottom player has broken posture and supports a high Mission Control leg. The top player's hand or forearm is still obstructing the intended arm isolation. Release any wrist, neck or elbow loading before opening the high-leg enclosure. Lower the controlling leg through a comfortable hip angle rather than pulling the foot against the knee.

Bring the forearms and knees between the bodies as space becomes available. Keep the shoulders and hips oriented toward the opponent while re-establishing a comfortable facing guard. Check posture, knee comfort and inside space before resuming a sweep or submission attack.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

Once the high-leg connection has opened, use a small shrimp with the supporting frames to recover an inside knee. Move the hips through free space instead of pulling a trapped knee sideways.

Treat the failed pummel as a guard retention problem: keep the forearm between the bodies as the knee returns. It supports the hip escape against chest pressure; if the cleared arm is still available, the New York overhook can be rebuilt only after posture and knee space are secure.
