<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**Demi Guard to Lapel Guard releases the foot-on-lapel configuration while preserving useful gi grips and leg frames, returning the bottom player to broader lapel-guard options.**

Demi addresses the passer who comes forward. When distance opens again, the guard player can stop insisting on that close knee-line configuration and retain a more general lapel guard. The source introduction explains the relationship between forward pressure and leaving the worm configuration: [Keenan's introduction](https://jiujitsux.com/courses/keenans-lapel-encyclopedia/lesson/16-1-introduction-to-the-demi-guard/).

This recovery ends at [[Positions/Lapel Guard]], whose own choices include [[Transitions/Lapel Sweep to Back]], [[Transitions/Lapel Omoplata Entry]] and [[Transitions/Lapel Triangle Setup]]. Those are subsequent exchanges with their own entry requirements, not direct finishes from Demi. Keeping usable fabric and a leg barrier is what makes that continuation possible.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]] (Bottom)
**Success Rate**: gi only

## Playing as Attacker

[[Demi Guard to Lapel Guard/Attacker|→ Full Attacker Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Keep useful fabric control during the change.

- Use the space created by the passer's retreat.

- Replace foot contact with a working leg barrier.

- Stay facing the passer while changing the connection.

- Treat a later attack as a separate setup.

### Common Mistakes


- **Dropping the lapel while changing the foot**
  - Consequence: The intended lapel-guard continuation loses its main control.
  - Correction: Retain a useful grip while replacing the leg barrier.

- **Removing the foot without a replacement frame**
  - Consequence: The passer can close the gap directly to your torso.
  - Correction: Arrange another guard frame during the foot change.

- **Keeping the old wrap tighter than the movement permits**
  - Consequence: Your own leg cannot move into the recovered guard.
  - Correction: Release only the tension that obstructs the change.

<section id="defender-summary" class="content-section">

## Playing as Defender

[[Demi Guard to Lapel Guard/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Recognize the change of collar, lapel and foot controls early.

- Keep a usable base or leg barrier while contesting the movement.

- Choose a response that fits the current controls rather than a lost grip.

- Release any connection that binds a knee during movement.

- Identify the resulting position before continuing the exchange.


### Recognition Cues


- The guard player keeps lapel contact as you move away.

- The foot leaves the tail while another leg frame replaces it.

- Their hips follow your direction to keep both knees in front.


### Defensive Options


- **Keep the existing configuration contained** - When: When the bottom player cannot make room for a new frame

- **Contest the frame change before it settles** - When: As the foot leaves the lapel tail

- **Clear the grip and legs through an exposed gap** - When: Only if the guard player has abandoned both useful fabric and leg frames
