<section id="overview" class="content-section">

As the defender in this scenario, you are the bottom butterfly guard player whose opponent is attempting to clear one of your hooks to advance to butterfly half guard top. Your primary objective is to maintain both butterfly hooks and prevent the positional downgrade from full butterfly guard to the less favorable butterfly half guard. Successful defense requires early recognition of the clearing attempt, active hook retention through hip movement and foot positioning, and the ability to counter with sweeps or transitions that punish the opponent's weight commitment during their clearing sequences.

The defensive framework operates on three levels: prevention through active hook pressure and upper body control that makes clearing difficult, reaction through immediate hook re-insertion when a clear is partially successful, and counter-offense through sweep attempts and guard transitions that capitalize on the opponent's shifting weight distribution during their clearing sequences. The best defenders combine all three levels, creating a layered defensive system where preventing the clear is the first priority, recovering is the backup, and counter-attacking is the reward for the opponent's failed attempt.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Butterfly Guard]] (Top)

<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Butterfly Guard to Butterfly Half Guard?</p>

- Opponent drives knees together compressing your hook structure, reducing the lateral space your hooks need for effective elevation
- Opponent establishes crossface and begins turning your head away from one side while shifting weight toward the opposite hook
- Opponent's hip pressure increases on one side as they attempt to pin your knee toward the mat to collapse hook angle
- Opponent breaks your upper body grips or underhook on one side and begins driving forward shoulder pressure asymmetrically
- Opponent starts backstep motion or begins lifting one leg to step past your hook line


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Butterfly Guard to Butterfly Half Guard?</p>

- Maintain constant upward elevator pressure through both hooks, treating passive hooks as the primary vulnerability that enables clearing
- Keep seated upright posture to maximize the structural connection between your upper body grips and lower body hooks
- Follow the opponent's hip movement with your corresponding hook, using hip rotation to track their clearing attempts
- Establish dominant upper body control through underhooks or collar ties that prevent the crossface needed for hook clearing
- Counter-attack immediately when the opponent commits weight to one side for hook clearing, exploiting the resulting imbalance
- Transition proactively to X-Guard or single leg X when you feel a hook being compromised rather than fighting a losing retention battle


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Butterfly Guard to Butterfly Half Guard?</p>

### 1. Active hook retention through hip rotation and following movement

- **When to use**: As soon as you feel the opponent attempting to compress or clear either hook through knee pinching or hip pressure
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Butterfly Guard]]
- **If successful**: Maintain full butterfly guard with both hooks active and reset to neutral position with offensive options preserved
- **Risk**: If you focus too much on retention without counter-attacking, the opponent will repeatedly attempt clearing until successful

### 2. Counter-sweep during opponent's weight commitment to clearing side

- **When to use**: When the opponent shifts weight toward one hook for clearing, creating an imbalance that can be exploited with a sweep in the opposite direction
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **If successful**: Sweep the opponent and establish top position, completely reversing the positional hierarchy
- **Risk**: Failed sweep attempt may leave you in a worse position with compromised hooks and broken posture

### 3. Transition to X-Guard or single leg X when hook is partially cleared

- **When to use**: When one hook is being successfully cleared and retention is failing, use the remaining hook to enter X-Guard before the opponent settles into butterfly half guard
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Butterfly Guard]]
- **If successful**: Establish X-Guard which offers superior sweeping mechanics and additional guard options from a new angle
- **Risk**: Requires giving up the half guard trap on the cleared leg, committing fully to an open guard transition

### 4. Underhook fight to prevent crossface establishment

- **When to use**: When opponent begins grip fighting for crossface position, which is the prerequisite for most hook clearing sequences
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Butterfly Guard]]
- **If successful**: Maintain upright posture with dominant underhook, preventing the upper body control the opponent needs to clear hooks
- **Risk**: Intense grip fighting can fatigue your arms and leave you reactive rather than proactive with sweep attempts

<p class="section-subtitle">What is the best outcome when defending Butterfly Guard to Butterfly Half Guard?</p>

### → [[Positions/Butterfly Guard]]

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Butterfly Guard to Butterfly Half Guard?</p>

### 1. Maintaining passive hooks without active upward elevator pressure during opponent's clearing attempts

- **Consequence**: Passive hooks are easily cleared because they offer no resistance to the opponent's knee compression and hip driving mechanics, resulting in rapid transition to butterfly half guard
- **Correction**: Drive hooks actively upward at all times, treating constant elevator pressure as the primary defense. Your hooks should feel like they are lifting the opponent even when not executing a sweep, creating resistance against clearing attempts.

### 2. Allowing opponent to establish crossface without immediately fighting to recover head position

- **Consequence**: Crossface turns your head away and breaks the kinetic chain between your upper body and hooks, making hook retention on the crossface side nearly impossible
- **Correction**: Fight the crossface immediately by turning into it, establishing your own underhook on the crossface side, or framing against their shoulder. Never accept a crossface passively, as it is the gateway to successful hook clearing.

### 3. Lying flat on back instead of maintaining seated posture when opponent applies forward pressure

- **Consequence**: Flat position eliminates leverage for hook retention and sweeping, making it easy for opponent to compress and clear hooks with simple forward pressure
- **Correction**: Maintain seated or semi-reclined posture by engaging core muscles and using underhook or collar tie to stay upright. If flattened, immediately work to re-establish seated posture before the opponent can capitalize on your compromised structure.

### 4. Failing to transition when hook clearing is clearly succeeding and retention is failing

- **Consequence**: Ending up in butterfly half guard bottom in a defensive scramble rather than proactively transitioning to X-Guard or deep half from a controlled position
- **Correction**: Recognize early when a hook is being successfully cleared and proactively transition to X-Guard, single leg X, or deep half guard rather than fighting a losing retention battle. Early transition preserves offensive initiative.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train defense against Butterfly Guard to Butterfly Half Guard?</p>

**Phase 1: Hook Retention Awareness** - Recognizing clearing attempts and maintaining active hooks
Partner performs slow-motion hook clearing sequences while you focus on feeling the compression, weight shifts, and positional changes. Practice maintaining active upward hook pressure and following hip movement. No sweeps or transitions, purely defensive retention.

**Phase 2: Counter-Timing Development** - Identifying sweep windows during clearing attempts
Partner attempts hook clearing at moderate speed while you practice identifying the weight commitment moments and executing counter-sweeps. Focus on the timing connection between their clearing commitment and your sweep initiation. Work on both same-side and opposite-side counter-sweep directions.

**Phase 3: Transition Flow Under Pressure** - Proactive transitions when retention fails
Partner successfully clears hooks at realistic speed and you practice flowing to X-Guard, deep half guard, or lockdown rather than accepting butterfly half guard passively. Develop the habit of transitioning proactively within three seconds of feeling a hook being cleared rather than fighting a losing retention battle.

**Phase 4: Full Resistance Integration** - Combining retention, counters, and transitions against live resistance
Positional sparring starting in full butterfly guard against partner actively working to clear hooks and pass. Integrate retention, counter-sweeps, and proactive transitions based on real-time reads. Track how often you maintain full butterfly versus transition versus get passed to identify areas for improvement.
