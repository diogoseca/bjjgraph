<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The top player is the choke defender even though this transition calls the moving player its attacker. Changing pins does not itself prove that the choke is gone. Clear the arm-and-leg loop first, and do not lift or slam the partner to escape.

Watch for an arm reaching past your head and connecting to the same-side leg. Avoid lifting, dropping, or twisting the partner's trapped leg to force an escape. Use safe inside frames before the arm-to-leg loop fully closes; do not pull or twist the partner's knee.

Move only through available space after the loop opens; tap if the neck is already enclosed. Establish Side Control only after the head is free. Keep the shoulders and hips controlled while moving outside the legs. Use balanced positional pressure and let the opponent communicate throughout the reset.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

Protect the neck as the hand-to-leg connection opens, then use hip control and a supported crossface to re-establish side control. Settle chest and shoulder pressure only after the head and arm are free of the loop.
