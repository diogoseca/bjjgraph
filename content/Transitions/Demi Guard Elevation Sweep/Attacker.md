<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You begin underneath in established Demi Guard. Use the connected collar, lapel and foot to respond to a visible forward weight shift. The goal is to reverse the top-bottom relationship and then stabilize; lifting the knee without changing the opponent's base is not a completed sweep.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Demi Guard Elevation Sweep?</p>

- Start with the full Demi configuration.
- Read a weight shift before lifting.
- Coordinate collar control with the foot-supported lapel.
- Follow the reversal rather than stopping at elevation.
- Free obstructing fabric and stabilize the real landing.


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Demi Guard Elevation Sweep?</p>

- You are in Demi Guard bottom with the lapel behind the knee.
- Your foot has useful contact on the lapel tail.
- The collar grip remains available to manage the upper body.
- The passer's pressure creates a visible change of balance.


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Demi Guard Elevation Sweep step by step?</p>

1. **Confirm the connections**: Check the collar grip, knee-line lapel and foot contact while keeping your legs between your torso and the passer.
2. **Read the pressure**: Identify the passer's weight moving forward onto the supported leg instead of trying to lift a fully balanced opponent.
3. **Create elevation**: Use the working foot-and-lapel connection to elevate the controlled knee, coordinating the movement with the collar connection.
4. **Follow the change of base**: Continue into the space created as the passer loses base; do not force the same motion if they recover a stable post.
5. **Come on top**: Follow the reversal with your hips and torso until you are above the opponent, recognizing any guard their legs retain.
6. **Release and stabilize**: Free fabric that binds your movement and settle top half guard or top open guard before attempting a separate pass.


</section>

## Opponent Counters

<p class="section-subtitle">How might your opponent counter Demi Guard Elevation Sweep?</p>

- **Recover a stable post and center your weight** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Keep the remaining guard or base and reassess the controls before repeating the attempt; do not force a knee against the lapel. → Leads to [[Positions/Demi Guard]]
- **Reduce the foot-on-lapel load while keeping balance** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Keep the remaining guard or base and reassess the controls before repeating the attempt; do not force a knee against the lapel. → Leads to [[Positions/Demi Guard]]
- **Clear the legs after the sweep structure collapses** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Keep the remaining guard or base and reassess the controls before repeating the attempt; do not force a knee against the lapel. → Leads to [[Positions/Side Control]]

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Demi Guard Elevation Sweep?</p>

### 1. Lifting before the passer's weight shifts

- **Consequence**: You work against a stable base and exhaust the grips.
- **Correction**: Recognize the forward loading before committing to elevation.

### 2. Pulling only with the collar arm

- **Consequence**: The knee line stays supported and the passer can recover posture.
- **Correction**: Coordinate the upper-body connection with the loaded foot and lapel.

### 3. Forcing the sweep against a fixed knee

- **Consequence**: The fabric can bind the joint while the bodies rotate.
- **Correction**: Release tension and reset if the leg cannot move comfortably.

### 4. Stopping after the opponent loses balance

- **Consequence**: The opponent can recover top position before you follow.
- **Correction**: Continue into a controlled top landing when the base actually gives way.

### 5. Assuming the reversal passed the guard

- **Consequence**: The opponent's remaining leg trap catches your next movement.
- **Correction**: Identify half or open guard and stabilize before a separate pass.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Demi Guard Elevation Sweep (Attacker)?</p>

**Recognition** - Identify the starting configuration
You are in Demi Guard bottom with the lapel behind the knee. With a cooperative partner, identify the required grips and leg contacts, then reset without applying pressure.

**Cooperative sequence** - Practice the control change
Perform the listed sequence slowly with a cooperative partner. Pause at the change of lapel or foot control and reset on any joint discomfort.

**One response** - Recognize the first defense
Let the partner use this response: Recover a stable post and center your weight. Practice keeping a safe base or guard when it blocks the attempt, then reset.

**Bounded exchange** - Confirm the landing
Start at the stated origin with light resistance. Stop and reset at a stable destination, a counter, or a request to release; do not continue through a bound knee.



</section>



<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Considerations

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the safety concerns for Demi Guard Elevation Sweep?</p>

Use controlled pressure with a cooperative partner when learning the lapel path. Release fabric immediately on a tap, verbal stop or discomfort. Do not rotate against a knee held by the lapel, and stop the drill when a stable landing is reached.
