<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You are the passer whose forward weight is being loaded onto the guard player's connection. Recover balance while the knee is still free to move, and reduce the foot-on-lapel barrier. If the reversal occurs, recover guard on landing rather than trying to save the exchange through a trapped joint.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Demi Guard Elevation Sweep?</p>

- Your controlled knee begins to rise with the lapel-supported foot.
- The collar connection draws your torso into the changing base.
- The guard player follows the elevation to come up rather than staying flat.


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Demi Guard Elevation Sweep?</p>

- Recognize the change of collar, lapel and foot controls early.
- Keep a usable base or leg barrier while contesting the movement.
- Choose a response that fits the current controls rather than a lost grip.
- Release any connection that binds a knee during movement.
- Identify the resulting position before continuing the exchange.


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Demi Guard Elevation Sweep?</p>

### 1. Recover a stable post and center your weight

- **When to use**: Before the elevation carries your hips beyond your base
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]]
- **If successful**: You remain on top while the bottom player retains Demi.
- **Risk**: A late post may not stop the reversal.

### 2. Reduce the foot-on-lapel load while keeping balance

- **When to use**: When you feel the controlled knee beginning to lift
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]]
- **If successful**: The sweep stalls and the position remains contested.
- **Risk**: Reaching without base can supply the missing weight shift.

### 3. Clear the legs after the sweep structure collapses

- **When to use**: Only after the collar and leg barrier no longer stop your advance
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Side Control]]
- **If successful**: You complete the counter-pass and settle side control.
- **Risk**: Driving before the connection is cleared can restart the elevation.



</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Demi Guard Elevation Sweep?</p>

### 1. Responding after the new controls are established

- **Consequence**: The attacker completes the movement before the response affects it.
- **Correction**: Recognize the listed cues during the control change.

### 2. Turning against a knee still bound by the lapel

- **Consequence**: The joint cannot follow the body movement freely.
- **Correction**: Release binding tension and reset rather than forcing the turn.

### 3. Ignoring the position left after the attempt

- **Consequence**: You continue with a response intended for a guard that has already changed.
- **Correction**: Check the actual leg and upper-body controls before the next action.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train defense against Demi Guard Elevation Sweep?</p>

**Recognition** - Name the cue
From the stated origin, have the partner begin slowly. Call out a listed recognition cue, then release and reset.

**Cooperative response** - Practice the first defense
Recover a stable post and center your weight. Use light resistance and stop when the response succeeds or the connection becomes uncomfortable.

**Bounded defense** - Recognize the outcome
Let the partner choose whether to continue or recover. Respond using the available controls; stop at the resulting stable position and name both players' roles.
