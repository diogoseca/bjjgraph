<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The attacker initially holds an Anaconda front headlock on top; Dead Orchard would require that player to reach a separate bottom rubber-guard configuration. Anaconda encloses the head and one arm with the arms. Dead Orchard encloses both arms with a high leg triangle from bottom guard. The reviewed sources establish these distinct configurations, not a direct top-player switch between them.

Confirm the front-headlock arm loop around the neck and one shoulder. Open the arm loop before either partner changes the body relationship. The intended Dead Orchard attacker must obtain a facing bottom-guard position; no direct transition is established here.

Bring the opponent close and support the high rubber-guard knee without pulling the foot. Only from that new guard position can the high leg triangle enclose both shoulders and arms. Confirm the Dead Orchard enclosure without loading a captured elbow or stacking the neck.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

The initial Anaconda is a vascular choke around the neck and one shoulder, so open its arm loop completely before changing roles. In the separate bottom-guard phase, use inside frames and a small hip escape if needed to make knee space before climbing into the both-arm leg triangle.
