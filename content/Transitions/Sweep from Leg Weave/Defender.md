<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Recognize the sleeve and far-trouser grips before committing more passing pressure. Recover a usable posting arm before the bottom player displaces the base. Keep your hips supported while following the controlled leg. If the sweep stalls, retain the leg-weave configuration with aligned knees. If the guard opens, finish clearing the legs before claiming side control.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Leg Weave]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Sweep from Leg Weave?</p>

- Begin underneath the passer's arm weave; retain enough space to establish the cloth grips used by this counter.
- Secure the sleeve so the passer cannot freely post that hand in the direction of the reversal.
- Establish the far-trouser connection. This cloth-dependent version has no modeled no-gi counterpart.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Sweep from Leg Weave?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: If the guard opens, finish clearing the legs before claiming side control.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Recover a usable posting arm before the bottom player displaces the base.
