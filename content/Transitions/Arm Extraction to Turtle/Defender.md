<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The endangered player has a turned-out foot enclosed by the opponent's upper body during a leg-entanglement exchange. An Aoki lock does not trap the arm in an omoplata. Do not rotate to turtle or bridge into a reversal while the foot remains fixed; these named destinations are not established by the reviewed foot-lock instruction.

Recognize the foot orientation without twisting it into position against resistance. Hold the instep and heel region securely beside the upper body rather than relying on calf compression. Use the entanglement or foot-on-hip frame to manage distance before considering a finish.

Stop the drill at a secure capture; the control can become a damaging rotation with very little movement. Keep track of your own exposed feet while concentrating on the captured foot. Open the arm enclosure and remove leg pressure immediately on a tap, sudden movement, or pain.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Aoki Lock Control]] (Bottom)

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Arm Extraction to Turtle?</p>

- The endangered player has a turned-out foot enclosed by the opponent's upper body during a leg-entanglement exchange.
- Prevent the foot enclosure and recover leg position while the opponent still lacks a secure connection.
- Address the upper-body grip before withdrawing the leg through a tight entanglement.

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Arm Extraction to Turtle?</p>

- Identify the turned foot: Recognize the foot orientation without twisting it into position against resistance.
- Enclose the foot: Hold the instep and heel region securely beside the upper body rather than relying on calf compression.
- Control the hip connection: Use the entanglement or foot-on-hip frame to manage distance before considering a finish.
- Keep pressure neutral: Stop the drill at a secure capture; the control can become a damaging rotation with very little movement.
- Respect counterattacks: Keep track of your own exposed feet while concentrating on the captured foot.
- Release before rotation: Open the arm enclosure and remove leg pressure immediately on a tap, sudden movement, or pain.
