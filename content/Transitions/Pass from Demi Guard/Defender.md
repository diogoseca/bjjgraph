<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You begin underneath and are defending your Demi Guard against the passer's grip and leg clearing. Preserve the collar connection and a useful leg barrier while you still can. If the passer overcommits forward weight, the remaining structure may permit a reversal; if the lapel is lost, recover an ordinary guard promptly.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]] (Top)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Pass from Demi Guard?</p>

- The passer stabilizes their base before reaching to the fabric.
- The collar grip or foot-on-tail contact is being contested.
- The passer moves the knee only after reducing the fabric connection.


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Pass from Demi Guard?</p>

- Recognize the change of collar, lapel and foot controls early.
- Keep a usable base or leg barrier while contesting the movement.
- Choose a response that fits the current controls rather than a lost grip.
- Release any connection that binds a knee during movement.
- Identify the resulting position before continuing the exchange.


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Pass from Demi Guard?</p>

### 1. Restore the collar and foot connection

- **When to use**: Before the passer has cleared the lapel from the knee
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]]
- **If successful**: You retain Demi underneath while the passer's attempt stalls.
- **Risk**: Reaching for lost fabric can sacrifice the leg barrier.

### 2. Keep your knees between the torsos

- **When to use**: As the passer begins moving past the foot-on-lapel barrier
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **If successful**: You retain half guard around one leg.
- **Risk**: A late frame may not recover space after the passer has cleared the knees.

### 3. Use forward loading to reverse

- **When to use**: When the passer loses balance while the lapel connection is still working
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **If successful**: You come on top and the former passer retains half guard.
- **Risk**: Forcing elevation against stable base can let the passer clear the legs.



</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Pass from Demi Guard?</p>

### 1. Responding after the new controls are established

- **Consequence**: The attacker completes the movement before the response affects it.
- **Correction**: Recognize the listed cues during the control change.

### 2. Turning against a knee still bound by the lapel

- **Consequence**: The joint cannot follow the body movement freely.
- **Correction**: Release binding tension and reset rather than forcing the turn.

### 3. Ignoring the position left after the attempt

- **Consequence**: You continue with a response intended for a guard that has already changed.
- **Correction**: Check the actual leg and upper-body controls before the next action.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train defense against Pass from Demi Guard?</p>

**Recognition** - Name the cue
From the stated origin, have the partner begin slowly. Call out a listed recognition cue, then release and reset.

**Cooperative response** - Practice the first defense
Restore the collar and foot connection. Use light resistance and stop when the response succeeds or the connection becomes uncomfortable.

**Bounded defense** - Recognize the outcome
Let the partner choose whether to continue or recover. Respond using the available controls; stop at the resulting stable position and name both players' roles.
