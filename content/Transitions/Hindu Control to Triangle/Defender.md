<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Recognize the leg exchange before the triangle closes. Maintain head and shoulder alignment while space remains. Keep the arm near the torso before the enclosure isolates it. Tap if the neck is loaded instead of forcing the exchange. Recognize ordinary closed guard if the high-leg control fully releases.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Hindulotine]] (Bottom)

</section>

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Hindu Control to Triangle?</p>

- Begin as the bottom Hindu Control holder with the leg-in head enclosure established.
- Remove finishing pressure before changing the head-and-leg arrangement.
- Support the raised knee through the hip angle as the enclosure changes.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Hindu Control to Triangle?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: Recognize ordinary closed guard if the high-leg control fully releases.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Maintain head and shoulder alignment while space remains.
