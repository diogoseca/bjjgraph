<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Wait for finishing pressure to release before changing posture. Maintain an inside frame as the second leg opens. Keep the head and shoulder aligned through the opening. Recognize that the first Chill Dog connection can still constrain posture. Recover ordinary guard posture only after the remaining high connection clears.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Rubber Guard/Invisible Collar]] (Bottom)

</section>

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Invisible Collar to Chill Dog?</p>

- Begin as the bottom holder with the two-leg Invisible Collar enclosure established.

- Preserve the first neck-cleared knee-hug connection as you prepare to open the second leg.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Invisible Collar to Chill Dog?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: Recover ordinary guard posture only after the remaining high connection clears.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Maintain an inside frame as the second leg opens.
