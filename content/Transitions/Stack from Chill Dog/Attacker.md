<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The top player faces a bottom guard player using Chill Dog; the head, shoulder or arm may still be enclosed. Forward pressure must not fold the partner onto the neck. The reviewed sources do not establish stacking as a required Chill Dog escape or an automatic Half Guard result.

Keep the free hand available for framing while preventing the isolated elbow from being pulled further away from your torso. The opponent changing the hand around your head signals the move beyond New York, not a turtle escape. Keep a balanced base and early inside frames; tap instead of moving against a completed choke or joint lock.

Create slack at the hip and thigh while keeping the head and captured arm aligned. Withdraw the shoulder and elbow only after the high leg connection has opened. Establish a safe posture with the high enclosure open only when the upper-body enclosure and relevant leg hooks have cleared.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

The safe passing checkpoint is a free shoulder and inside arm position, with hip control that opens the high leg without folding the partner onto the neck. Stop there and re-establish posture if the leg remains attached; downward force is not a substitute for clearance.
