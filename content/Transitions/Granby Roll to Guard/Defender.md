<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Follow the controlled roll while maintaining a stable and balanced base. Keep the recovering player's neck free of stacked body weight. Address the returning knees before guard is established. Recognize Turtle if the roll is interrupted before guard recovery. Claim rear control only when its actual torso and leg connections exist.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Turtle]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Granby Roll to Guard?</p>

- Begin on the knees and forearms in Turtle/Bottom, underneath or alongside the opponent's controlling ride.
- Check that the head and rolling shoulder can move safely before beginning the recovery.
- Roll diagonally across the shoulder and upper back rather than planting the head or loading the neck.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Granby Roll to Guard?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: Claim rear control only when its actual torso and leg connections exist.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Keep the recovering player's neck free of stacked body weight.
