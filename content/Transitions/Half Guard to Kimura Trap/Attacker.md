<section id="overview" class="content-section">

As the attacker you are on the bottom of half guard, and your job is to trade a moment of arm commitment for a grip that outlasts the position. Stay on your side with the inside hip off the mat: flat on your back the thread has no room and your shoulder has no leverage. Wait for the arm — do not chase it. The top player will post to base, reach for the crossface, or extend during a pass, and any of the three exposes the tricep. Thread, grip your own wrist, and close the figure four before you think about where you are going with it. The grip is the position; everything after is a choice.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Half Guard]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Half Guard to Kimura Trap?</p>

- The grip is the objective — do not trade it for a sweep or a finish before it is closed and deep
- Stay on your side with the inside hip elevated; a flattened hip removes both the thread space and the shoulder leverage
- Take the arm the opponent gives you rather than hunting a specific one — post, crossface and underhook all expose the tricep
- Keep the half guard leg entanglement live throughout; the grip only controls because the legs prevent disengagement
- Close the figure four as close to the opponent's elbow as the arm allows — a wrist-end grip gives the shoulder room to rotate free
- Treat a failed entry as free: you return to half guard having spent only grip effort, so the attack is repeatable


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Half Guard to Kimura Trap?</p>

- Half guard established with the opponent's near leg entangled and controlled
- Bottom player on their side, inside hip elevated, head off the mat
- The opponent's near arm posted, reaching, or otherwise extended away from their ribs
- Free hand available to meet the threading arm and close the figure four


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Half Guard to Kimura Trap step by step?</p>

1. **Establish the side position**: Shrimp onto your inside hip so your shoulders are stacked vertically rather than flat. This creates the gap under the opponent's chest that the threading arm needs and puts your shoulder in a position to generate rotation.
2. **Anchor the legs**: Confirm the half guard entanglement on the opponent's near leg, adding the lockdown if their base is strong. The legs are what convert a grip into control by removing the option to stand and pull free.
3. **Wait for the arm to commit**: Let the opponent post to base, reach for the crossface, or drive an underhook. Do not reach for an arm that is still tight to their ribs — a premature thread gives them your back and costs you the underhook battle.
4. **Thread the inside arm**: Drive your inside arm under the exposed tricep and up along the far side of their arm, palm turning toward their back. Keep the elbow tight to your own body as it travels so the arm does not get flattened out.
5. **Close the figure four**: Meet the threading hand with your free hand, grip your own wrist, and close the loop over the top of their arm. Slide the whole grip toward their elbow to shorten the lever they can rotate against.
6. **Consolidate the trap**: Pull the captured arm across their centreline or toward their back to load the shoulder, and re-settle your hips underneath. You are now in Kimura Trap Bottom with the grip established and the finish, the sweep and the back take all available as separate decisions.

<section id="common-counters" class="content-section">

## Opponent Counters

<p class="section-subtitle">How might your opponent counter Half Guard to Kimura Trap?</p>

- **Hiding the arm — the top player keeps the elbow pinned to their ribs and refuses to post** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Attack their base instead: lockdown or off-balance them so they must post to stay upright, then take the arm they are forced to give. → Leads to [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **Ripping the arm free before the figure four closes** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Close the loop earlier and higher on the arm, and keep your threading elbow tight so there is no straight line for them to extract along. → Leads to [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **Passing the entangled leg while your arm is committed** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Abandon the thread the moment the leg is cleared and recover the knee shield; the grip is not worth the pass. → Leads to [[Positions/Side Control]]


</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Half Guard to Kimura Trap?</p>

### 1. Threading from a flat back

- **Consequence**: No space under the opponent's chest and no shoulder leverage, so the arm gets pinned and the underhook is lost.
- **Correction**: Shrimp onto the inside hip first and confirm your shoulders are stacked before the arm moves.

### 2. Reaching for an arm that has not committed

- **Consequence**: The opponent circles the arm free and takes the underhook you vacated, often ending in a pass.
- **Correction**: Bait the commitment with a lockdown or an off-balance and take the arm only once it is posted or extended.

### 3. Gripping at the wrist end of the arm

- **Consequence**: The lever is long, the shoulder rotates free easily, and the trap never consolidates.
- **Correction**: Walk the closed figure four toward the elbow before you load the shoulder.

### 4. Releasing the leg entanglement to chase the grip

- **Consequence**: The opponent stands or steps over, and the grip controls nothing because they can disengage.
- **Correction**: Treat the legs as a prerequisite, not an afterthought — the entanglement stays live for the whole entry.

### 5. Going straight for the finish as the grip closes

- **Consequence**: You surrender the positional consolidation and the opponent extracts during the rotation.
- **Correction**: Establish the trap first. The finish is a separate decision made from an established position.

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Half Guard to Kimura Trap (Attacker)?</p>

**Isolation** - The thread
Partner posts a hand on the mat and holds still. Thread and close the figure four for reps until the path is automatic from both the knee shield and the flat half guard.

**Positional** - Reading the commitment
Partner passes at 50% and may post, crossface or underhook. Take only the arm that commits; reset without threading if none does.

**Resistance** - Closing under pressure
Partner actively hides the arm and defends the loop. Start in half guard bottom, win the grip or reset after thirty seconds.

**Integration** - Entry into the system
From live half guard bottom rolling, enter the trap and consolidate only — no finish, no sweep — to train the positional discipline the entry depends on.



</section>



<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Considerations

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the safety concerns for Half Guard to Kimura Trap?</p>

This is a control entry, not a submission, and no joint should be loaded during it. Closing the figure four captures the shoulder but must not rotate it: the finish is a separate, deliberate decision made from the established trap. Threading fast into a partner's committed arm can hyperextend an elbow that is already straight, so build the grip progressively and never snap it closed.
