<section id="overview" class="content-section">

As the top player your defence is decided before the grip closes, not after. Once the figure four is locked and consolidated you are in their control system and every option costs you something. The whole defensive game is therefore about arm discipline: base without posting your hand where it can be threaded, crossface without extending, and pass without feeding the underhook arm. If the thread starts, your window is the moment before the two hands meet — that is when the arm still comes out cleanly. After that, your best line is usually to accept the grip and pass the entangled leg rather than fight the shoulder.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Half Guard]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Half Guard to Kimura Trap?</p>

- The bottom player shrimps onto their inside hip and their shoulders stack vertically
- Their inside hand starts travelling under your near tricep rather than framing on your hip
- They stop attacking the underhook and go quiet, waiting for your arm to commit
- The lockdown closes on your trapped leg, which usually precedes an attack on your base


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Half Guard to Kimura Trap?</p>

- Win this before the grip closes — after the figure four consolidates every option costs you something
- Base on your head, knee and hip rather than a posted hand whenever the bottom player is on their side
- Keep the elbow pinned to your ribs; an arm tight to the body has no tricep gap to thread under
- If the thread starts, extract before the two hands meet — that is the only clean window
- Once the grip is closed, pass the entangled leg instead of fighting the shoulder — position beats grip strength
- Never let them flatten you onto your side while the grip is live; that is the sweep, not the submission


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Half Guard to Kimura Trap?</p>

### 1. Hide the arm — keep the elbow pinned to your ribs and base on head, knee and hip

- **When to use**: Pre-emptively, whenever the bottom player is on their side with an elevated hip.
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **If successful**: The entry never starts and you continue your pass.
- **Risk**: Basing without a posted hand is less stable; a committed off-balance can sweep you.

### 2. Extract early — rip the arm straight back before the two hands meet

- **When to use**: The instant you feel the thread begin and the loop is still open.
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **If successful**: You recover the arm and are back to a neutral half guard pass.
- **Risk**: Ripping late feeds the grip and can load your own shoulder.

### 3. Concede the grip and pass the leg

- **When to use**: The figure four is already closed and consolidating.
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Side Control]]
- **If successful**: You reach side control with the arm still gripped but the entanglement gone, which neutralises the trap.
- **Risk**: Committing to the pass while the shoulder is loaded exposes you to the finish and the back take.



</section>

<section id="favorable-outcomes" class="content-section">

## Best-Case Outcomes for Defender

<p class="section-subtitle">What is the best outcome when defending Half Guard to Kimura Trap?</p>

### → [[Positions/Side Control]]

Clear the trapped leg with a knee cut or a back step the moment their inside arm leaves its frame, then drive to side control before the figure four consolidates. This is the counter outcome: you pass while their arm is committed to the thread.

### → [[Positions/Half Guard]]

Extract the arm before the loop closes and re-establish your base. The entry fails, nothing changes, and you return to the same half guard pass you started.



</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Half Guard to Kimura Trap?</p>

### 1. Posting a flat hand on the mat to base

- **Consequence**: You hand over the exact arm the entry needs, fully extended, with the tricep exposed.
- **Correction**: Base on head, knee and hip instead, and post only on the far side away from the thread.

### 2. Fighting the shoulder after the grip is closed

- **Consequence**: You spend energy against a lever designed to beat arm strength, and lose the passing window.
- **Correction**: Accept the grip and change the problem — pass the entangled leg and the trap loses its anchor.

### 3. Ripping the arm late, after the two hands have met

- **Consequence**: The rotation loads your own shoulder against the closed figure four and can finish you.
- **Correction**: Extract only while the loop is open; once it is closed, switch to the positional answer.

### 4. Reaching across for the crossface against an elevated hip

- **Consequence**: The reaching arm is the most commonly captured one in this entry.
- **Correction**: Flatten their hip first, then crossface — never crossface into a bottom player who is already on their side.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train defense against Half Guard to Kimura Trap?</p>

**Isolation** - Arm discipline
Hold half guard top against a partner on their side for one-minute rounds without posting a hand on the mat. Base on head, knee and hip only.

**Positional** - The extraction window
Partner threads slowly and announces the moment their hands meet. Practise extracting before that call, then practise recognising when it has passed.

**Resistance** - Conceding correctly
Start with the grip already closed. Your only objective is to pass the entangled leg to side control without fighting the shoulder.
