<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Keep the captured arm compact until the holder removes shoulder pressure. Follow the leg opening without rotating against a fixed wrist. Maintain an inside frame as the high clamp comes apart. Recognize retained Carni control if the holder cannot safely open the leg connection. Recover posture after the shoulder is free, while respecting the remaining open guard.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Rubber Guard/Carni]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Carni Release to Guard?</p>

- Begin as the player applying Carni, with the opponent's upper arm and shoulder captured by your leg connection.
- Stop the submission loading before letting the opponent's arm move through the enclosure.
- Support the knee and move through the hip to open the clamp; do not wrench the foot to free the arm.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Carni Release to Guard?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: Recover posture after the shoulder is free, while respecting the remaining open guard.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Follow the leg opening without rotating against a fixed wrist.
