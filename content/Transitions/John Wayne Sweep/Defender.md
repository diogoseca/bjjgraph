<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The bottom player has half guard around one leg, with a usable underhook/whizzer relationship and a lower-leg anchor. Shawn Williams demonstrates a shallow foot anchor and knee lever, finishing on top in half guard. A butterfly-hook position and direct mount are not the demonstrated start and finish.

A shallow lower-leg anchor with the opposing knee entering your hip signals the sweep. Keep a hand or foot able to support the tipping direction before the upper body is captured. Avoid letting the bottom player connect their upper body directly under your base.

Address the shallow foot anchor while the knee and hip can still move safely. Do not post a locked arm or twist a trapped knee to resist a completed reversal. Use inside frames and the remaining leg connection after the opponent comes on top.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

If the knee lever removes your base, release unsafe arm tension and begin an escape from the position you actually land in. Recover a shoulder frame and inside knee before the new top player closes the space beside your ribs.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Half Guard]] (Bottom)

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting John Wayne Sweep?</p>

- The bottom player has half guard around one leg, with a usable underhook/whizzer relationship and a lower-leg anchor.
- Use an underhook and control the whizzer or posting arm without leaving the elbow exposed to a front-headlock attack.
- Bring the knee into the hip line as the other foot supplies a controlled post.

## Key Defensive Principles
