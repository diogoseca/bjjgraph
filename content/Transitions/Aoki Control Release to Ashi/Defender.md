<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Wait for joint pressure to release before moving the foot. Keep the knee and foot aligned during the opening. Recover a comfortable foot position without an explosive twist. Recognize the retained Ashi connection after the grip opens. Square up if both players fully disengage their legs.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Aoki Lock Control]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Aoki Control Release to Ashi?</p>

- Begin as the holder in Aoki Lock Control/Bottom with the foot grip and the leg connection established.
- Remove the finishing pressure before allowing the captured foot to change angle.
- Open the foot grip without pulling the foot through a fixed knee angle.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Aoki Control Release to Ashi?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: Square up if both players fully disengage their legs.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Keep the knee and foot aligned during the opening.
