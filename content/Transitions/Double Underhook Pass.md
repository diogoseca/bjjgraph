<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**A double-underhook pass reaches beneath both thighs after the high rubber-guard clinch has opened, then clears the legs under controlled pressure.**

The top player faces a bottom guard player using Crackhead Control; the head, shoulder or arm may still be enclosed. Do not thread both arms beneath the legs while the head and shoulders remain trapped in Crackhead Control. A double-under pass is a new configuration after release.

Avoid extending an arm between the high legs where it can be isolated. A second leg climbing behind your head increases the posture-control problem. Keep a balanced base and early inside frames; tap instead of moving against a completed choke or joint lock.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

Instructional sources: [Eddie Bravo — MTS 169: the Pump](https://www.10thplanetjj.com/techniques/mts-169-rubber-guard-the-pump/); [10th Planet Costa Mesa — basic path with double bagger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W2OFKidf9lE).

## Playing as Defender

[[Double Underhook Pass/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Start with broken posture: Establish the first high leg and its hand support before adding the second leg.

- Bring the second leg high: Reinforce the clinch without forcing the knees beyond a comfortable hip angle.

- Track both elbows: Do not assume that high legs alone have isolated an arm or completed Dead Orchard.

- Keep knees close: Use hip position and connected leg pressure rather than pulling the feet against fixed knees.

- Return to one-leg control: Lower the reinforcing leg while preserving the first shin connection if you need Mission Control again.

- Open against a stack: Release and frame when the opponent compresses your upper back or you cannot comfortably maintain the clinch.


### Recognition Cues


- The top player faces a bottom guard player using Crackhead Control; the head, shoulder or arm may still be enclosed.

- A second leg climbing behind your head increases the posture-control problem.

- Create slack at the hip and thigh while keeping the head and captured arm aligned.
