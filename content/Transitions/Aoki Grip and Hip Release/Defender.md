<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Retain the foot connection without forcing rotation. Follow the early hip movement with a supported leg connection. Release immediately if the partner taps or signals discomfort. Recognize Ashi Garami if the foot grip opens but the legs remain connected. Recognize facing open guard if both connections clear.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Aoki Lock Control]] (Top)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Aoki Grip and Hip Release?</p>

- Begin as the caught player in Aoki Lock Control/Top; the opposing player controls your foot from underneath.
- Work only while the foot and knee remain unloaded. Tap instead of turning against established joint pressure.
- Address the foot grip before trying to rotate the hips through the retained leg connection.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Aoki Grip and Hip Release?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: Recognize facing open guard if both connections clear.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Follow the early hip movement with a supported leg connection.
