<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**Passing Demi Guard combines a controlled lapel clear with leg clearance and upper-body stabilization, advancing the gi passer to side control when every barrier has been removed.**

Demi uses a collar grip and foot-on-lapel support at the knee to manage forward pressure, as described by [Legion](https://legionsandiego.com/the-jiu-jitsu-dictionary/#demi-guard) and [Keenan's introduction](https://jiujitsux.com/courses/keenans-lapel-encyclopedia/lesson/16-1-introduction-to-the-demi-guard/). The top player therefore needs to address that connection while keeping base. These are conservative passing priorities inferred from the documented guard structure, not a claim to reproduce a named Demi passing lesson.

The objective here is to clear the legs and settle side control. If the bottom player recaptures one leg, recognize [[Positions/Half Guard]]; if they reverse the players, protect yourself and recover guard on landing. Driving harder into an intact connection is not equivalent to clearing it.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]] (Top)
**Success Rate**: gi only

## Playing as Attacker

[[Pass from Demi Guard/Attacker|→ Full Attacker Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Base comes before reaching to clear fabric.

- Reduce the foot-supported lapel connection before driving forward.

- Free the knee without twisting against the remaining wrap.

- Check the leg barrier separately from the fabric grip.

- Recognize the actual landing before continuing.

### Common Mistakes


- **Reaching down before securing base**
  - Consequence: Your weight moves onto the opponent's elevation structure.
  - Correction: Stabilize your base before the fabric-clearing reach.

- **Driving forward while the foot remains supported**
  - Consequence: The same pressure can increase the bottom player's sweep opportunity.
  - Correction: Reduce the foot-on-lapel connection before applying passing pressure.

- **Rotating the knee against taut fabric**
  - Consequence: The lapel restricts the leg during the rotation.
  - Correction: Release the binding tension before moving the knee.

## Playing as Defender

[[Pass from Demi Guard/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Recognize the change of collar, lapel and foot controls early.

- Keep a usable base or leg barrier while contesting the movement.

- Choose a response that fits the current controls rather than a lost grip.

- Release any connection that binds a knee during movement.

- Identify the resulting position before continuing the exchange.


### Recognition Cues


- The passer stabilizes their base before reaching to the fabric.

- The collar grip or foot-on-tail contact is being contested.

- The passer moves the knee only after reducing the fabric connection.


### Defensive Options


- **Restore the collar and foot connection** - When: Before the passer has cleared the lapel from the knee

- **Keep your knees between the torsos** - When: As the passer begins moving past the foot-on-lapel barrier

- **Use forward loading to reverse** - When: When the passer loses balance while the lapel connection is still working
