<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You are the passer as the bottom player changes the close Demi configuration into broader lapel control. Removing their foot from the tail does not mean all fabric control has disappeared. Preserve your balance while contesting the remaining grip and leg frames, and recognize whether a guard still separates your torsos.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Demi Guard to Lapel Guard?</p>

- The guard player keeps lapel contact as you move away.
- The foot leaves the tail while another leg frame replaces it.
- Their hips follow your direction to keep both knees in front.


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Demi Guard to Lapel Guard?</p>

- Recognize the change of collar, lapel and foot controls early.
- Keep a usable base or leg barrier while contesting the movement.
- Choose a response that fits the current controls rather than a lost grip.
- Release any connection that binds a knee during movement.
- Identify the resulting position before continuing the exchange.


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Demi Guard to Lapel Guard?</p>

### 1. Keep the existing configuration contained

- **When to use**: When the bottom player cannot make room for a new frame
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]]
- **If successful**: The attempted reconfiguration stalls in Demi Guard.
- **Risk**: Pressuring without balance can load the Demi sweep.

### 2. Contest the frame change before it settles

- **When to use**: As the foot leaves the lapel tail
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]]
- **If successful**: The bottom player must restore their starting guard.
- **Risk**: A reach that loses base opens a sweep rather than stopping the change.

### 3. Clear the grip and legs through an exposed gap

- **When to use**: Only if the guard player has abandoned both useful fabric and leg frames
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Side Control]]
- **If successful**: You settle a pin after clearing the recovered-guard attempt.
- **Risk**: Remaining lapel tension or a leg frame can still stop the pass.



</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Demi Guard to Lapel Guard?</p>

### 1. Responding after the new controls are established

- **Consequence**: The attacker completes the movement before the response affects it.
- **Correction**: Recognize the listed cues during the control change.

### 2. Turning against a knee still bound by the lapel

- **Consequence**: The joint cannot follow the body movement freely.
- **Correction**: Release binding tension and reset rather than forcing the turn.

### 3. Ignoring the position left after the attempt

- **Consequence**: You continue with a response intended for a guard that has already changed.
- **Correction**: Check the actual leg and upper-body controls before the next action.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train defense against Demi Guard to Lapel Guard?</p>

**Recognition** - Name the cue
From the stated origin, have the partner begin slowly. Call out a listed recognition cue, then release and reset.

**Cooperative response** - Practice the first defense
Keep the existing configuration contained. Use light resistance and stop when the response succeeds or the connection becomes uncomfortable.

**Bounded defense** - Recognize the outcome
Let the partner choose whether to continue or recover. Respond using the available controls; stop at the resulting stable position and name both players' roles.
