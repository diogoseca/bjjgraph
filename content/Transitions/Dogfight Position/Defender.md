<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Keep an upper-body frame between the rising player and your torso. Deny a deep underhook before the bottom player connects to the far leg. Keep a stable base while following the player's rise. If the underhook counter stalls, rebuild the leg-weave pressure without twisting the knees. If the legs clear, consolidate side control before counting the pass.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Leg Weave]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Dogfight Position?</p>

- Begin underneath the arm-weaving passer, with a frame protecting the space around your upper body.
- Use the frame to make room for the underhook without reaching an unsupported arm away from the body.
- Connect the underhook before trying to lift the shoulders and rise underneath the passer.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Dogfight Position?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: If the legs clear, consolidate side control before counting the pass.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Deny a deep underhook before the bottom player connects to the far leg.
