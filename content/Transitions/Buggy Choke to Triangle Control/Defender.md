<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Maintain head and shoulder alignment while the loop opens. Keep the arm connected to the torso before the leg triangle forms. Use inside frames during the leg exchange. Recognize open guard if it settles before a triangle enclosure forms. Confirm side control only after the holder loses both the loop and the incoming leg control.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Buggy Choke]] (Bottom)

</section>

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Buggy Choke to Triangle Control?</p>

- Begin as the bottom player holding the same-side arm-and-leg Buggy loop beneath side control.
- Unload the choke before changing the leg and arm connections.
- Recover the legs in front through the available space while keeping the knee movement comfortable.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Buggy Choke to Triangle Control?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: Confirm side control only after the holder loses both the loop and the incoming leg control.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Keep the arm connected to the torso before the leg triangle forms.
