<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The bottom player faces the opponent in Zombie, with a high leg controlling posture. The reviewed rubber-guard instruction supports controlling posture and a posting arm. It does not establish this node's unspecified direct sweep to mount; a specific sweep setup must be chosen before live practice.

Maintain posture control while freeing the other arm for the pummel. Determine which arm can prevent the intended reversal; do not assume an isolated arm removes every post. Use a safe wrist or arm connection without manipulating individual fingers.

Use a small hip movement to check balance while retaining a stable guard. Proceed only after a coach has established the leg placement and direction for the chosen sweep. Release dangerous arm tension and establish the top position that actually results; mount requires additional leg clearance.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

The New York overhook can control one posting arm after the pummel, but it does not supply a complete sweep. If the base remains stable, guard retention needs a short hip escape and an inside frame against a crossface and chest pressure; a specific submission or reversal still needs its own demonstrated setup.
