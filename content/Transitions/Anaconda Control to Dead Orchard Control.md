<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**Anaconda Control is a front-headlock arm enclosure; Dead Orchard is a bottom-player leg triangle around both arms, so changing between them requires a separate guard exchange.**

The attacker initially holds an Anaconda front headlock on top; Dead Orchard would require that player to reach a separate bottom rubber-guard configuration. Anaconda encloses the head and one arm with the arms. Dead Orchard encloses both arms with a high leg triangle from bottom guard. The reviewed sources establish these distinct configurations, not a direct top-player switch between them.

Confirm the front-headlock arm loop around the neck and one shoulder. Open the arm loop before either partner changes the body relationship. The intended Dead Orchard attacker must obtain a facing bottom-guard position; no direct transition is established here.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

Instructional sources: [Nathan Orchard — Dead Orchard, part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XoiB1D2WJwE); [Ben Eddy — the Zombie pummel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JzSxFvNA5Uo).

## Playing as Defender

[[Anaconda Control to Dead Orchard Control/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Recognize both-arm capture: Treat a high leg triangle containing both your arms as an arm-lock threat even when the neck is not being choked.

- Protect the elbows: Keep the arms compact and avoid offering a straight arm while trying to force posture.

- Prevent the leg closure: Maintain an early frame at the hip or thigh before the figure-four closes around the shoulders.

- Clear a shoulder safely: Work on opening the leg enclosure before withdrawing the shoulder and elbow together; stop if an arm is already loaded.

- Recover base in stages: Only raise posture after the shoulder line is free; a sudden pull can extend a captured elbow.

- Tap with any free signal: Use a verbal tap or foot tap if both hands are trapped, and do not rely on pain to judge remaining range.
