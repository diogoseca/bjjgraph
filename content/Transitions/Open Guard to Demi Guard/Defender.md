<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You are the top player facing a bottom player who is changing guards. Recognize the collar grip, lapel feed behind your knee and approaching foot as one entry. Keep a stable base and contest the connection before pressing into it; if their legs remain in front, you still face a guard.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Open Guard]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Open Guard to Demi Guard?</p>

- The guard player takes or adjusts a collar grip.
- The lapel is being guided behind your knee.
- A foot approaches the lapel tail as the knees remain between you.


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Open Guard to Demi Guard?</p>

- Recognize the change of collar, lapel and foot controls early.
- Keep a usable base or leg barrier while contesting the movement.
- Choose a response that fits the current controls rather than a lost grip.
- Release any connection that binds a knee during movement.
- Identify the resulting position before continuing the exchange.


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Open Guard to Demi Guard?</p>

### 1. Contest the feed while preserving base

- **When to use**: Before the lapel reaches a useful position behind your knee
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Open Guard]]
- **If successful**: The passer remains in front of open guard after preventing the lapel feed.
- **Risk**: A late forward drive can load the completed Demi connection.

### 2. Prevent the foot from settling on the tail

- **When to use**: During the change of leg position
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Open Guard]]
- **If successful**: The guard player must retain the original guard or restart the entry.
- **Risk**: Reaching down without balance exposes a sweep opportunity.

### 3. Clear the legs during an abandoned feed

- **When to use**: Only when the guard player has lost both the lapel connection and the leg barrier
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Side Control]]
- **If successful**: You settle side control after completing leg clearance.
- **Risk**: A partial clear can leave you caught in a guard again.



</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Open Guard to Demi Guard?</p>

### 1. Responding after the new controls are established

- **Consequence**: The attacker completes the movement before the response affects it.
- **Correction**: Recognize the listed cues during the control change.

### 2. Turning against a knee still bound by the lapel

- **Consequence**: The joint cannot follow the body movement freely.
- **Correction**: Release binding tension and reset rather than forcing the turn.

### 3. Ignoring the position left after the attempt

- **Consequence**: You continue with a response intended for a guard that has already changed.
- **Correction**: Check the actual leg and upper-body controls before the next action.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train defense against Open Guard to Demi Guard?</p>

**Recognition** - Name the cue
From the stated origin, have the partner begin slowly. Call out a listed recognition cue, then release and reset.

**Cooperative response** - Practice the first defense
Contest the feed while preserving base. Use light resistance and stop when the response succeeds or the connection becomes uncomfortable.

**Bounded defense** - Recognize the outcome
Let the partner choose whether to continue or recover. Respond using the available controls; stop at the resulting stable position and name both players' roles.
