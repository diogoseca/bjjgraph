<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The top player faces a bottom guard player using Chill Dog; the head, shoulder or arm may still be enclosed. This is defense inside facing rubber guard. Closed or half guard depends on which lower-body connections remain; neither is guaranteed by raising the head.

Keep the opposing hand on the mat and hug the knee before changing the head-side grip. Bring the knee toward the chest through hip movement so the neck-clearing motion does not twist the knee. Move the controlling arm around the head while keeping the leg and trapped arm connected.

Use the forearm near the shoulder line to manage forward pressure; do not let the frame collapse across your own wrist. Keep the knee hug while choosing further control such as Invisible Collar or an omoplata setup. Return the legs to a comfortable guard if the opponent withdraws the elbow or stacks your upper back.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

If the high control opens, switch to guard retention with an inside shoulder frame and hip movement that returns a knee. Do not chase the head while the top player advances chest pressure into the space left by the falling leg.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Rubber Guard/Chill Dog]] (Top)

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Posture from Chill Dog?</p>

- The top player faces a bottom guard player using Chill Dog; the head, shoulder or arm may still be enclosed.
- The opponent changing the hand around your head signals the move beyond New York, not a turtle escape.
- Create slack at the hip and thigh while keeping the head and captured arm aligned.
