<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Keep the Buggy enclosure connected while respecting neck safety. Follow small posture changes with controlled hip movement. Use the remaining connection if the caught player cannot clear the loop. Recognize the distinct triangle enclosure only if the neck and one arm are captured by the legs. If the player fully disengages, retain facing open guard instead of claiming the choke remains.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Buggy Choke]] (Top)

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Frame and Posture Escape from Buggy Choke?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: If the player fully disengages, retain facing open guard instead of claiming the choke remains.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Follow small posture changes with controlled hip movement.
