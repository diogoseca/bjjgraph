<section id="overview" class="content-section">

Defending the Rolling Kimura means stopping a bottom player who has already secured a figure-four on your arm from converting it into a sweep or back take. You face the same dilemma you are trying to impose on others: protect the trapped shoulder from the kimura while refusing to be rolled over the top. The decisive truth is that prevention beats cure, because once the bottom player has a deep elbow grip and your weight commits forward, the roll becomes very hard to stop. Your highest-percentage defense is winning the grip fight before the figure-four locks and keeping the trapped elbow tight to your own body. If the grip is already set, your job shifts to denying the roll: keep a wide, heavy base, avoid driving your weight blindly forward into the rotation, and be ready to ride or counter-roll so that if you are taken over, you land on top with the attacker dumped onto the bottom rather than surrendering your back.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Kimura Trap]] (Bottom)

</section>

<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Rolling Kimura?</p>

- The bottom player threads one arm under yours and clamps a figure-four on your wrist, sliding the grip up toward your elbow
- Your trapped wrist is dragged toward your own back, loading the shoulder and pre-setting the rotation
- The bottom player turns onto the shoulder of your trapped-arm side and compacts their knees toward their chest
- You feel rotational pressure building on your shoulder the instant you post or drive your weight forward
- Their hips dig under yours and their chest wraps tight to you, the signature connection that precedes the roll


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Rolling Kimura?</p>

- Win the grip fight early and strip the figure-four before it locks deep at your elbow, because a wrist-level grip is far easier to clear
- Keep the threatened elbow pinned tight to your own ribs so the arm cannot be isolated or loaded
- Maintain a wide, heavy base and avoid committing your weight blindly forward, since your forward drive is the fuel the roll needs
- If the grip is locked, post the far hand and far leg to wall off the rotation before it starts
- Protect the shoulder first and the position second; tap to genuine joint pressure rather than risking the arm to save the back
- If you are being rolled, ride it and counter-roll to land on top rather than fighting to stay put and feeding the back take
- Stay aware of the secondary branches, because basing wide invites the Old School sweep and turning in invites the back take


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Rolling Kimura?</p>

### 1. Strip the figure-four with your free hand and retract the trapped elbow tight to your ribs

- **When to use**: The instant you feel the grip being threaded under your arm or clamped on your wrist, before it locks deep at the elbow
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Back Control]]
- **If successful**: The grip dies, the back take is off the table, and you keep top control to continue passing
- **Risk**: Diving both hands to the grip can lighten your base and open a sweep or a different attack if you are slow

### 2. Widen your base and post your far hand and far leg to wall off the rotation

- **When to use**: When the figure-four is already locked but the roll has not started; keep your weight off the trapped side
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Kimura Trap]]
- **If successful**: Without an axis to roll across, the attacker cannot sweep you up to the top kimura and the attack stalls
- **Risk**: A wide base exposes the far ankle to an Old School sweep, so be ready to defend that branch

### 3. Ride the roll and counter-roll, driving your weight through their rotation to land on top

- **When to use**: When the roll is already underway and you cannot stop it; commit to coming out on top rather than resisting in place
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Turtle]]
- **If successful**: You surface on top with the attacker dumped onto the bottom in turtle, and the kimura grip is weakened by the angle change
- **Risk**: Mistimed, the counter-roll accelerates their sweep and feeds you straight to back control

### 4. Pin the shoulder safe and straighten the trapped arm to clear it as the roll loads

- **When to use**: When the grip is set but shallow toward your wrist, giving you room to extend and slip the arm free
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Turtle]]
- **If successful**: The arm slips the figure-four, the rotational threat dissolves, and the exchange resets to a neutral scramble
- **Risk**: Straightening into a deep elbow grip can hand the attacker an even tighter kimura or an armbar



</section>

<section id="favorable-outcomes" class="content-section">

## Best-Case Outcomes for Defender

<p class="section-subtitle">What is the best outcome when defending Rolling Kimura?</p>

### → [[Positions/Turtle]]

When you cannot prevent the roll, ride it instead of fighting it in place: post your free hand, follow the attacker's rotation, and drive your weight through so that as the roll completes you surface on top and the attacker lands on the bottom in turtle. Time the counter-roll to the peak of their momentum, keep the shoulder safe, and immediately pressure them down before they re-establish the grip.



</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Rolling Kimura?</p>

### 1. Letting the figure-four lock deep before you start fighting the grip

- **Consequence**: Once the elbow-deep grip is set and your weight commits forward, the roll becomes extremely hard to stop
- **Correction**: React to the first contact on your wrist by peeling the grip and tucking the elbow to your ribs before it slides to the elbow

### 2. Driving your weight blindly forward into the bottom player

- **Consequence**: Your forward pressure is exactly the momentum the roll feeds on, so you power your own sweep
- **Correction**: Keep a wide, heavy, controlled base and post off to the side; do not lunge your weight over a player who has your arm

### 3. Fighting to stay put once the roll has clearly started

- **Consequence**: Resisting in place lets the loaded shoulder drag you over slowly and surrenders your back
- **Correction**: When the roll is unavoidable, commit to riding and counter-rolling so you land on top instead of being taken over

### 4. Ignoring the shoulder to save the position

- **Consequence**: Riding out heavy rotational pressure risks a torn shoulder for the sake of a position you may lose anyway
- **Correction**: Protect the joint first and tap to genuine kimura pressure rather than gambling your shoulder on a scramble

### 5. Basing wide without respecting the secondary sweeps

- **Consequence**: A wide base stops the roll but feeds the Old School sweep off your exposed far leg
- **Correction**: Pair the wide base with active far-leg awareness, keeping the ankle out of reach or staying ready to defend the hook



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train defense against Rolling Kimura?</p>

**Week 1-2: Recognition and Grip Defense** - Spotting the figure-four early and stripping it before it locks
Partner slowly threads and clamps the kimura grip from the bottom while you drill recognizing the first contact and peeling the grip with your free hand, tucking the elbow to your ribs. Use zero resistance at first, building to light resistance as your reaction speed improves.

**Week 3-4: Base and Anti-Rotation Defense** - Denying the roll once the grip is locked
Partner locks the grip and tries to set the rolling axis with moderate effort. Drill widening your base, posting the far hand and leg, keeping weight off the trapped side, and refusing to drive forward. Build the automatic habit of basing rather than lunging when your arm is controlled.

**Week 4-6: Counter-Rolling and Top Recovery** - Riding the roll to land on top
Partner initiates the full roll while you drill riding it and counter-rolling to surface on top with them on the bottom. Start at half speed to develop timing, then build to full speed. Emphasize posting the free hand, protecting the shoulder, and pressuring them down immediately on landing.

**Week 7-9: Live Positional Defense** - Defending the Rolling Kimura under full resistance
Start in Kimura Trap top with the bottom player working the full system, including the Rolling Kimura, Old School sweep, and back take. Drill distinguishing the roll from the other branches and responding with the right defense in real time, integrating shoulder safety and base management under live pressure.
