<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The bottom player is already facing the opponent in Chill Dog, with a high rubber-guard leg connection. The bottom player owns Chill Dog rather than being trapped in a turtle ride. An escape from the high guard by the top player is a different action.

The bottom player is on the back or hip with a high rubber-guard leg across the upper back. One of the top player's arms is isolated beside the bottom player rather than framing inside. Release any wrist, neck or elbow loading before opening the high-leg enclosure. Lower the controlling leg through a comfortable hip angle rather than pulling the foot against the knee.

Bring the forearms and knees between the bodies as space becomes available. Keep the shoulders and hips oriented toward the opponent while re-establishing a comfortable facing guard. Check posture, knee comfort and inside space before resuming a sweep or submission attack.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

After fully releasing the high-leg connection, use a small hip escape to restore inside frames and knees. This guard retention keeps a facing passer from advancing while ordinary guard is rebuilt.

As the high leg comes down, place a forearm inside the approaching shoulder to prevent a crossface and renewed chest pressure. Keep that frame while the hips make room for the returning knee; opening the leg without protecting this space invites a pin.
