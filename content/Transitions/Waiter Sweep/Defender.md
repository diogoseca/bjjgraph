<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The bottom player is underneath the opponent's hips in deep half guard with one leg available to be lifted and supported. The waiter leg shelf and deep-half hip position distinguish this sweep from a generic underhook wrestle-up. The landing may retain half guard; side control requires clearing the legs.

A deep-half player supporting your elevated leg is setting the waiter mechanism. Preserve hand or foot support before the opponent controls the tipping direction. Avoid leaving the hips directly above an established deep-half shoulder connection.

Return the elevated leg only while the knee can move without twisting. Accept a controlled reversal if balance is lost rather than risking a shoulder or knee. Use inside frames and the remaining leg connection if the opponent comes on top.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

An early escape withdraws the elevated leg or restores a usable post before your weight is carried over the bottom player. If you land underneath, recover frames and knees instead of trying to hold the original standing base through the reversal.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Half Guard/Deep Half Guard]] (Bottom)

<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Waiter Sweep?</p>

- The bottom player is underneath the opponent's hips in deep half guard with one leg available to be lifted and supported.
- Shift the opponent's weight enough to make the controlled leg available for elevation.
- Use an appropriate sleeve grip in gi or a suitable arm connection without assuming the same grip exists in no-gi.


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Waiter Sweep?</p>

- Recognize the leg shelf: A deep-half player supporting your elevated leg is setting the waiter mechanism.
- Keep an available post: Preserve hand or foot support before the opponent controls the tipping direction.
- Manage hip position: Avoid leaving the hips directly above an established deep-half shoulder connection.
- Recover the leg early: Return the elevated leg only while the knee can move without twisting.
- Fall without a locked-arm post: Accept a controlled reversal if balance is lost rather than risking a shoulder or knee.
- Retain guard on landing: Use inside frames and the remaining leg connection if the opponent comes on top.
