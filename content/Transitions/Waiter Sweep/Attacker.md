<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The bottom player is underneath the opponent's hips in deep half guard with one leg available to be lifted and supported. The waiter leg shelf and deep-half hip position distinguish this sweep from a generic underhook wrestle-up. The landing may retain half guard; side control requires clearing the legs.

Bring the shoulder under the hip while controlling the leg and protecting the exposed arm. Shift the opponent's weight enough to make the controlled leg available for elevation. Support the lower leg with the arm and shoulder connection while using the legs to help lift.

Use an appropriate sleeve grip in gi or a suitable arm connection without assuming the same grip exists in no-gi. Coordinate leg elevation and post control rather than trying to bench-press the opponent. Follow the hips to a stable top position; pass the remaining leg only if that separate opening exists.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

In Valle's gi demonstration, the collar connection manages the upper body while hip movement brings the lifted leg into the waiting hand; control of the posting sleeve then assists the reversal. Keep the head protected from a crossface as you move underneath. Once on top, establish hip control and balanced chest pressure for the actual landing before beginning a separate pass.

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Waiter Sweep?</p>

- Establish deep half: Bring the shoulder under the hip while controlling the leg and protecting the exposed arm.
- Create a balance reaction: Shift the opponent's weight enough to make the controlled leg available for elevation.
- Build the waiter shelf: Support the lower leg with the arm and shoulder connection while using the legs to help lift.
- Manage the posting arm: Use an appropriate sleeve grip in gi or a suitable arm connection without assuming the same grip exists in no-gi.
- Tip the controlled base: Coordinate leg elevation and post control rather than trying to bench-press the opponent.
- Come up and settle: Follow the hips to a stable top position; pass the remaining leg only if that separate opening exists.
