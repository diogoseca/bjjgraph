<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Retain the high enclosure without forcing neck pressure. Follow small posture changes with a supported hip angle. Keep the first connection if the second leg begins to open. Restore closed guard when the high enclosure releases and distance permits. Use open guard if the ankles cannot close comfortably.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Rubber Guard/Invisible Collar]] (Top)

</section>

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Invisible Collar Posture Recovery?</p>

- Begin as the top player caught by the bottom holder's high Invisible Collar leg enclosure.
- Keep the head and shoulders aligned instead of forcing posture through a closed enclosure.
- Release the high enclosure while respecting the position of the head, arm and raised knees.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Invisible Collar Posture Recovery?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: Use open guard if the ankles cannot close comfortably.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Follow small posture changes with a supported hip angle.
