<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**Demi Guard to Half Guard replaces a failing gi lapel-and-foot connection with a conventional one-leg trap while the bottom player keeps frames against the passer.**

When the foot-on-lapel barrier stops managing pressure, the nearby leg may still be available for ordinary half guard. This recovery exchanges the special fabric control for a leg trap; it does not turn every loose Demi position into half guard automatically.

The defining Demi configuration is documented by [Legion](https://legionsandiego.com/the-jiu-jitsu-dictionary/#demi-guard). The half-guard recovery is a conservative connection between that configuration and [[Positions/Half Guard]], requiring the leg to remain within reach and frames to prevent a completed pass. Once half guard is established, its own underhook, back-take and submission routes require their usual controls.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]] (Bottom)
**Success Rate**: gi only

## Playing as Attacker

### Key Principles


- Recognize loss of useful lapel tension early.

- Keep frames while changing the legs.

- Capture a reachable leg rather than stretching after it.

- Release fabric that prevents a safe change.

- Reestablish half-guard posture before attacking.

### Common Mistakes


- **Waiting until both knees have been passed**
  - Consequence: No reachable leg remains for the intended recovery.
  - Correction: Start the change when the lapel barrier first becomes ineffective.

- **Releasing all upper-body frames**
  - Consequence: The passer closes chest-to-chest space during the leg change.
  - Correction: Keep at least one effective frame while exchanging the controls.

- **Clamping while the lapel still binds a knee**
  - Consequence: The new trap can add tension to an already restricted joint.
  - Correction: Release the obstructing fabric before tightening the leg trap.

## Playing as Defender

[[Demi Guard to Half Guard/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Recognize the change of collar, lapel and foot controls early.

- Keep a usable base or leg barrier while contesting the movement.

- Choose a response that fits the current controls rather than a lost grip.

- Release any connection that binds a knee during movement.

- Identify the resulting position before continuing the exchange.


### Recognition Cues


- The bottom player removes the foot from the lapel tail.

- Both knees begin closing around one of your legs.

- Their hands change from fabric control to frames protecting their torso.


### Defensive Options


- **Keep the knee line outside the closing leg clamp** - When: Before both of the guard player's legs capture your leg

- **Contest the recovery while preserving base** - When: As the guard player exchanges foot contact for a leg clamp

- **Clear both legs and settle the pin** - When: When the opponent has released Demi without securing a leg trap
