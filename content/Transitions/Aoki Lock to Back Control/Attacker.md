<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The initial Aoki Lock Control configuration must be identified before considering Back Control. The captured limb is the foot and lower leg. This is distinct from an omoplata-style shoulder trap and from a bent-knee calf slicer. Either player can become the foot-lock attacker during an entanglement exchange. The reviewed instruction does not establish a direct Aoki Lock Control-to-Back Control connection. Release the original lock safely and establish the destination's own prerequisites; do not treat these checkpoints as a demonstrated continuous entry.

The opposing foot is turned outward and enclosed near the armpit, upper arm, and forearm. Rotational foot locks can injure the ankle or knee before pain provides useful warning. Use cooperative capture-and-release practice, no spinning escapes, and the event's actual division rules. Open the original connection before either partner turns, rises or changes the captured limb.

Establish a separate chest-to-back connection before reaching for hooks or a seatbelt. Use controlled upper-body and leg attachment while preserving neck alignment. Confirm that the opponent cannot simply turn to face you before considering a back attack.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

The separate rear attachment needs hip control as well as chest-to-back contact, so the opponent cannot simply turn toward you. The preceding foot capture must be fully released before using the legs for that new torso connection.
