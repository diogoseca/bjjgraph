<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The bottom player is already facing the opponent in Dead Orchard Control, with a high rubber-guard leg connection. The bottom player owns the arm trap. The opponent who must escape the trap is the top player, so releasing the guard and escaping its attack are different actions.

The bottom player faces the opponent and keeps the hips close beneath the shoulders. The legs form a high figure-four around the shoulder line, with both opposing arms inside. Release any wrist, neck or elbow loading before opening the high-leg enclosure. Lower the controlling leg through a comfortable hip angle rather than pulling the foot against the knee.

Bring the forearms and knees between the bodies as space becomes available. Keep the shoulders and hips oriented toward the opponent while re-establishing a comfortable facing guard. Check posture, knee comfort and inside space before resuming a sweep or submission attack.

Move through available space with a stable base. Stop and release any trapped neck, shoulder or knee before changing direction; do not force a roll or stack to preserve the route.

Open the both-arm leg triangle completely before a small hip escape restores inside frames. Move the hips only after the partner's shoulders and arms can follow freely.

Recover a forearm inside the passer's shoulder as the both-arm leg triangle opens. That frame prevents a crossface and chest pressure from filling the space where your knee needs to return for guard retention.
