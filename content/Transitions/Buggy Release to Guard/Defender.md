<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Wait for the choke pressure to release before changing angle. Maintain a balanced base as the loop opens. Address the incoming knee before half guard is established. Retain side-control contact without loading the opponent's neck. Recognize the actual half-guard connection if a leg is captured.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Buggy Choke]] (Bottom)

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Buggy Release to Guard?</p>

- Begin underneath the opponent with your arm-and-leg Buggy enclosure still present.
- Remove the choke pressure before opening the enclosure or asking the partner to change direction.
- Release the loop through a comfortable hip angle without pulling the raised knee against resistance.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Buggy Release to Guard?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: Recognize the actual half-guard connection if a leg is captured.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Maintain a balanced base as the loop opens.
