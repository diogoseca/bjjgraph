<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You are the passer trying to prevent the guard player from replacing the foot-on-lapel barrier with a leg trap. Track their knees and your controlled leg while keeping base. If they capture one leg between both of theirs, you must recognize the resulting half guard before continuing.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Demi Guard to Half Guard?</p>

- The bottom player removes the foot from the lapel tail.
- Both knees begin closing around one of your legs.
- Their hands change from fabric control to frames protecting their torso.


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Demi Guard to Half Guard?</p>

- Recognize the change of collar, lapel and foot controls early.
- Keep a usable base or leg barrier while contesting the movement.
- Choose a response that fits the current controls rather than a lost grip.
- Release any connection that binds a knee during movement.
- Identify the resulting position before continuing the exchange.


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Demi Guard to Half Guard?</p>

### 1. Keep the knee line outside the closing leg clamp

- **When to use**: Before both of the guard player's legs capture your leg
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]]
- **If successful**: The opponent must retain their existing Demi configuration.
- **Risk**: Pulling against uncleared fabric can bind your knee.

### 2. Contest the recovery while preserving base

- **When to use**: As the guard player exchanges foot contact for a leg clamp
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]]
- **If successful**: The change stalls while the original guard remains contested.
- **Risk**: Overcommitting weight can give the opponent an elevation opportunity.

### 3. Clear both legs and settle the pin

- **When to use**: When the opponent has released Demi without securing a leg trap
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Side Control]]
- **If successful**: You establish side control after denying the recovery.
- **Risk**: An incomplete clear leaves a conventional half guard.



</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Demi Guard to Half Guard?</p>

### 1. Responding after the new controls are established

- **Consequence**: The attacker completes the movement before the response affects it.
- **Correction**: Recognize the listed cues during the control change.

### 2. Turning against a knee still bound by the lapel

- **Consequence**: The joint cannot follow the body movement freely.
- **Correction**: Release binding tension and reset rather than forcing the turn.

### 3. Ignoring the position left after the attempt

- **Consequence**: You continue with a response intended for a guard that has already changed.
- **Correction**: Check the actual leg and upper-body controls before the next action.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train defense against Demi Guard to Half Guard?</p>

**Recognition** - Name the cue
From the stated origin, have the partner begin slowly. Call out a listed recognition cue, then release and reset.

**Cooperative response** - Practice the first defense
Keep the knee line outside the closing leg clamp. Use light resistance and stop when the response succeeds or the connection becomes uncomfortable.

**Bounded defense** - Recognize the outcome
Let the partner choose whether to continue or recover. Respond using the available controls; stop at the resulting stable position and name both players' roles.
