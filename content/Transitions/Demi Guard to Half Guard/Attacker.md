<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You are underneath as Demi's lapel connection becomes unreliable. Recover a conventional leg trap while keeping your torso facing the passer. Release cloth that obstructs the change and retain frames; the recovery ends at half guard rather than at an immediate sweep or submission.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Demi Guard]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Demi Guard to Half Guard?</p>

- Recognize loss of useful lapel tension early.
- Keep frames while changing the legs.
- Capture a reachable leg rather than stretching after it.
- Release fabric that prevents a safe change.
- Reestablish half-guard posture before attacking.


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Demi Guard to Half Guard?</p>

- You begin in Demi Guard bottom.
- The lapel or foot control is failing or no longer useful.
- One opponent's leg remains between or within reach of your legs.
- You can maintain frames that prevent a completed side-control pin.


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Demi Guard to Half Guard step by step?</p>

1. **Recognize the failing barrier**: Notice when the foot no longer has useful support from the lapel, before the passer clears both knees and reaches your torso.
2. **Keep your frames**: Use the remaining upper-body frames and facing angle to preserve space for the legs to change without surrendering chest control.
3. **Release obstructing fabric**: Let go of lapel tension that binds your leg movement, keeping another frame in place rather than reaching after lost cloth.
4. **Capture the nearby leg**: Bring both legs around the reachable opponent's leg without turning against a knee that is still caught in fabric.
5. **Settle half guard**: Confirm the one-leg trap and recover your side-facing posture with frames between the two upper bodies.
6. **Choose the next half-guard action**: Assess the passer's posture and upper-body controls before pursuing the existing half-guard sweep, back-take or recovery options.


</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Demi Guard to Half Guard?</p>

### 1. Waiting until both knees have been passed

- **Consequence**: No reachable leg remains for the intended recovery.
- **Correction**: Start the change when the lapel barrier first becomes ineffective.

### 2. Releasing all upper-body frames

- **Consequence**: The passer closes chest-to-chest space during the leg change.
- **Correction**: Keep at least one effective frame while exchanging the controls.

### 3. Clamping while the lapel still binds a knee

- **Consequence**: The new trap can add tension to an already restricted joint.
- **Correction**: Release the obstructing fabric before tightening the leg trap.

### 4. Chasing a distant leg with straight arms

- **Consequence**: The passer gains a path around your remaining barrier.
- **Correction**: Keep your torso protected and recover a reachable guard.

### 5. Attacking before confirming half guard

- **Consequence**: The opponent frees the leg during your premature reach.
- **Correction**: Confirm the leg trap and posture before changing to offense.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Demi Guard to Half Guard (Attacker)?</p>

**Recognition** - Identify the starting configuration
You begin in Demi Guard bottom. With a cooperative partner, identify the required grips and leg contacts, then reset without applying pressure.

**Cooperative sequence** - Practice the control change
Perform the listed sequence slowly with a cooperative partner. Pause at the change of lapel or foot control and reset on any joint discomfort.

**One response** - Recognize the first defense
Let the partner use this response: Keep the knee line outside the closing leg clamp. Practice keeping a safe base or guard when it blocks the attempt, then reset.

**Bounded exchange** - Confirm the landing
Start at the stated origin with light resistance. Stop and reset at a stable destination, a counter, or a request to release; do not continue through a bound knee.



</section>



<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Considerations

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the safety concerns for Demi Guard to Half Guard?</p>

Use controlled pressure with a cooperative partner when learning the lapel path. Release fabric immediately on a tap, verbal stop or discomfort. Do not rotate against a knee held by the lapel, and stop the drill when a stable landing is reached.
