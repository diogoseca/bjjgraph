<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**The worm-wrestling pressure counter closes distance as the bottom player leaves the worm wrap to come up, keeping the passer on top while the opponent may establish Demi Guard.**

[Keenan's Demi introduction](https://jiujitsux.com/courses/keenans-lapel-encyclopedia/lesson/16-1-introduction-to-the-demi-guard/) describes the passer switching from retreat to forward pressure to stop a worm-wrestling stand-up. The guard player has removed the foot from the original worm configuration and can answer with Demi.

Here the top player's immediate goal is to contain the attempt to come up, not to complete a pass. Landing in [[Positions/Demi Guard]] top means that goal has been met while a new guard remains. The opponent can still elevate through the foot-on-lapel connection, so continued pressure requires renewed attention to base and fabric control.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Worm Guard]] (Top)
**Success Rate**: gi only

## Playing as Attacker

[[Worm Wrestling Pressure Counter/Attacker|→ Full Attacker Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Recognize the foot leaving the original worm wrap.

- Respond to the attempt to come up rather than pressuring a static wrap.

- Close distance with a stable base.

- Recognize the replacement Demi connection immediately.

- Separate containing a stand-up from passing the guard.

### Common Mistakes


- **Applying this response before the foot leaves the wrap**
  - Consequence: The original worm connection still restricts the moving leg.
  - Correction: Wait for the actual get-up transition and confirm that the knee can move.

- **Reaching forward without moving the base**

  - Correction: Recognize the foot-on-tail connection and stop driving blindly.

## Playing as Defender

[[Worm Wrestling Pressure Counter/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Feel the shift from retreat to forward pressure.

- Keep a guard barrier available while beginning the rise.

- Retain useful lapel control without binding your own movement.

- Use a visible loss of base rather than forcing elevation.

- Recover and identify the actual guard before attacking again.


### Recognition Cues


- The passer stops retreating as your foot leaves the wrap.

- Their torso and base move forward together.

- The space needed to come up becomes smaller.


### Defensive Options


- **Retain the original wrap when the get-up has not committed** - When: Before the foot has fully left Worm Guard

- **Keep a leg barrier while denying the forward engagement** - When: While the passer is trying to close the remaining space

- **Redirect the forward weight into elevation** - When: When the passer loses base against your working lapel connection
