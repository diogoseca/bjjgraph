<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You are the bottom player attempting to come up from Worm Guard. The passer's forward movement can remove the space your get-up needs. Recover a working guard instead of insisting on the rise; Demi is the source-described response after the original foot configuration has been left.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Worm Guard]] (Top)

</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Worm Wrestling Pressure Counter?</p>

- The passer stops retreating as your foot leaves the wrap.
- Their torso and base move forward together.
- The space needed to come up becomes smaller.


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Worm Wrestling Pressure Counter?</p>

- Feel the shift from retreat to forward pressure.
- Keep a guard barrier available while beginning the rise.
- Retain useful lapel control without binding your own movement.
- Use a visible loss of base rather than forcing elevation.
- Recover and identify the actual guard before attacking again.


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Worm Wrestling Pressure Counter?</p>

### 1. Retain the original wrap when the get-up has not committed

- **When to use**: Before the foot has fully left Worm Guard
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Worm Guard]]
- **If successful**: You stay underneath in the original guard rather than allowing the pressure transition.
- **Risk**: Forcing the old wrap after it is lost can obstruct recovery.

### 2. Keep a leg barrier while denying the forward engagement

- **When to use**: While the passer is trying to close the remaining space
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Worm Guard]]
- **If successful**: The pressure counter stalls at the original guard.
- **Risk**: A late barrier can leave you needing a different guard.

### 3. Redirect the forward weight into elevation

- **When to use**: When the passer loses base against your working lapel connection
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **If successful**: You reverse the players and land on top while the former passer retains half guard.
- **Risk**: The passer can stabilize if their base remains intact.



</section>

<section id="favorable-outcomes" class="content-section">

## Best-Case Outcomes for Defender

<p class="section-subtitle">What is the best outcome when defending Worm Wrestling Pressure Counter?</p>

### → [[Positions/Worm Guard]]

Retain the original wrap and leg barrier early enough to stop the pressure transition.

### → [[Positions/Half Guard]]

Use a genuine loss of base to reverse the passer, then release binding fabric and stabilize on top.



</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Worm Wrestling Pressure Counter?</p>

### 1. Insisting on standing after the space has closed

- **Consequence**: The passer follows the rise without a remaining leg barrier.
- **Correction**: Recover a usable guard as the pressure returns.

### 2. Trying to rebuild the original wrap under all forward pressure

- **Consequence**: The old foot configuration may no longer be accessible.
- **Correction**: Recognize when the Demi connection better fits the available position.

### 3. Forcing elevation against a balanced passer

- **Consequence**: The lapel tension increases without a useful reversal.
- **Correction**: Keep guard and wait for a real change of base.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train defense against Worm Wrestling Pressure Counter?</p>

**Recognition** - Feel the pressure change
Begin a cooperative worm-wrestling get-up while the partner moves forward slowly. Name the change of space and reset.

**Recovery** - Replace the lost wrap
When the partner closes distance, recover a working guard. Stop and identify the resulting leg and lapel configuration.

**Bounded response** - Choose retention or reversal
With light resistance, retain guard or use a clear loss of base. Reset at a stable outcome, without forcing knee rotation.
