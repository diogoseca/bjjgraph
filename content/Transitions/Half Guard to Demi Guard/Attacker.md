<section id="overview" class="content-section">

From [[Positions/Half Guard]] bottom, first preserve frames and a facing angle before exchanging the leg trap for foot-on-lapel control. Your task is to replace the starting controls without losing the leg barrier. Work through the feed while facing the passer, and stop chasing it when the opponent has already cleared your knees.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Half Guard]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Half Guard to Demi Guard?</p>

- Keep the legs in front during the grip change.
- Arrange the collar control before relying on the lapel barrier.
- Route the lapel behind the knee without twisting the joint.
- Confirm foot contact on the tail before calling the entry complete.
- Retain the starting guard when the feed cannot be completed.


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Half Guard to Demi Guard?</p>

- You are in Half Guard bottom, facing the passer.
- The passer wears a gi with accessible lapel material.
- Your legs can still prevent direct chest-to-chest control.
- The knee line is accessible without forcing a bound joint.


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Half Guard to Demi Guard step by step?</p>

1. **Preserve the half-guard frames**: Stay facing the passer with a leg still captured and enough upper-body space to move your hands without losing the guard.
2. **Establish collar control**: Take the collar while the existing leg trap and frames prevent the passer from immediately connecting chest to chest.
3. **Bring the lapel to the knee line**: Make the lapel tail accessible beside the controlled knee without releasing the leg trap or forcing a feed through pressure.
4. **Route the fabric behind the knee**: Guide the lapel behind the knee only when there is clear space; retain ordinary half guard if the feed remains blocked.
5. **Exchange the leg control**: Open the leg clamp only as you can place a foot on the lapel tail and keep both knees working in front of your torso.
6. **Settle the new guard**: Confirm the foot-on-lapel barrier and collar connection, then release the old half-guard clamp rather than twisting between both structures.


</section>

## Opponent Counters

<p class="section-subtitle">How might your opponent counter Half Guard to Demi Guard?</p>

- **Contest the feed while preserving base** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Keep the remaining guard or base and reassess the controls before repeating the attempt; do not force a knee against the lapel. → Leads to [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **Prevent the foot from settling on the tail** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Keep the remaining guard or base and reassess the controls before repeating the attempt; do not force a knee against the lapel. → Leads to [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **Clear the legs during an abandoned feed** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Keep the remaining guard or base and reassess the controls before repeating the attempt; do not force a knee against the lapel. → Leads to [[Positions/Side Control]]

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Half Guard to Demi Guard?</p>

### 1. Releasing every starting control at once

- **Consequence**: The passer can advance through the unprotected space.
- **Correction**: Transfer one connection at a time while keeping the leg barrier.

### 2. Feeding cloth while your knees are already passed

- **Consequence**: The collar and lapel cannot replace missing guard frames.
- **Correction**: Recover your legs before attempting this entry.

### 3. Placing the foot on slack fabric

- **Consequence**: The foot has no useful connection to the opponent's knee.
- **Correction**: Recheck the lapel path and remove only enough slack to make contact work.

### 4. Forcing a feed around a bound knee

- **Consequence**: Turning against the trapped leg can create discomfort.
- **Correction**: Release and reset whenever the joint cannot move comfortably.

### 5. Mistaking a lapel grip for the completed guard

- **Consequence**: You attempt elevation before the control structure exists.
- **Correction**: Confirm collar, knee-line lapel and foot contact together.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Half Guard to Demi Guard (Attacker)?</p>

**Recognition** - Identify the starting configuration
You are in Half Guard bottom, facing the passer. With a cooperative partner, identify the required grips and leg contacts, then reset without applying pressure.

**Cooperative sequence** - Practice the control change
Perform the listed sequence slowly with a cooperative partner. Pause at the change of lapel or foot control and reset on any joint discomfort.

**One response** - Recognize the first defense
Let the partner use this response: Contest the feed while preserving base. Practice keeping a safe base or guard when it blocks the attempt, then reset.

**Bounded exchange** - Confirm the landing
Start at the stated origin with light resistance. Stop and reset at a stable destination, a counter, or a request to release; do not continue through a bound knee.



</section>



<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Considerations

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the safety concerns for Half Guard to Demi Guard?</p>

Use controlled pressure with a cooperative partner when learning the lapel path. Release fabric immediately on a tap, verbal stop or discomfort. Do not rotate against a knee held by the lapel, and stop the drill when a stable landing is reached.
