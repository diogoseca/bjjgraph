<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Recognize that this is an exploratory continuation rather than a validated finishing sequence. Keep the head and shoulders aligned while the neck is unloaded. Stop if the raised knee or neck encounters pressure. Recognize retained Hindu Control if the continuation is not established. Return to ordinary guard only after the high enclosure has actually released.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Hindulotine]] (Bottom)

</section>

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Hindulotine to Anaconda Choke?</p>

- Exploratory anaconda-style continuation reported by one practitioner against an unskilled partner, with retained New York and deeper foot/armpit alignment; no instructor endorsement or competitive validation. Anaconda Control/Top labels the controller seat and does not establish a physical sweep or require releasing the legs.
- The performer begins as the bottom Hindu Control holder, with the leg-in enclosure already established.
- The report describes retaining New York during the exploration; releasing the legs is not an established prerequisite.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Hindulotine to Anaconda Choke?</p>

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
