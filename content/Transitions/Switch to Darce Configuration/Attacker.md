<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You start underneath side control holding the Buggy Choke: your arm around the top player's head and one arm, connected to your raised leg. The switch is for one moment, when they posture up or press up with both hands to escape. Keep the arm around the head and arm, bring your far hand through under their stomach or armpit, then let go of the leg. Now come out from under: run or spin your legs through and walk your feet away until you reach their head, with them bent forward on their knees.

You finish this move in a front headlock, not in D'Arce control. Holding the head and arm from underneath does not reverse the players; coming out to the head does. The D'Arce is your next attack from the front headlock.

If they stay low and heavy, keep the Buggy. If they pull the head and arm free as the leg lets go, you are underneath side control: frame and protect your inside space.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Buggy Choke]] (Bottom)

</section>



<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Switch to Darce Configuration?</p>

- The trigger is the top player posturing up or pressing up with both hands to escape the Buggy; do not start the switch against a flat, heavy top player
- Keep the arm around the head and arm for the whole switch; the leg connection is the only thing you let go
- Reach the far hand under their stomach or armpit before you let go of the leg
- Coming out from under is its own movement: run or spin the legs through and walk the feet away until you are at their head
- The switch ends at the head in a front headlock; the D'Arce is attacked from there as the next move, not assumed during the switch


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Switch to Darce Configuration?</p>

- Buggy Choke from bottom side control: your arm around the top player's head and one arm, connected to your raised leg
- The top player postures up or presses up with both hands to escape the Buggy
- The head and arm are still trapped inside your arm
- Space under their stomach or armpit for your far hand as they lift


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Switch to Darce Configuration step by step?</p>

1. **Feel the posture**: Hold the Buggy and feel for the top player posturing up, or pressing up with both hands, to escape it. That reaction is the trigger. If they stay low and heavy, keep the Buggy rather than starting the switch.
2. **Keep the head and arm**: Keep your arm wrapped around their head and trapped arm. This connection stays on through every later step; it is what you take with you to the front headlock.
3. **Reach under the stomach**: As they lift, bring your far hand through underneath their stomach or armpit. Their posture is what makes the space for it.
4. **Let go of the leg**: Release the Buggy's connection to your raised leg. Only the leg comes off; the arm around the head and arm stays where it is.
5. **Come out from under**: Run or spin your legs through and walk your feet away, rotating out from underneath them instead of staying flat on your back.
6. **Arrive at the head**: Finish the rotation at their head, with them bent forward on their knees: a front headlock, the head and arm still held. Attack the D'Arce from here as the next move.


</section>

<section id="common-counters" class="content-section">

## Opponent Counters

<p class="section-subtitle">How might your opponent counter Switch to Darce Configuration?</p>

- **The top player stays low and heavy and never postures, so the trigger does not appear** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Keep the Buggy loop and its own attacks; start the switch only when they lift → Leads to [[Positions/Buggy Choke]]
- **The top player drops back down before your far hand gets under their stomach, closing the space** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Keep the leg connection and the Buggy, and wait for the next posture → Leads to [[Positions/Buggy Choke]]
- **The top player pulls the head and arm free as the leg lets go and settles their weight back on you** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: The connection is gone: frame and protect your inside space from bottom side control instead of chasing the neck → Leads to [[Positions/Side Control]]


</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Switch to Darce Configuration?</p>

### 1. Starting the switch while the top player is flat and heavy

- **Consequence**: There is no space under their stomach and nothing to come out through, so the Buggy is given up for nothing
- **Correction**: Wait for them to posture up or press up with both hands; that is the trigger

### 2. Letting go of the leg before the far hand is under their stomach or armpit

- **Consequence**: The loop opens with nothing replacing it, and the top player can clear the head and arm
- **Correction**: Reach under first, then let go of the leg

### 3. Letting the head and arm go during the rotation

- **Consequence**: You come out without the connection that makes the front headlock, or stay underneath side control
- **Correction**: Keep the arm around the head and arm from the first step to the last

### 4. Staying flat on your back after letting go of the leg

- **Consequence**: Nothing has changed position: the top player is still on top of you
- **Correction**: Run or spin the legs through and walk the feet away until you are at their head

### 5. Counting the grip change as being on top in D'Arce control

- **Consequence**: Holding the head and arm from underneath does not reverse the players; you are still on the bottom
- **Correction**: Treat coming out to the head as its own step, and attack the D'Arce from the front headlock afterwards

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Switch to Darce Configuration (Attacker)?</p>

**Week 1-2** - Trigger and order
Start in bottom Buggy with a cooperative partner. The partner postures up on cue; reach the far hand under their stomach, then let go of the leg. Stop there and check that the head and arm are still held.

**Week 3-4** - Coming out from under
Add the rotation: run or spin the legs through and walk the feet away until you reach the head, with the partner bent forward on their knees. Check that you finish at the head with the head and arm held.

**Week 5-6** - Reading the reaction
The partner chooses between staying heavy and posturing up. Keep the Buggy when they stay heavy and switch only when they lift. Count a repetition only when you reach the front headlock.

**Week 7-8** - Positional rounds
Rounds start in bottom Buggy. The partner tries to escape by posturing and to clear the head and arm. Score a front headlock reached with the head and arm held; restart when they settle side control.



</section>



<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Considerations

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the safety concerns for Switch to Darce Configuration?</p>

The Buggy and the D'Arce are both chokes built around the head and arm, so apply pressure progressively and release at the tap. While coming out from under with the head and arm held, move your body around their head rather than twisting their neck to turn them. Partners with neck or shoulder injuries should say so before drilling, and the defender should tap rather than posture hard against a secured neck.
