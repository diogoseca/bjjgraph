<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The Switch to Darce Configuration begins from Buggy Choke bottom, with the attacker underneath side control and holding a same-side arm-and-leg loop around the opponent's head and arm. Read the top player's movement against the loop and check whether it opens the armpit-to-neck channel. Release the raised-leg connection only when a separate head-and-arm entry is available. The attacking arm must then find the accessible armpit and neck path; there is no collar grip to withdraw and no existing top ride to preserve.

The darce configuration is mechanically distinct from the anaconda because the choking arm enters under the opponent's far armpit first and then wraps around the neck, whereas the anaconda wraps the neck first and connects under the armpit. This threading direction means the darce is favored when the opponent's far arm is elevated or posted wide, creating space under the armpit for arm insertion. The Buggy loop does not already supply that arm path: the raised leg must be released and the new head-and-arm enclosure established without assuming top control.

This transition depends on the opponent's arm position and the space their reaction actually creates. If the armpit stays closed, keep the Buggy loop and re-read the reaction. The darce configuration uses the attacking forearm and the opponent's trapped shoulder on opposite sides of the neck. Its grip does not by itself reverse the players: Darce Control/Top requires the former bottom player to establish top control as well. Sprawl and hip-walk details apply only after that top position has been reached, as a separate finishing continuation. If the opponent clears the loop and retains the pin, the attacker remains underneath side control.

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Switch to Darce Configuration?</p>

- Read the opponent's rotation toward the choking arm as the trigger to initiate the darce switch rather than forcing the original buggy choke angle
- Release the Buggy leg connection only for an available head-and-arm entry, then redirect the arm through the exposed armpit and around the neck
- Thread the choking arm under the opponent's far armpit first, then wrap around the back of the neck to establish the darce arm path
- Lock the figure-four grip with the choking arm's hand gripping your own bicep and the support arm's hand pressing the opponent's head or trapped shoulder down
- Maintain head-and-arm contact and protect inside space from underneath; a grip change alone does not provide chest-to-back pressure or top control
- Use sprawl and hip-walk mechanics only after top Darce Control is separately established; they are not available from the bottom Buggy starting position
- Prioritize threading depth over speed—a shallow darce grip that does not fully encircle the neck-armpit channel will not generate bilateral carotid compression


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Switch to Darce Configuration?</p>

- Buggy Choke bottom control is established with a same-side arm-and-leg loop enclosing the top player's head and one arm
- Opponent has begun rotating or turning toward the choking arm, exposing the far armpit and neck channel for darce arm threading
- Bottom player retains head-and-arm contact and a usable body angle without surrendering all inside space as the leg connection opens
- Opponent's far arm is elevated, posted, or separated from their body enough to allow threading under the far armpit
- The opponent's near arm is positioned such that the darce threading path between neck and far arm is open for arm insertion


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Switch to Darce Configuration step by step?</p>

1. **Recognize rotation trigger**: Feel how the top player moves against your Buggy loop and check whether their reaction exposes the armpit-to-neck channel. An elevated or posted arm may create access, but do not assume every turn supplies it. If the armpit stays closed, retain the Buggy loop from underneath and re-read the reaction.
2. **Release leg connection and redirect arm**: Open the arm connection to your raised leg without pulling the foot against the knee. Redirect the available arm toward the exposed armpit and neck while preserving useful head-and-arm contact. This replaces the bottom Buggy loop with a separate arm enclosure; it does not begin with a collar grip or a ride on the opponent's back.
3. **Thread under far armpit**: Thread through the available armpit first and then around the neck, only while that path is open. Work toward enough depth for the forearm to reach the opposite side of the neck with an opposing arm inside the loop. The arm path must be established from your actual bottom-side angle rather than assumed from a turtle-top grip.
4. **Secure opponent's head position**: Use your support arm (the arm that was not threading) to cup the back of the opponent's head or press their trapped shoulder down, preventing them from posturing up or extracting their head from the darce channel. The opponent's near arm should be trapped between their own head and your choking arm, acting as the compression fulcrum.
5. **Lock figure-four grip**: Connect the figure-four by gripping your own bicep with the choking arm's hand while the support arm's hand presses the back of the opponent's head or their trapped shoulder firmly downward. Bring your elbows together to eliminate slack in the grip structure and confirm a separate head-and-arm enclosure before any finishing pressure.
6. **Consolidate darce control position**: Walk your hips toward the opponent's head and sprawl your legs back to create a perpendicular angle between your body and theirs. Drop your weight low against the mat and let gravity drive the compression downward through the figure-four structure. The opponent's own trapped shoulder provides the secondary compression point against the opposite carotid.
7. **Verify top Darce Control**: Squeeze your elbows together while maintaining the sprawl and hip angle to finish the darce choke. If the opponent defends by rolling to their back, follow them and maintain the grip—the darce finishes effectively from this angle as well. If the grip is not deep enough to finish, maintain darce control and work to improve arm depth before re-attempting the squeeze.


</section>

<section id="common-counters" class="content-section">

## Opponent Counters

<p class="section-subtitle">How might your opponent counter Switch to Darce Configuration?</p>

- **Opponent tucks chin hard and clamps far elbow tight to ribs, denying the armpit threading path during the redirect phase** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: If the far armpit is blocked, keep the Buggy arm-and-leg loop and re-read the reaction from underneath side control before attempting a separate darce entry → Leads to [[Positions/Buggy Choke]]
- **Opponent extracts their near arm from the trapped position before the figure-four grip is secured, eliminating the compression fulcrum** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Without the trapped arm, do not treat the partial neck wrap as darce control. Retain or recover the Buggy loop only if its head-and-arm connection remains available, then re-read the opening → Leads to [[Positions/Buggy Choke]]
- **Opponent clears the head-and-arm enclosure during the grip transition and settles their weight back into side control** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Accept that the choke connection is lost and protect yourself with inside frames from bottom side control. Recover usable guard space before considering a new attack rather than chasing a neck that has already been freed → Leads to [[Positions/Side Control]]
- **Opponent keeps the elbow tight and maintains top pressure, closing the space needed for the darce arm to thread** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Keep the Buggy loop while it remains available and wait for an actual opening. Do not attempt a snapdown or assume a top ride from underneath the opponent's pressure → Leads to [[Positions/Buggy Choke]]

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Switch to Darce Configuration?</p>

### 1. Abandoning the Buggy leg connection and all head-and-arm contact before the new threading path is available

- **Consequence**: Opponent clears the loop and settles side-control pressure while the bottom player has neither the original Buggy enclosure nor a new darce grip
- **Correction**: Confirm the armpit opening before releasing the leg connection, preserve useful contact during the arm change and keep inside space protected from underneath

### 2. Attempting the darce switch when the opponent has not rotated and the far armpit remains closed

- **Consequence**: The threading path does not exist, resulting in a shallow grip that cannot generate choking pressure and wasting the existing buggy choke position
- **Correction**: Only initiate the darce switch when you feel genuine rotation toward your choking arm and the far arm is posted or elevated. If the opponent is static, maintain the buggy choke or wait for a defensive reaction

### 3. Threading the choking arm too shallow under the far armpit without achieving full neck encirclement

- **Consequence**: The grip cannot lock at the correct depth, creating a loose darce that the opponent easily defends by posturing up or stripping with minimal effort
- **Correction**: Drive the choking arm deep under the far armpit until the forearm blade seats fully across the throat against the near-side carotid. Prioritize depth over speed for reliable finishing pressure

### 4. Failing to keep the opponent's near arm trapped inside the darce channel during the transition

- **Consequence**: Without the trapped arm acting as a compression fulcrum, the darce becomes a one-sided neck crank rather than a bilateral carotid choke, dramatically reducing finishing percentage
- **Correction**: Keep the opposing arm enclosed beside the head while arranging the new grip. Verify that the arm remains inside the loop without assuming chest weight from a top position you have not reached

### 5. Trying to apply a top sprawl finish while still underneath the opponent in the Buggy starting position

- **Consequence**: The required top body angle is absent, and squeezing the arms cannot substitute for establishing the head-and-arm control and top position
- **Correction**: Treat top control as a separate requirement before using sprawl and hip-walk mechanics; do not count a grip change from underneath as a completed positional reversal

### 6. Releasing the Buggy arm-and-leg loop before confirming an available darce arm path

- **Consequence**: The opponent can free the head and arm and restore the side-control pin before the new enclosure is established
- **Correction**: Check the opening while retaining the Buggy loop, then coordinate leg release with the new arm entry. If the path stays shut, keep the original control and reassess

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Switch to Darce Configuration (Attacker)?</p>

**Week 1-2** - Grip mechanics isolation
Practice with the attacking player underneath side control and the partner inside an unloaded Buggy arm-and-leg loop. Identify the available armpit before releasing the leg connection, then rehearse only the separate arm path. Drill 15-20 repetitions per side with pauses to check the starting role, knee support and new head-and-arm connection.

**Week 3-4** - Rotation recognition and timing
Partner provides light resistance and controlled movements from top side control. Practice reading whether the reaction actually opens the armpit for a darce entry while retaining the Buggy loop when it does not. Distinguish an accessible arm path from a closed elbow position without assuming that a turn has reversed the players.

**Week 5-6** - Transition chains and decision tree
Partner provides controlled reactions including elbow clamping, arm recovery and renewed side-control pressure. Practice retaining the Buggy loop when the darce path is closed and recovering inside frames when the loop is lost. Treat an established darce grip and a completed change to top control as separate checkpoints rather than importing a turtle-top attack chain.

**Week 7-8** - Competition simulation and finishing
Positional practice starts with the Buggy attacker underneath side control. The attacker reads the top player's defenses and checks the available arm path; the partner works to deny it and clear the original loop. Count Darce Control/Top only when both the head-and-arm enclosure and top position are actually established. A sprawl finish belongs after that positional change, not at the starting checkpoint.



</section>



<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Considerations

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the safety concerns for Switch to Darce Configuration?</p>

The darce choke is a blood choke that compresses both carotid arteries simultaneously, producing unconsciousness within seconds when properly applied. Always apply the grip progressively during training and allow adequate time for your partner to recognize the pressure and tap before the choke fully engages. The arm threading under the far armpit can strain the opponent's shoulder if forced aggressively, so thread smoothly rather than yanking. Partners should tap early when bilateral pressure is felt rather than waiting for vision changes. Practitioners with cervical spine issues should communicate this before drilling, as the head pressure component can aggravate existing neck conditions. Never apply cranking or twisting force—the darce is a squeeze, not a crank. Release immediately upon tap and monitor your partner for delayed symptoms after successful applications.
