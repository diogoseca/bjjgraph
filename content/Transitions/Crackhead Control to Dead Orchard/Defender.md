<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Keep your elbows compact before both arms enter the high legs. Maintain an inside frame before the second leg closes the enclosure. Move shoulder and elbow together while the leg connection is still open. If the second high-leg arrangement remains, recognize Crackhead Control rather than assuming the guard has opened. If the high connection is lost, recover posture inside the remaining closed guard.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Rubber Guard/Crackhead Control]] (Bottom)

</section>

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Crackhead Control to Dead Orchard?</p>

- Begin as the bottom player with Crackhead's second leg reinforcing the first high-leg clinch.
- Keep the knees supported by the hip angle; do not pull a foot against a fixed knee to make the transition.
- Use the double-bagger sequence to organize both high-leg connections while keeping the opponent close.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Crackhead Control to Dead Orchard?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: If the high connection is lost, recover posture inside the remaining closed guard.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Maintain an inside frame before the second leg closes the enclosure.
