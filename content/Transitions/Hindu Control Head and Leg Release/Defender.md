<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Keep the leg-in control connected while the opponent's neck remains unloaded. Follow an early posture change without twisting the head. Release immediately at a tap or verbal stop. Return to ordinary closed guard if the enclosure clears and the distance permits. Use facing open guard when a closed guard cannot comfortably form.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Hindulotine]] (Top)

## How to Recognize This Attack

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Hindu Control Head and Leg Release?</p>

- Begin as the top player facing the bottom Hindu Control holder, with the head-and-leg enclosure still present.
- Protect head and neck alignment; tap instead of rotating against established pressure.
- Work on releasing the enclosure while the head and leg can still move without forced joint pressure.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Hindu Control Head and Leg Release?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: Use facing open guard when a closed guard cannot comfortably form.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Follow an early posture change without twisting the head.
