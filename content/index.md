**Remember what you learned in BJJ class**

**BJJ Graph is a free Brazilian jiu-jitsu study app** that connects positions,
techniques, escapes, and submissions in an interactive map. Use it between classes
to revisit a move, understand what comes next, and test your memory with flashcards
and simulated rolls. It runs in your browser on a phone or computer. You can start
without an account.

**Find a useful starting point**

- **"I keep forgetting techniques after class."** Look up the position or move you
  practiced, review its explanation, and use the flashcards to check what you remember.
- **"I'm new to BJJ. What should I learn?"** Start with [[Positions|the position guides]]
  and [[Learning/Position Over Submission|position before submission]]. Use
  [[Principles|the principles]] to connect unfamiliar technique names to common ideas.
- **"I get stuck underneath mount."** Open [[Positions/Mount/Bottom|mount from the bottom]]
  to explore the position and connected escapes. For side control, start with
  [[Positions/Side Control/Bottom|side control from the bottom]].
- **"How do I connect my techniques?"** Follow the map from a position into a move
  and its possible outcomes, or explore [[Systems|systems]] of related techniques.

## The whole Brazilian jiu-jitsu game, mapped as one graph

Explore [[Positions|positions]] from both sides: [[Positions/Mount|mount]] changes
from top to bottom, while [[Positions/Closed Guard|closed guard]] gives the person
underneath attacking options. Follow [[Transitions|transitions]] such as
[[Transitions/Knee Slice Pass|the knee slice pass]] into their possible next positions,
or explore [[Submissions|submissions]] such as [[Submissions/Rear Naked Choke|the rear naked choke]] from [[Positions/Back Control|back control]].

[[Principles|Principles]] explain shared mechanics; [[Systems|systems]] connect
techniques into game plans. Start with [[Learning|the learning articles]].

**Questions before you start**

**Is BJJ Graph free? Do I need an account?**

BJJ Graph is free and currently in beta. Public guides and study tools are available
without signing in. Your study progress stays in your browser. Optional Google or
email sign-in lets you sync progress across devices. See the [terms](/terms) and
[privacy policy](/privacy) for details.

**Is it useful for beginners?**

Beginners can use BJJ Graph to learn position names and revisit ideas from class.
More experienced grapplers can explore connected techniques and systems. Start
with a position you already practiced, then review a small number of related moves.

**Can it replace classes or predict whether a technique will work?**

Use BJJ Graph alongside instruction at a BJJ academy. Reading, flashcards, and
simulated rolls help with study; they do not replace coached practice. The graph's
probabilities are model estimates, not measured success rates for you or guarantees
about a real roll. Content is under active development and can contain errors.

BJJ Graph is operated by Diogo Seca. You can report a content issue through the
[public project](https://github.com/diogoseca/bjjgraph/issues).
