## The whole Brazilian jiu-jitsu game, mapped as one graph

Brazilian jiu-jitsu is usually taught as a list of techniques. It is actually played as a
**state machine**: you are always in some position, you always have a handful of moves worth
attempting, and each attempt has a real chance of working, failing, or being countered.

BJJ Graph maps that machine. Every [[Positions|position]] is a state with two sides to play —
[[Positions/Mount|Mount]] is a very different problem from the top than from the bottom, and
[[Positions/Closed Guard|Closed Guard]] rewards the person underneath. Every
[[Transitions|transition]] is a node with weighted outcomes, so
[[Transitions/Knee Slice Pass|the knee slice pass]] does not merely "exist" — it lands you in a
specific next position a specific percentage of the time. Every
[[Submissions|submission]] ends the match, whether that is a
[[Submissions/Rear Naked Choke|rear naked choke]] from [[Positions/Back Control|back control]]
or an armbar from an escape gone wrong.

Because it is a graph rather than a curriculum, it answers questions a syllabus cannot: what
actually leads here, what is worth attempting from here, and which of your escapes are load
bearing. The [[Principles|principles]] section covers the mechanics that generalize across
positions, and [[Systems|systems]] bundles related nodes into coherent games — closed guard
sweeps, leg entanglements, pressure passing.

Explore the graph, roll through a random sequence, or work the flashcards built from every node.
