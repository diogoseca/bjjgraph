## Lapel Guard: Study the Entry Obstacles

**A topic guide using Keenan Cornelius’s published lapel-denial discussion to identify what a lapel player needs before an entry can develop.**


The selected article explains lapel entry from the passer’s perspective. For the guard player, it offers a way to distinguish having a lapel grip from having the fabric and leg access needed to develop an entanglement.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- You can reach a lapel but cannot tell why the intended guard entry remains unavailable.


### Consider an alternative if

- For a guard curriculum organized around two sleeve grips and lasso/spider configurations, compare Jonathan Thomas’s guide.


</section>

## Start here: Compare a lapel grip with usable lapel length

Read Tip #1 and compare the article’s description of one freed lapel with its description of freeing both. In your study notes, separate possession of the grip from available fabric length. Then read Tip #2 and note why the passer’s approach may prevent the bottom player from using that grip.

<section id="coverage" class="content-section">

## What the sources cover

- The article separates taking slack out of the lapel, approaching outside the legs and using a foot-control grip.
- Its first section explicitly distinguishes merely holding the lapel from freeing enough fabric for an entry.

<span id="common-obstacles"></span><span id="assessment-metrics"></span>

### Limits

- This selected source concerns denying entry; it is not a full account of worm, squid or their sweep sequences.
