## Giles and Tabak: Guard Retention Around and Under

**A companion to Around and Under that routes study by passing direction and distinguishes retention from later offense.**


This release organizes retention by the passer’s route and then adds specific situations. Identify the route first; a familiar pass name alone may hide where the exchange became difficult.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- Your recurring guard loss involves a passer moving around the legs, toward north-south, or underneath the legs.


### Consider an alternative if

- If you can retain guard and now need posture-specific K-guard entries and offense, compare Lachlan Giles’s K-Guard guide.


</section>

## Start here: Name the route before the pass

Use one guard-loss exchange from your notes or footage. Record whether the passer went around your legs, reached north-south, or went underneath them. Match that description to parts 3, 4 or 5, and compare it with the opening general-concepts section before choosing a specific-pass lesson.
