## Blue to Purple: Build a Study Inventory

**Organize what you can explain from top and bottom, then separate study priorities from promotion eligibility.**


When your notes contain many techniques but leave some positions blank, an inventory can make the next study choice clearer. Kesting’s Sample Technique List separates submissions, top transitions and bottom transitions. Use those categories to organize your existing learning. Belt recognition is a separate question addressed by the IBJJF graduation rules and your professor.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- You are a blue belt with scattered technique notes and want a specific subject to bring to your next coaching conversation.


### Consider an alternative if

- If your immediate goal is a tournament, Competition Strategy narrows the inventory to the options you intend to use at that event.

## Start here: Find a gap in the same position from opposite roles

Open the Sample Technique List and select a position you encounter often. Sort your own notes into its top-transition, bottom-transition and submission categories. Mark a category you cannot yet explain and take that specific gap to your instructor. Use Article 3 separately if you need to check belt-recognition eligibility.

Published section: Kesting: Sample Technique List, printed pages 29–30. IBJJF graduation: Article 3.
