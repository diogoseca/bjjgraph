## Priit Mihkelson: Turtle Defense and Exits

**Priit Mihkelson's turtle course organizes posture and threat responses before presenting routes back to guard or standing.**


Choose this material when your difficult exchange begins in turtle. The outline lets you distinguish an early posture question from a hook, harness or choke problem, then locate an exit destination instead of treating remaining in turtle as the entire objective.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- You repeatedly reach turtle and want to separate posture study from responses to a particular attack.
- You want to compare returning to guard with standing from turtle.


### Consider an alternative if

- You are already held in rear mount: Back Defense Protocol points to Brian Glick's separate overhook-side, underhook-side and body-triangle sections.


</section>

## Start here: Separate the turtle threat from the exit destination

Choose a turtle exchange from your own footage or notes. Name the attack you can actually identify, then choose which destination you wanted: guard or standing. Find that threat in Volumes 2–3 and compare the guard-return and standing entries in Volume 4. If the attack is unclear, start your course study at Turtle Posture and record what the instructor uses to distinguish the situations before selecting a later lesson.

Published section: Volume 1: Turtle Posture (8:33–12:57); Volume 4: Shin Frame Spin to Guard (20:06–22:13) and Standing from Turtle (30:08–34:16)

## What the sources cover

- Volume 1 lists posture, neck and elbow topics, resistance to pushing and pulling, and hooks.
- Volumes 2–3 address harnesses, clock chokes, passing situations, wrestling tilts, front-headlock submissions and crucifix threats.
- Volume 4 moves from hip-pull and harness responses to spin escapes, guard returns, Granby movement and standing.

<span id="common-obstacles"></span><span id="assessment-metrics"></span>

### Limits

- The course is organized around turtle; established rear-mount escapes have a more direct study route in the Glick companion.
- The published lesson names distinguish threats and exits but do not explain the posture adjustments or movements.

</section>
