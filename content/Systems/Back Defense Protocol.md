## Back Defense: Brian Glick's Rear-Mount Study Guide

**Brian Glick's Only Way Out: Dynamic Back Escapes separates rear-mount escapes by overhook side, underhook side, body triangle and special control situations.**


Two exchanges both called back control may require different study sections. Glick's outline separates side-specific escapes from lower-body restrictions and strangle defenses, which makes it useful when a general back-escape lesson does not match the position you keep encountering.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- You need to find the lesson that matches your particular rear-mount configuration.
- Your recurring problem involves a trapped arm, body triangle or a strangle attempt during an escape.


### Consider an alternative if

- Your exchange still begins in turtle without established rear mount: Priit Mihkelson Turtle Defense System has a more direct posture-and-exit outline.

## Start here: Identify the back-control configuration before choosing an escape

Pause a back-control exchange at the moment you would begin an escape. Record the upper-body control separately from the legs: which side can you identify, and are hooks or a body triangle visible? Use Volumes 2–4 to locate the matching section. If an arm is trapped or a strangle is already being attempted, mark that as a separate study problem and locate Volume 1 or 6 rather than assuming the same starting conditions.

Published section: Volume 1: Essential Skills - 1 - Gripping - Defensive Hand Fighting Fundamentals (24:53); Volumes 2–4: side-specific and body-triangle escapes

## What the sources cover

- Volume 1 introduces rear mount, defensive hand fighting, trapped-arm situations and four named essential skills.
- Volumes 2–4 organize overhook-side, underhook-side and body-triangle escapes; Volume 5 adds crucifix, cross-body ride and standing cases.
- Volume 6 distinguishes early, middle and late strangle defenses; Volume 7 supplies an executive summary.

<span id="common-obstacles"></span><span id="assessment-metrics"></span>

### Limits

- This is a rear-control escape course; mount and side-pin recovery are outside its main organizing sections.
- Strangle-defense chapter labels identify stages but do not establish how to recognize or execute a late defense.
