## John Danaher: Pin Escapes and Turtle Escapes

**John Danaher's Pin Escapes & Turtle Escapes connects general escape concepts with mount, side-pin, north-south, rear-mount and turtle material.**


The outline devotes several volumes to side-position escapes rather than treating all pins as one problem. A useful way into this long course is to identify both the starting pin and the destination you want to understand, then compare variants within that section.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- You need an organized introduction to recovery from several bottom positions.
- You want to compare mount or side-pin escape families before choosing one lesson to study closely.


### Consider an alternative if

- Established rear mount is your main problem: Back Defense Protocol offers Glick's more finely divided overhook-side, underhook-side and body-triangle outline.

## Start here: Compare the starting pin with the destination

Choose one mount or side-control exchange and describe its first and last recognizable positions. Did it end in guard, standing, another pin or an unclear scramble? For mount, locate the bridge and elbow-escape families in Volume 2; for side control, compare the elbow material beginning in Volume 3 with the knee-escape material in Volume 4. Begin course viewing with Escapes Overview to check how Danaher defines the outcome you are trying to study.

Published section: Volume 1: Escapes Overview (6:56–43:37); Volume 2: Bridging Escape From Mounted Position (UPA) (0:00) and Elbow Escape From Mounted Position (30:01)

## What the sources cover

- Volume 1 covers escape goals, pinning concepts and body movements; Volumes 2–3 develop mount bridging and elbow-escape material.
- Volumes 3–6 address side-position elbow and knee escapes, framing, insertion points and several alternative escape families.
- Volume 7 includes knee-on-belly and north-south; Volume 8 covers rear mount and turtle.

<span id="common-obstacles"></span><span id="assessment-metrics"></span>

### Limits

- The organizing subject is positional escape; a specific submission already in progress may call for a separate submission-defense lesson.
- The outline names minimal and positive escapes but does not define those categories in its public text.
