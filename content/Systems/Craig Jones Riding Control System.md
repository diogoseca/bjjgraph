## Craig Jones: Riding Control with Power Ride

**Use Power Ride to separate leg control, upper-body control and turtle riding when studying why a top position fails to hold.**


Power Ride: A New Philosophy on Pinning by Craig Jones organizes top control through lower-body tools, upper-body tools and turtle rides. This guide helps locate the kind of control problem you want to investigate before moving to the course’s submission material.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- You reach top position but cannot identify which part of your control breaks first.


### Consider an alternative if

- For a study anchored specifically to mounted position and remounting, compare Gordon Ryan’s mount course.

## Start here: Compare what each control section is trying to explain

Read the free Volume 2 and Volume 3 contents beside a note about one top position you recently lost. Mark whether your unanswered question concerns the legs, upper body, or both. Then locate the matching entry position in Volume 5. With course access, begin with Standard Operating Procedure before combining those sections.

## What the sources cover

- Separate volumes on leg control and upper-body tools.
- Turtle rides and entries from side control, mount and passing positions.
- A final volume connecting control topics to submission topics.
