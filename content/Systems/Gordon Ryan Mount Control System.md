## Gordon Ryan: Mount Maintenance and Attacks

**Choose between mount entry, maintenance, remounting and attack sections instead of treating every loss of mount as the same problem.**


Systematically Attacking From Top Pins: Mount by Gordon Ryan lists mount entries before maintenance, recovery and submission sections. Rolling and commentary follow later. Use that organization to decide whether your immediate question concerns reaching mount, staying there, or attacking from it.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- You reach mount but lose it during a bridge, a recovery attempt or the start of an attack.


### Consider an alternative if

- For lower- and upper-body riding tools across several top positions, compare Craig Jones’s Power Ride.


</section>

## Start here: Distinguish arrival from the first escape attempt

Recall one mount loss and note whether it happened while arriving, during a bridge, or while starting an attack. Compare the outline’s Countering a Bridge entries in Volumes 1 and 5 with Volume 3’s Countering a Push & Bridge. Their surrounding chapters identify different study contexts. With access, start in Volume 2’s maintenance introduction and select the relevant context afterward.

Published section: Volume 2: Mount Maintenance Intro (0).

## What the sources cover

- Mount entries and a dedicated maintenance volume.
- Remounting from three-quarter mount and responses to bridge or knee-elbow recovery.
- Arm-isolation and submission topics, followed by rolling and commentary.

<span id="common-obstacles"></span><span id="assessment-metrics"></span>

### Limits

- Mount is the organizing position; side-control and north-south maintenance are covered by a separate course.
