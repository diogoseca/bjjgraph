## Planning a BJJ Lesson

**Audit a lesson’s purpose, explanation and understanding checks with a published BJJ teaching format.**


A demonstration can show a movement without making its purpose clear. John Connors and Jon Grayzel describe a four-part presentation format and prompted recall in Teaching BJJ to Kids. The article says the presentation format is also used in adult classes. This guide uses that example to inspect lesson design.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- You explain techniques and want to check whether students understand the problem each lesson addresses.


### Consider an alternative if

- For planning your own position study, use Blue to Purple Progression; this page concerns presenting a lesson to other people.


</section>

## Start here: Audit a lesson you already teach

Read the presentation and recall paragraphs. Take notes from one of your own lessons and label where you explain the purpose, identify the technique, describe execution and give students an opportunity to apply it. The article’s “What If?” phase is partner application with gradually increasing resistance. Inspect how your notes distinguish demonstration from student practice; identify an unclear practice objective without adding a drill. Draft a recall prompt and a helpful hint.

## What the sources cover

- A presentation format separating purpose, identification and execution from students applying the technique with gradually increasing resistance.
- Recall prompts with hints when a student struggles.

<span id="common-obstacles"></span><span id="assessment-metrics"></span>

### Limits

- One instructor’s account does not establish a universal curriculum or a ranking of teaching methods.
- The article does not supply promotion criteria or a certification process for instructors.
