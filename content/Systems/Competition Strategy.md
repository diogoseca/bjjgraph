## Competition Strategy: Map Your Intended Game

**Turn a broad technique collection into a position-by-position plan for a specific tournament.**


Watson BJJ’s Gameplan / Strategy Map distinguishes general development from tournament preparation: the competition version lists the game you plan to use at that event. Its separate top and bottom sections make missing answers visible. Use the map to organize familiar options, then check their scoring and legality against your event’s rules.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- You have several favorite techniques but no written plan for where a match might leave you.


### Consider an alternative if

- If the main uncertainty is what scored, Competition Scoring Strategy is the more focused starting point.


</section>

<section id="start-here" class="content-section">

## Start here: Compare your planned route with your last match

Use the blank map on pages 2–3. Enter your intended standing choice and the position you expect next using techniques you already study. Trace one recent match through the corresponding boxes and circle the first box where your plan had no entry. Put a familiar option there for discussion with your coach; keep a separate list for techniques still to learn.

Published section: Gameplan / Strategy Map: Competition Prep, page 1; worksheet, pages 2–3.

<section id="coverage" class="content-section">

## What the sources cover

- Separate planning spaces for standing, guard, pins, turtle and leg-attack situations.
- A distinction between options you intend to use now and material you want to develop later.

<span id="common-obstacles"></span><span id="assessment-metrics"></span>

### Limits

- The worksheet is a planning aid, not evidence that a listed sequence will succeed.
- Its technical examples are not a universal game plan or a division-specific legality list.
