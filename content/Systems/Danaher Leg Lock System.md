## John Danaher: Leglocks — Enter The System

**John Danaher’s Leglocks: Enter The System organizes leg-attack study around inside position, ashi variations, knee-line problems and entries.**


If collecting entries has left you unsure which positional problem to study, this syllabus offers separate starting points for gaining inside position and recovering the knee line. Later volumes expand into submission mechanics and entries from standing and the ground.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- You want to distinguish gaining an entanglement from recovering control after it starts to slip.


### Consider an alternative if

- For an ankle-lock-centered course organized around seated and kneeling opponents, compare Jason Rau Ankle Lock System.

## Start here: Separate entry trouble from recovery trouble

In the free Volume 2 outline, locate the lessons on attaining inside position, the knee line and recovering it. Match one recurring problem from your own training notes to entry or recovery. If you have the course, begin with the matching lesson and record the starting situation it actually demonstrates.

Published section: Volume 2: Knee and Knee Line (48:48–53:54); Recovering Knee Line (1:17:43–1:22:41).

## What the sources cover

- Inside-position and knee-line study in Volumes 1–2; ashi configurations and heel hooks in Volume 3.
- Later volumes address rotational resistance, leg switches and entries from standing and ground positions.

### Limits

- The curriculum extends into heel hooks and toe holds; it is broader than straight-ankle-lock study.
