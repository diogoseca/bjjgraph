## Marcelo Garcia: X-Guard Entries and Sweeps

**An X-guard companion separating the course’s routes underneath the opponent from sweeping finishes and counter responses.**


The first two volumes emphasize entries, the third concentrates on finishes and the fourth collects responses and transitions. Collar and sleeve examples appear in the outline alongside butterfly and shin-to-shin routes.

</section>

<section id="fit" class="content-section">

## Is this useful for you?

- You want to distinguish a failed X-guard entry from a stalled stand-up sweep or an opponent’s pummel.


### Consider an alternative if

- For a gi single-leg-X emphasis with sleeve/lapel sweep variations, compare Adam Wardzinski’s guide.


</section>

## Start here: Track the stage at which the exchange stalls

Use the chapter groupings to label an X-guard exchange as entry, finish or counter response. For a stand-up sweep problem, compare the initial finish with the following opponent-pulls-away lesson. For a connection lost before the finish, search the entry or pummel sections instead.

## What the sources cover

- Butterfly, grip-based and shin-to-shin entry sections.
- Stand-up, push-back and tilt finishes; pummel, backstep and single-leg-X transition material.

### Limits

- Several entries and counters explicitly use collar or sleeve grips; this is not exclusively a no-gi syllabus.
