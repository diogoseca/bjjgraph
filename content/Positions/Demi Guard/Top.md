<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You are the passer above an opponent who has connected your collar and knee line with your own lapel. Their foot presses on the lapel tail, and their bent legs remain between your bodies. Driving forward can load that structure instead of bringing you closer to a pass. Keep a usable base while addressing the fabric and foot connection.

Clearing the lapel may leave you facing [[Positions/Open Guard]] or caught in [[Positions/Half Guard]]. Neither is a completed pass. Advance to [[Positions/Side Control]] only after clearing the legs and controlling the upper body. The defensive priorities here are applications of the published configuration; they do not claim to reproduce an unavailable Demi passing lesson.

</section>



<section id="state-invariants" class="content-section">

## Position Definition

<p class="section-subtitle">What is Demi Guard (Top)?</p>

- The top player's torso faces the bottom player while one knee is controlled by the lapel running behind it.
- The bottom player's bent legs remain in front of the passer, with one foot pressing against the lapel tail.
- The collar grip connects the passer's upper body to the guard player while the passer still has space to adjust base.


</section>

<section id="prerequisites" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before playing Demi Guard (Top)?</p>

- Your opponent has established the collar and knee-line lapel connections.
- Their foot remains engaged with the lapel tail.
- You are still facing a guard rather than holding a completed pin.


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Offensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for attacking from Demi Guard?</p>

- Keep balance while identifying the loaded foot and fabric.
- Reduce the lapel connection before committing forward weight.
- Move the controlled leg only when the fabric permits it.
- Recognize a remaining leg trap as half guard.
- Complete leg clearance before settling a side-control pin.


</section>

<section id="decision-tree" class="content-section">

## Decision Making from This Position

<p class="section-subtitle">What should you do from Demi Guard (Top)?</p>

**The foot-on-lapel barrier is intact and prevents a safe passing step:**
- Execute **[[Transitions/Lapel Clear from Demi Guard/Attacker|Lapel Clear from Demi Guard]]** → [[Open Guard/Top]] (Probability: gi only)


**You can clear the fabric and control the legs without losing balance:**
- Execute **[[Transitions/Pass from Demi Guard/Attacker|Pass from Demi Guard]]** → [[Side Control/Top]] (Probability: gi only)


**The bottom player starts elevating your controlled knee:**
- Execute **[[Transitions/Lapel Clear from Demi Guard/Attacker|Lapel Clear from Demi Guard]]** → [[Open Guard/Top]] (Probability: gi only)




</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Offensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when attacking from Demi Guard?</p>

### 1. Driving into the intact lapel barrier

- ❌ **Consequence**: Your weight loads the guard player's elevation structure.
- ✅ **Correction**: Restore balance and address the foot and fabric first.

### 2. Pulling the controlled knee free by force

- ❌ **Consequence**: The remaining wrap can bind the leg during the movement.
- ✅ **Correction**: Loosen the fabric before moving the controlled leg.

### 3. Watching only the collar-gripping hand

- ❌ **Consequence**: The foot-on-lapel connection remains active below you.
- ✅ **Correction**: Track the collar, foot and knee-line connection together.

### 4. Treating a cleared lapel as a finished pass

- ❌ **Consequence**: The opponent's legs can still retain an ordinary guard.
- ✅ **Correction**: Check whether a leg trap or open-guard frame remains.

### 5. Reaching with both hands while losing base

- ❌ **Consequence**: The bottom player can redirect your unsupported weight.
- ✅ **Correction**: Keep a stable base throughout the grip-clearing exchange.



</section>

<section id="training-drills" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Drills for Attacks

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Demi Guard attacks?</p>

### Connection recognition

Begin in a cooperative Demi configuration. Name the collar grip, lapel path and loaded foot, then reset before attempting to clear any of them.

**Duration**: 2 minutes per role


### Clear and stop

Start with light lapel tension. Clear the connection while the partner retains ordinary leg frames; stop at open guard and check that neither knee is bound.

**Duration**: 3 minutes per role


### Pass or reset

Begin in Demi with light resistance. Attempt the clear and pass; reset on side control, a sweep, or a recovered guard. Stop immediately on discomfort.

**Duration**: Three 1-minute rounds per role
