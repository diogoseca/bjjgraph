<p class="often-confused-with"><em>Often confused with</em> <strong>[[Positions/Half Guard|Half Guard]]</strong> — Demi uses a collar grip and foot-on-lapel knee control; Half Guard traps one leg between both legs and also exists in no-gi.</p>

<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You face the passer from underneath with the lapel routed behind their knee, a collar grip controlling their upper body, and a foot pressing on the lapel tail. Keep your knees in front of your torso so the connection can support distance and elevation. Having the lapel in your hand alone does not establish Demi Guard.

Use the passer's pressure as the cue for [[Transitions/Demi Guard Elevation Sweep]]. If the fabric or foot connection stops working, recover [[Positions/Half Guard]] or return to [[Positions/Lapel Guard]] before chasing another attack. The latter has existing back-take, omoplata and triangle setups; those require a new position and their own controls. Do not treat the word Demi as another name for ordinary half guard.

</section>



<section id="state-invariants" class="content-section">

## Position Definition

<p class="section-subtitle">What is Demi Guard (Bottom)?</p>

- The bottom player faces the passer with bent knees between their torsos rather than allowing chest-to-chest side control.
- The passer's lapel runs behind the controlled knee and reaches the bottom player's foot, which presses on its tail.
- The bottom player maintains a collar grip and a working lapel connection so the foot can help manage pressure at the knee line.


</section>

<section id="prerequisites" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before playing Demi Guard (Bottom)?</p>

- The opponent wears a gi with an accessible lapel.
- You can keep your legs between the players while arranging the grips.
- There is space to route the lapel behind the knee and place the foot on its tail.


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Demi Guard?</p>

- Keep the collar and knee-line connections working together.
- Place the foot on the lapel tail rather than merely holding loose cloth.
- Keep bent legs between you and the passer.
- Read forward pressure before attempting elevation.
- Recover another guard promptly when the Demi connection is lost.


</section>

<section id="decision-tree" class="content-section">

## Decision Making from This Position

<p class="section-subtitle">What should you do from Demi Guard (Bottom)?</p>

**The passer's weight comes forward over your working lapel connection:**
- Execute **[[Transitions/Demi Guard Elevation Sweep/Attacker|Demi Guard Elevation Sweep]]** → [[Half Guard/Top]] (Probability: gi only)


**The passer retreats and you retain useful lapel control:**
- Execute **[[Transitions/Demi Guard to Lapel Guard/Attacker|Demi Guard to Lapel Guard]]** → [[Lapel Guard/Bottom]] (Probability: gi only)


**The foot-on-lapel control fails but one opponent's leg remains within reach:**
- Execute **[[Transitions/Demi Guard to Half Guard/Attacker|Demi Guard to Half Guard]]** → [[Half Guard/Bottom]] (Probability: gi only)




</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Demi Guard?</p>

### 1. Holding fabric without engaging the foot

- ❌ **Consequence**: The characteristic knee-line barrier is incomplete.
- ✅ **Correction**: Confirm the lapel path and foot contact before relying on Demi.

### 2. Letting both knees drift behind the passer

- ❌ **Consequence**: The passer can approach your torso without a leg barrier.
- ✅ **Correction**: Recover your legs in front before attempting another lapel feed.

### 3. Trying to lift a balanced opponent with the arms

- ❌ **Consequence**: The grips tire without creating a useful change of base.
- ✅ **Correction**: Wait for or create a visible weight shift before elevation.

### 4. Hanging onto a lost lapel while being passed

- ❌ **Consequence**: The grip occupies a hand needed for frames and recovery.
- ✅ **Correction**: Release the ineffective connection and recover a guard.

### 5. Keeping the lapel bound after coming on top

- ❌ **Consequence**: The same connection can obstruct your own stabilization.
- ✅ **Correction**: Release unnecessary fabric and establish the actual landing position.



</section>

<section id="training-drills" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Drills for Defense

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Demi Guard defense?</p>

### Build and identify

From seated open guard, arrange the collar, knee-line lapel and foot contact with a cooperative partner. Identify all three before releasing and resetting.

**Duration**: 2 minutes per role


### Pressure recognition

With light grips, let the passer alternate forward pressure and retreat. Name the sweep or recovery opportunity; reset without forcing a loaded knee.

**Duration**: 3 minutes per role


### Guard recovery exchange

Start in Demi and allow a gradual lapel clear. Recover half guard or another lapel configuration; stop and reset when the destination is stable or the passer clears both legs.

**Duration**: Three 1-minute rounds per role
