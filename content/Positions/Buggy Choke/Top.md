<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The top player is responsible for recognizing and defending the control. The top player is beside the bottom player in side-control geometry. The bottom player has turned partly toward the opponent and brought an arm past the head. That arm connects to the same-side raised leg, enclosing the top player's head and one arm. The controlling elbow stays near the knee so the loop does not depend on wrenching the foot.

Watch for an arm reaching past your head and connecting to the same-side leg. Use balanced posture and safe inside frames before the loop closes. Treat the choke as live even though you hold top side control.

Free the head-and-arm enclosure before changing to north-south or chasing another pin. Avoid lifting, dropping, or twisting the partner's trapped leg to force an escape. Stop before dizziness or loss of normal responsiveness; top position does not make a secured choke safe.

The bottom player supplies the arm-and-leg loop around the opponent's head and one arm. It does not require a collar grip, a turtle ride, or trapping the opponent's arm alone across their own throat. Protect both the defender's neck and the attacker's knee. Do not pull the foot against a fixed knee, use a slam, or remain attached when lifted. Open the whole loop on any stop signal.

</section>

## Position Definition

<p class="section-subtitle">What is Buggy Choke (Top)?</p>

- The top player is beside the bottom player in side-control geometry.
- The bottom player has turned partly toward the opponent and brought an arm past the head.
- That arm connects to the same-side raised leg, enclosing the top player's head and one arm.
- The controlling elbow stays near the knee so the loop does not depend on wrenching the foot.

## Key Offensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for attacking from Buggy Choke?</p>

- Recognize the loop early: Watch for an arm reaching past your head and connecting to the same-side leg.
- Prevent hand-leg connection: Use balanced posture and safe inside frames before the loop closes.
- Protect the neck: Treat the choke as live even though you hold top side control.
- Clear before circling: Free the head-and-arm enclosure before changing to north-south or chasing another pin.
- Do not slam or wrench: Avoid lifting, dropping, or twisting the partner's trapped leg to force an escape.
- Tap early: Stop before dizziness or loss of normal responsiveness; top position does not make a secured choke safe.
