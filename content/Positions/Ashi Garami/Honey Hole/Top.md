<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**Honey Hole Top** (also called Inside Sankaku, Saddle Top, or 411) is an advanced leg entanglement where you trap one of your opponent's legs between your own legs while facing them. This position provides direct access to **[[Transitions/Heel Hook]]**, **[[Transitions/Outside Heel Hook]]**, **[[Transitions/Kneebar Finish]]**, and **[[Transitions/Toe Hold]]**. Retention rate: 60%. Submission probability: 42%. Requires: ashi garami fundamentals, heel hook mechanics, leg entanglement hierarchy.

Honey Hole Top represents the culmination of modern leg lock development, refined through systematic study of leg entanglement mechanics by practitioners like John Danaher and Eddie Bravo. From this position, the top player maintains significant control advantages while threatening multiple submission paths simultaneously. The position is characterized by a figure-four leg configuration that isolates the opponent's leg for attack: your inside leg triangles around their trapped thigh, while your outside leg crosses over their hip, creating mechanical disadvantage for the defender.

This position excels in no-gi grappling and submission-only rulesets where leg attacks are permitted. It requires careful control and technical precision to maintain while setting up submissions. The risk level is medium because improper execution — particularly over-rotating before establishing hip control — can result in counter-entanglements or leg lock exposure for the attacker. The position's effectiveness stems from its ability to create mechanical disadvantage for the defender while establishing multiple submission threats from **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]]** and related positions.

<section id="state-invariants" class="content-section">

## What Is the Honey Hole Top Position?

- One opponent leg trapped in figure-four configuration between your legs, with your inside leg triangling around their thigh just above the knee while your ankle hooks behind their leg, creating an inescapable entanglement that prevents leg extraction
- Your outside leg crosses over opponent's hip at approximately mid-thigh level with your shin driving downward across their hip flexor, creating constant pressure that pins their hip to the mat and prevents rotational escapes
- Perpendicular body alignment maintained in T-shape relative to opponent, with your shoulders and upper body positioned at roughly 90 degrees to their torso, your chest facing toward their trapped leg while your head is positioned away from their free leg to avoid counters

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## How to Execute Inside Heel Hook from Honey Hole Top (Step by Step)

Follow these 10 steps to finish with the inside heel hook from **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Honey Hole]]**:

1. **Enter the Entanglement**: From **[[Positions/X-Guard/Single Leg X-Guard]]**, ashi garami, or a scramble, shoot your inside leg between opponent's legs. Your inside foot should thread through to hook behind the crease of their near knee.

2. **Lock the Inside Leg Triangle**: Close your inside leg triangle by pulling your ankle tightly behind their knee crease. This prevents linear leg extraction and is the foundation of the entire position.

3. **Apply Outside Leg Hip Pressure**: Drive your outside leg's shin across their near hip at mid-thigh level, creating constant downward pressure. This is your primary control — without it, opponent scoots away or rotates to escape.

4. **Establish Perpendicular Alignment**: Adjust your body to a T-shape (90 degrees to their torso). Your chest faces their trapped leg; your head points away from their free leg to prevent counter-entanglement.

5. **Control Upper Body**: Reach across and grip their near arm sleeve, shorts at the hip, or their far knee. This stops them from sitting up to create defensive frames and limits their rotation options.

6. **Confirm Heel Exposure**: Check that their toes are pointing away from you (heel exposed). If they point toes toward you to hide the heel, transition to **[[Transitions/Kneebar Finish]]** or **[[Transitions/Toe Hold]]** instead.

7. **Secure the Heel Grip**: Cup their exposed heel with your bottom hand — four fingers on top of the heel, thumb underneath. Your second hand grips their ankle or shin for dual control. Do not grip the heel until position is fully established.

8. **Engage Your Hips Forward**: Drive your hips forward and down to increase pressure on their trapped leg. This eliminates the space they need to rotate out while loading the knee joint for the finish.

9. **Apply Rotational Finishing Pressure**: Pull your elbows toward your own chest while rotating their heel outward and away from their body. The torque rotates their knee ligaments past their safe range. Do not pull upward — rotate horizontally.

10. **Complete or Transition**: Opponent taps → release fully and immediately. If they counter by pulling knee to chest → **[[Transitions/Kneebar Finish]]**. If they rotate toward you → enter **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]]** via **[[Transitions/Saddle Entry from Top]]**. If they turn away → take **[[Positions/Back Control]]**.

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## What Are Common Mistakes in Honey Hole Top?

### 1. Insufficient hip control allowing opponent to create space

- ❌ **Consequence**: Allows opponent to escape by creating distance and removing their leg from entanglement, potentially reversing position or establishing guard
- ✅ **Correction**: Keep your hips tight to opponent's hip, using your legs to clamp their trapped leg while maintaining constant downward pressure to eliminate escape routes. Your outside leg should drive across their hip creating a wedge that prevents rotation.

### 2. Over-rotating too early before establishing proper control

- ❌ **Consequence**: Exposes your own legs to counter-attacks and allows opponent to escape or reverse the entanglement into their own leg lock position
- ✅ **Correction**: Establish solid hip control first with proper leg configuration, then rotate gradually while maintaining pressure. Control precedes submission attempts.

### 3. Neglecting upper body control and focusing only on leg entanglement

- ❌ **Consequence**: Opponent can use their upper body mobility to frame, create space, and facilitate leg extraction even with good leg entanglement
- ✅ **Correction**: Secure grips on opponent's upper body (collar, sleeve, or body lock) to limit their ability to sit up or create frames. Upper body control complements leg entanglement.

### 4. Attacking heel hook with improper hand positioning before securing control

- ❌ **Consequence**: Telegraphs submission attempt and gives opponent time to defend by hiding heel or extracting leg. Also reduces control making escape easier
- ✅ **Correction**: Establish position completely first, then grip for submission. Hand should cup heel with four fingers on top and thumb underneath, wrist control with other hand. Only grip when ready to finish.

### 5. Failing to adjust when opponent rotates or shifts weight

- ❌ **Consequence**: Allows opponent to escape entanglement or reverse position. Static control in dynamic situation leads to lost position
- ✅ **Correction**: Constantly adjust your hip position and leg configuration to match opponent's movements. If they rotate, follow with your hips. If they create space, immediately close distance and retighten entanglement.

### 6. Crossing feet too tight on figure-four reducing mobility

- ❌ **Consequence**: Limits your ability to adjust position and follow opponent's movements. Creates static position that skilled opponents can escape
- ✅ **Correction**: Maintain figure-four with controlled tension - tight enough for control but loose enough to allow hip adjustments and transitions. Your feet should be engaged but not locked rigidly.

Partner starts in honey hole, you maintain position while they use 50% resistance to attempt escapes. Focus on hip pressure, leg configuration, and making constant adjustments. Start with 30-second rounds, progress to 2-minute rounds as control improves. Partner should attempt all standard escapes: hip rotation, leg extraction, inside position reversal.

**Duration**: 5 rounds of 2 minutes


### Entry to Control Progression

Start from various guard positions (**[[Positions/X-Guard]]**, **[[Positions/X-Guard/Single Leg X-Guard]]**, **[[Positions/Ashi Garami]]**) and practice entering honey hole with proper leg configuration and hip control. Do 10 repetitions from each starting position, focusing on smooth transitions and immediate control establishment. Partner offers 25% resistance initially, increasing to 75% as technique improves.

**Duration**: 15-20 minutes


### Submission Chain Drill

From established honey hole, flow between outside heel hook, inside heel hook, **[[Transitions/Kneebar Finish]]**, and **[[Transitions/Toe Hold]]** without releasing position. Practice transitioning between submissions based on opponent's defensive reactions. 5-minute rounds focusing on maintaining control while threatening multiple attacks. This builds submission awareness and control endurance.

**Duration**: 3-5 rounds of 5 minutes


### Escape Defense Sequence

Partner in bottom position attempts one specific escape technique at 75% intensity. Your goal is to maintain honey hole and counter their escape attempt. Cycle through all major escapes (hip rotation, leg extraction, inside reversal, rolling to turtle) with 1-minute rounds each. Focus on recognizing escape patterns early and countering before they gain momentum.

**Duration**: 10-15 minutes

## Test Your Honey Hole Top Knowledge

**Q1**: What hip adjustment should you make when your opponent begins rotating their body toward you?
**A**: Follow their rotation by driving your hips forward and down, maintaining perpendicular alignment while increasing pressure through your outside leg across their hip. Your hips must move with them to prevent them from creating the angle needed to extract their leg. Simultaneously tighten your inside leg triangle to prevent any slack that rotation could exploit.

**Q2**: What are the three essential control points for maintaining Honey Hole position?
**A**: The three essential control points are: (1) Inside leg triangle around opponent's thigh controlling hip rotation, (2) Outside leg crossing their hip with shin pressure pinning them down, and (3) Upper body control through grips on their far leg, belt/shorts, or arm to prevent them from sitting up and creating frames. All three must work together for dominant control.

**Q3**: How do you shut down the primary hip rotation escape from Honey Hole top?
**A**: Anticipate the rotation by maintaining constant downward hip pressure through your outside leg, which acts as a wedge against their hip. When you feel them load up to rotate, drive your hips forward and slightly toward their centerline, collapsing any space they need to turn. Keep your inside leg triangle tight and your body perpendicular - rotation becomes impossible when your weight pins their hip flat.

**Q4**: What grip priorities should you establish before attempting heel hook from Honey Hole?
**A**: First priority is controlling opponent's far leg or hip to prevent rotation and boot scooting. Second is securing their upper body to stop them from sitting up. Only then grip the heel - cup the heel with fingers on top, thumb underneath, with your other hand controlling their ankle or wrist. Gripping heel before securing control telegraphs the attack and gives them time to defend.

**Q5**: How do you apply pressure correctly in Honey Hole to drain opponent's energy while maintaining position?
**A**: Apply pressure through your outside leg driving across their hip while keeping your hips tight against their trapped leg. Your body weight should flow through your hips and legs, not through pushing with arms. Maintain steady, crushing pressure rather than explosive bursts - this forces them to work constantly against your weight while you conserve energy. The triangle configuration does the work; you just maintain tension.

**Q6**: Your opponent suddenly bridges and tries to invert toward your legs - what immediate adjustment do you make?
**A**: Immediately drive your outside leg deeper across their hip while posting your far hand on the mat for base. Shift your hips slightly away from their inversion direction to prevent them from rolling underneath you. If they commit heavily to the inversion, consider transitioning to their back as they turn, or tighten your triangle and follow their movement while maintaining heel access. Never let them complete the inversion without immediate positional adjustment.

**Q7**: How do you manage your energy when opponent is actively defending for extended periods?
**A**: Let the position do the work - your triangle and hip pressure require minimal effort to maintain once properly established. Focus on steady, consistent pressure rather than explosive adjustments that drain your cardio. When they rest, you rest. When they move, make small efficient adjustments rather than large corrections. Attack submissions only when openings appear naturally from their defensive errors, not through forced attempts that waste energy.

**Q8**: After opponent partially extracts their leg but you maintain triangle control, how do you recover full position?
**A**: Immediately drive your hips forward to close the space they created, using your outside leg to push their hip back down. Re-tighten your inside leg triangle by pulling your ankle deeper behind their leg. Use your far hand to grip their pants/shorts at the knee and pull their leg back into the entanglement. Re-establish perpendicular alignment and hip pressure before they can create more space. Speed is critical - partial escapes must be recovered immediately.

<section id="faq" class="content-section">

## Frequently Asked Questions About Honey Hole Top

### Is the Honey Hole position legal in BJJ competitions?

Honey Hole itself (the positional control) is legal in all BJJ rulesets. The inside heel hook — the primary submission from this position — is restricted by belt level and ruleset. Under IBJJF rules, inside heel hooks are only permitted at brown and black belt in no-gi. Submission-only formats (EBI, Polaris, ADCC) generally allow inside heel hooks regardless of belt level. The outside heel hook is even more restricted — typically only allowed at the highest no-gi levels. Always verify the specific ruleset before competing.

### What is the difference between Honey Hole and Saddle in BJJ?

**[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Honey Hole]]** and **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]]** describe the same leg entanglement — different communities use different names for identical mechanics. The figure-four configuration, hip pressure requirements, and available submissions are identical. "Honey Hole" is common in 10th Planet Jiu-Jitsu and the Eddie Bravo system. "Saddle" is used in John Danaher's leg lock curriculum. "Inside Sankaku" and "411" are other names for the same position.

### How do I enter Honey Hole from guard or standing?

The most common entry sequences are:
- From **[[Positions/X-Guard/Single Leg X-Guard]]**: elevate opponent's leg and thread your inside leg through to establish the entanglement
- From **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]]**: use **[[Transitions/Inside Ashi-Garami to Honey Hole]]** by adding the outside leg cross
- From **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/50-50 Guard]]**: use inside position recovery to switch from symmetric to asymmetric entanglement
- From passing guard: when opponent turtles or scrambles, thread legs to establish inside position

### Why is the inside heel hook from Honey Hole so high percentage?

The inside heel hook from Honey Hole is high percentage because the figure-four configuration isolates the knee joint mechanically while simultaneously controlling the hip. Unlike outside heel hooks where rotation is the primary escape, the inside position removes the defender's ability to rotate in the "safe" direction. The knee ligaments (ACL, MCL, LCL) are stretched rapidly under the twisting force, leaving minimal reaction time between "uncomfortable" and "injured" — which is why this submission demands immediate tapping from training partners.

### What do I do when my opponent hides their heel?

When opponent points their toes toward you to hide the heel, three options exist: (1) Transition to **[[Transitions/Kneebar Finish]]** — pull their knee toward your chest while extending their leg, (2) Attack **[[Transitions/Toe Hold]]** by gripping the top of their foot and rotating, (3) Use **[[Transitions/Saddle Entry from Top]]** to reconfigure into a tighter entanglement that re-exposes the heel. Never force a heel hook grip when the heel is hidden — this wastes energy and telegraphs your attack.

### How do I prevent my opponent from counter-entangling to 50-50?

Opponent can counter to **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/50-50 Guard]]** by threading their free leg into your legs and establishing symmetric entanglement. Prevent this by: (1) Keeping your outside leg tightly across their hip so their free leg cannot hook yours, (2) Maintaining constant hip pressure that limits their free leg mobility, (3) Attacking submissions before they can establish the counter — once you grip the heel, their priority shifts to tapping rather than entangling.

### When should I transition from heel hook to kneebar from Honey Hole?

Transition to **[[Transitions/Kneebar Finish]]** when: (1) Opponent pulls their knee toward their chest to hide the heel, (2) Opponent creates enough space that the heel hook angle is lost, (3) You want to threaten a different submission path to create a dilemma. The kneebar from Honey Hole is a secondary threat that forces opponents to choose which defense to prioritize — defending heel hook exposes the knee to bar, and defending kneebar exposes the heel.

### How is Honey Hole different from regular outside ashi garami?

**[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Outside Ashi-Garami]]** uses only one leg to control the opponent's leg with an outside position — mechanically weaker and easier to extract from. Honey Hole uses both legs in a figure-four configuration that is mechanically superior: the inside triangle prevents extraction while the outside leg cross pins the hip. Honey Hole also provides access to the inside heel hook (higher percentage than outside heel hook) and enables cleaner transitions to **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]]** and back control.
