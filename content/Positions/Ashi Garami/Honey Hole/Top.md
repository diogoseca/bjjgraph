## What Is the Honey Hole Position in BJJ? (Inside Sankaku / 411)

The **Honey Hole** (also called **Inside Sankaku** or **411**) is an advanced leg entanglement where the top player traps one of the opponent's legs in a figure-four configuration between their own legs while facing them. All three names — Honey Hole, Inside Sankaku, and 411 — refer to the identical position. Submission probability: 42% inside heel hook, 14% kneebar, 12% outside heel hook. Requires: understanding of the **[[Positions/Ashi Garami]]** system and leg lock safety protocol.

This position is one of the most dangerous and effective in modern no-gi BJJ, providing simultaneous access to the **[[Transitions/Inside Heel Hook]]**, **[[Transitions/Kneebar]]**, and **[[Transitions/Toe Hold]]** while severely limiting the defender's escape options. The figure-four leg configuration creates mechanical disadvantage for the defender, making position maintenance far easier than in basic [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]].

The Honey Hole sits at the apex of the **[[Positions/Ashi Garami]]** system: basic ashi garami builds the framework, inside ashi-garami develops the approach, and the honey hole (with the [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]] as its advanced refinement) provides the controlling platform for inside leg lock attacks. It excels in no-gi grappling and submission-only rulesets where leg attacks are fully permitted.

</section>

<section id="state-invariants" class="content-section">

## How Is the Honey Hole Defined? (Leg Configuration Details)

- One opponent leg trapped in figure-four configuration between your legs, with your inside leg triangling around their thigh just above the knee while your ankle hooks behind their leg, creating an inescapable entanglement that prevents leg extraction
- Your outside leg crosses over opponent's hip at approximately mid-thigh level with your shin driving downward across their hip flexor, creating constant pressure that pins their hip to the mat and prevents rotational escapes
- Perpendicular body alignment maintained in T-shape relative to opponent, with your shoulders and upper body positioned at roughly 90 degrees to their torso, your chest facing toward their trapped leg while your head is positioned away from their free leg to avoid counters

<section id="legality" class="content-section">

## Is Honey Hole Legal in BJJ Competition?

The **Honey Hole position itself is legal** in all major BJJ rulesets. However, the submissions it enables have different restrictions depending on the ruleset and belt level:

| Ruleset | Honey Hole Position | Inside Heel Hook | Outside Heel Hook | Kneebar |
|---------|--------------------|-----------------|--------------------|---------|
| **IBJJF Gi** | Legal | Prohibited all belts | Prohibited all belts | Brown/Black only |
| **IBJJF No-Gi** | Legal | Brown/Black only | Brown/Black only | Brown/Black only |
| **ADCC** | Legal | Legal | Legal | Legal |
| **Submission Only** | Legal | Legal (typically) | Legal (typically) | Legal (typically) |
| **EBI / No-Gi Worlds** | Legal | Legal | Legal | Legal |

**Key rule:** At white, blue, and purple belt IBJJF competition, entering honey hole is permitted but you cannot finish with an inside heel hook. Use kneebar (brown/black only) or toe hold (varies by federation) as alternatives, or use the position to pursue a back take.

At higher levels and in submission-only formats, the honey hole's full submission arsenal is available.

</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## How Do You Attack from Honey Hole? (Offensive Principles)

- **Leg Configuration Control**: Maintain proper figure-four entanglement with inside leg triangling and outside leg crossing hip — this is the foundation of every attack
- **Hip Pressure**: Apply constant downward hip pressure to pin opponent and prevent rotation — your weight flows through your hips, not your arms
- **Distance Management**: Control space between bodies to facilitate submissions while preventing counter-attacks and leg extractions
- **Upper Body Control**: Secure opponent's upper body with grips or frames to limit defensive reactions and prevent them from sitting up
- **Submission Progression**: Build attacks systematically from control to breaking mechanics to finish — never rush a heel hook
- **Escape Prevention**: Anticipate common escape attempts (hip rotation, leg extraction) and make constant positional adjustments to maintain control
- **Positional Patience**: Maintain control under pressure without rushing submissions — let position create submission opportunities naturally

<section id="entry-sequences" class="content-section">

## How Do You Enter the Honey Hole?

Common entry sequences into honey hole:

1. **From [[Positions/X-Guard/Single Leg X-Guard]]**: Rotate your hips toward the opponent's trapped leg while maintaining leg entanglement, transitioning from outside-facing to inside-facing position
2. **From [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]]**: Advance leg configuration by bringing your outside leg over the opponent's hip while maintaining the inside leg triangle
3. **From [[Positions/X-Guard]]**: Use the X-guard elevation to create angle, then rotate into inside-facing position while maintaining leg control
4. **From [[Positions/Ashi Garami/50-50 Guard]]**: Directional transition through leg configuration adjustment when opponent presents inside position
5. **From top**: When opponent is on their back, insert your inside leg under their thigh and swing your outside leg over their hip to establish the figure-four

All entries require understanding the **[[Positions/Ashi Garami]]** hierarchy — the honey hole is not typically entered from neutral; it is advanced from existing leg entanglement control.

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Why Do People Lose the Honey Hole Position? (Common Mistakes)

### 1. Insufficient hip control allowing opponent to create space

- ❌ **Consequence**: Allows opponent to escape by creating distance and removing their leg from entanglement, potentially reversing position or establishing guard
- ✅ **Correction**: Keep your hips tight to opponent's hip, using your legs to clamp their trapped leg while maintaining constant downward pressure to eliminate escape routes. Your outside leg should drive across their hip creating a wedge that prevents rotation.

### 2. Over-rotating too early before establishing proper control

- ❌ **Consequence**: Exposes your own legs to counter-attacks and allows opponent to escape or reverse the entanglement into their own leg lock position
- ✅ **Correction**: Establish solid hip control first with proper leg configuration, then rotate gradually while maintaining pressure. Control precedes submission attempts.

### 3. Neglecting upper body control and focusing only on leg entanglement

- ❌ **Consequence**: Opponent can use their upper body mobility to frame, create space, and facilitate leg extraction even with good leg entanglement
- ✅ **Correction**: Secure grips on opponent's upper body (collar, sleeve, or body lock) to limit their ability to sit up or create frames. Upper body control complements leg entanglement.

### 4. Attacking heel hook with improper hand positioning before securing control

- ❌ **Consequence**: Telegraphs submission attempt and gives opponent time to defend by hiding heel or extracting leg. Also reduces control making escape easier
- ✅ **Correction**: Establish position completely first, then grip for submission. Hand should cup heel with four fingers on top and thumb underneath, wrist control with other hand. Only grip when ready to finish.

### 5. Failing to adjust when opponent rotates or shifts weight

- ❌ **Consequence**: Allows opponent to escape entanglement or reverse position. Static control in dynamic situation leads to lost position
- ✅ **Correction**: Constantly adjust your hip position and leg configuration to match opponent's movements. If they rotate, follow with your hips. If they create space, immediately close distance and retighten entanglement.

### 6. Crossing feet too tight on figure-four reducing mobility

- ❌ **Consequence**: Limits your ability to adjust position and follow opponent's movements. Creates static position that skilled opponents can escape
- ✅ **Correction**: Maintain figure-four with controlled tension — tight enough for control but loose enough to allow hip adjustments and transitions. Your feet should be engaged but not locked rigidly.



</section>

<section id="safety" class="content-section">

## Safety Notice — Injury Risks from Honey Hole

The Honey Hole is a high-risk position for the **defender**. Inside heel hooks applied from this position can cause rapid, severe knee injuries with minimal warning.

**Injuries possible from inside heel hook:**
- ACL (anterior cruciate ligament) tear
- MCL (medial collateral ligament) damage
- Popliteal ligament injury
- Combined ligament damage with minimal force

**Critical safety rules for training:**

1. **Tap early** — do not wait to feel pain; heel hook damage occurs before pain is perceived
2. **Communication** — always establish tap protocols with training partners before drilling
3. **Slow finishing** — in training, apply heel hook pressure slowly and pause at any resistance
4. **Respect taps** — release immediately and completely upon any tap signal
5. **Skill-appropriate drilling** — do not drill inside heel hooks with partners who do not yet understand tapping protocol

For competition, always verify ruleset heel hook permissions before entering honey hole with finish intent. See the IBJJF and ADCC competition legality section above.

</section>

## Training Drills for Attacks

### Entanglement Retention Drill

Partner starts in honey hole, you maintain position while they use 50% resistance to attempt escapes. Focus on hip pressure, leg configuration, and making constant adjustments. Start with 30-second rounds, progress to 2-minute rounds as control improves. Partner should attempt all standard escapes: hip rotation, leg extraction, inside position reversal.

**Duration**: 5 rounds of 2 minutes


### Entry to Control Progression

Start from various guard positions ([[Positions/X-Guard]], [[Positions/X-Guard/Single Leg X-Guard]], [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]]) and practice entering honey hole with proper leg configuration and hip control. Do 10 repetitions from each starting position, focusing on smooth transitions and immediate control establishment. Partner offers 25% resistance initially, increasing to 75% as technique improves.

**Duration**: 15-20 minutes


### Submission Chain Drill

From established honey hole, flow between outside heel hook, inside heel hook, kneebar, and toe hold without releasing position. Practice transitioning between submissions based on opponent's defensive reactions. 5-minute rounds focusing on maintaining control while threatening multiple attacks. This builds submission awareness and control endurance.

**Duration**: 3-5 rounds of 5 minutes


### Escape Defense Sequence

Partner in bottom position attempts one specific escape technique at 75% intensity. Your goal is to maintain honey hole and counter their escape attempt. Cycle through all major escapes (hip rotation, leg extraction, inside reversal, rolling to turtle) with 1-minute rounds each. Focus on recognizing escape patterns early and countering before they gain momentum.

**Duration**: 10-15 minutes

## Test Your Knowledge

**Q1**: What hip adjustment should you make when your opponent begins rotating their body toward you?
**A**: Follow their rotation by driving your hips forward and down, maintaining perpendicular alignment while increasing pressure through your outside leg across their hip. Your hips must move with them to prevent them from creating the angle needed to extract their leg. Simultaneously tighten your inside leg triangle to prevent any slack that rotation could exploit.

**Q2**: What are the three essential control points for maintaining Honey Hole position?
**A**: The three essential control points are: (1) Inside leg triangle around opponent's thigh controlling hip rotation, (2) Outside leg crossing their hip with shin pressure pinning them down, and (3) Upper body control through grips on their far leg, belt/shorts, or arm to prevent them from sitting up and creating frames. All three must work together for dominant control.

**Q3**: How do you shut down the primary hip rotation escape from Honey Hole top?
**A**: Anticipate the rotation by maintaining constant downward hip pressure through your outside leg, which acts as a wedge against their hip. When you feel them load up to rotate, drive your hips forward and slightly toward their centerline, collapsing any space they need to turn. Keep your inside leg triangle tight and your body perpendicular — rotation becomes impossible when your weight pins their hip flat.

**Q4**: What grip priorities should you establish before attempting heel hook from Honey Hole?
**A**: First priority is controlling opponent's far leg or hip to prevent rotation and boot scooting. Second is securing their upper body to stop them from sitting up. Only then grip the heel — cup the heel with fingers on top, thumb underneath, with your other hand controlling their ankle or wrist. Gripping heel before securing control telegraphs the attack and gives them time to defend.

**Q5**: How do you apply pressure correctly in Honey Hole to drain opponent's energy while maintaining position?
**A**: Apply pressure through your outside leg driving across their hip while keeping your hips tight against their trapped leg. Your body weight should flow through your hips and legs, not through pushing with arms. Maintain steady, crushing pressure rather than explosive bursts — this forces them to work constantly against your weight while you conserve energy. The triangle configuration does the work; you just maintain tension.

**Q6**: Your opponent suddenly bridges and tries to invert toward your legs — what immediate adjustment do you make?
**A**: Immediately drive your outside leg deeper across their hip while posting your far hand on the mat for base. Shift your hips slightly away from their inversion direction to prevent them from rolling underneath you. If they commit heavily to the inversion, consider transitioning to their back as they turn, or tighten your triangle and follow their movement while maintaining heel access. Never let them complete the inversion without immediate positional adjustment.

**Q7**: How do you manage your energy when opponent is actively defending for extended periods?
**A**: Let the position do the work — your triangle and hip pressure require minimal effort to maintain once properly established. Focus on steady, consistent pressure rather than explosive adjustments that drain your cardio. When they rest, you rest. When they move, make small efficient adjustments rather than large corrections. Attack submissions only when openings appear naturally from their defensive errors, not through forced attempts that waste energy.

**Q8**: After opponent partially extracts their leg but you maintain triangle control, how do you recover full position?
**A**: Immediately drive your hips forward to close the space they created, using your outside leg to push their hip back down. Re-tighten your inside leg triangle by pulling your ankle deeper behind their leg. Use your far hand to grip their pants/shorts at the knee and pull their leg back into the entanglement. Re-establish perpendicular alignment and hip pressure before they can create more space. Speed is critical — partial escapes must be recovered immediately.

## Honey Hole vs. Saddle — What's the Difference?

Both the **Honey Hole** and the **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]]** are inside heel hook positions within the **[[Positions/Ashi Garami]]** system, but they differ in control tightness and submission mechanics:

| Feature | Honey Hole | [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle\|Saddle]] |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| Leg configuration | Figure-four with inside leg triangle | Tighter wrap — legs more fully encircle hip |
| Inside heel hook access | Direct | Enhanced — tighter hip isolation |
| Control tightness | High | Higher — more complete hip capture |
| Entry difficulty | Moderate | Higher — requires precise advancement |
| Escape difficulty for defender | Hard | Harder |
| Submission variety | Heel hook, kneebar, toe hold | Same — with tighter mechanics |

**Summary**: The honey hole is the primary control and attack position. The **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]]** is an advanced refinement that offers tighter control and more leveraged inside heel hook finishing mechanics. Advancing from honey hole to saddle is a common progression when the defender partially defends or you need tighter control for the finish.

</section>

## What Will Defenders Try to Escape?

Understanding escape attempts lets you anticipate and counter them before they develop:

1. **Hip rotation toward you**: Defender rotates their hips to create angle for leg extraction
   - Counter: Drive outside leg deeper across their hip; advance to [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]] through the rotation

2. **Boot scoot away**: Defender uses their free leg to push and scoot their hips back
   - Counter: Maintain hip-to-hip connection by scooting with them; use your arms to grip their leg or hip

3. **Invert toward your legs**: Defender attempts a forward roll to counter-entangle
   - Counter: Follow the inversion toward [[Positions/Back Control]], or tighten triangle and post your hand to prevent them completing the roll

4. **Hide the heel**: Defender tucks their foot deep into your armpit to prevent heel hook grip
   - Counter: Transition to [[Transitions/Kneebar]] — hip extension attacks the straight leg directly

5. **Leg extraction by pulling knee to chest**: Defender bends their knee toward their own chest
   - Counter: Advance immediately to [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]] using their knee movement as the entry point

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the honey hole position in BJJ?

The **Honey Hole** (also called **Inside Sankaku** or **411**) is an advanced leg entanglement within the **[[Positions/Ashi Garami]]** system where the top player traps one opponent leg in a figure-four configuration between their own legs while facing the opponent. It provides direct access to **[[Transitions/Inside Heel Hook]]**, **[[Transitions/Kneebar]]**, and **[[Transitions/Toe Hold]]**, making it one of the most submission-rich positions in modern no-gi BJJ.

### Is honey hole legal in BJJ competition?

The honey hole **position** is legal in all major rulesets. The inside heel hook it enables is restricted in IBJJF competition to brown/black belt no-gi only. At lower IBJJF belt levels, use the position for kneebar (check belt-level rules), toe hold, or back take entries instead. In ADCC, submission-only, and EBI formats, all honey hole submissions are generally permitted.

### What is the difference between honey hole and saddle?

Both are inside heel hook positions. The **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]]** is a tighter refinement of honey hole where the attacker's legs wrap more completely around the defender's hip, creating enhanced control and inside heel hook leverage. The honey hole is the primary attack platform; the saddle is an advanced progression for tighter finishing mechanics.

### What is the 411 or inside sankaku position?

**411** and **Inside Sankaku** are alternate names for the honey hole — all three terms refer to the identical leg entanglement. Inside Sankaku is the Japanese BJJ term ("sankaku" means triangle, describing the leg configuration). 411 was popularized in submission grappling circuits. Use any name depending on your training context.

### How dangerous is honey hole for the defender?

Honey hole is high-risk because inside heel hooks cause rapid, severe knee injuries (ACL, MCL, popliteal ligament) with little warning. The attacker can reach finishing mechanics faster than pain signals travel. Defenders must tap early — never wait to feel pain. Attackers must release immediately upon any tap and apply pressure slowly in training.

### What submissions are available from honey hole?

Primary: **[[Transitions/Inside Heel Hook]]** (42%), [[Transitions/Back Take from Ashi]] to [[Positions/Back Control]] (14%), **[[Transitions/Kneebar]]** (14%), [[Transitions/Outside Heel Hook]] (12%), [[Transitions/Toe Hold]] (8%), [[Transitions/Estima Lock]] (3%). The submission chain means defending one attack typically opens the next.

### How do you enter the honey hole position?

Most common entries: from **[[Positions/X-Guard/Single Leg X-Guard]]** by rotating into inside-facing position, from **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]]** by advancing the leg configuration over the hip, and from **[[Positions/X-Guard]]** using the elevation to create entry angle. All entries require existing leg entanglement — honey hole is advanced from position, not entered from neutral.
