<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**Saddle Top** (also called **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Honey Hole|Honey Hole]]**, Inside Sankaku, or 4/11) is the highest-level leg entanglement position in modern BJJ, offering dominant mechanical control and a self-defending submission system. From this position you can finish with **[[Submissions/Inside Heel Hook|inside heel hook]]**, **[[Transitions/Outside Heel Hook|outside heel hook]]**, **[[Submissions/Straight Ankle Lock|straight ankle lock]]**, **[[Submissions/Toe Hold|toe hold]]**, and **[[Transitions/Kneebar Finish|kneebar]]**. Submission probability from established Saddle Top: 70%. Difficulty level: Advanced. Ruleset: inside heel hooks legal in no-gi (IBJJF brown/black belt and most submission-only events).

Saddle Top represents the culmination of modern leg lock development, operating through structural control of the opponent's leg using your entire body as a lever system. This creates mechanical advantage so significant that size and strength differences become largely irrelevant when the position is properly established. Unlike traditional top positions that rely on weight distribution, **[[Positions/Leg Entanglement|Saddle]]** operates through biomechanical joint isolation targeting the hip, knee, and ankle simultaneously.

The offensive power of Saddle Top stems from its self-defending submission system: when you attack **[[Submissions/Inside Heel Hook|inside heel hook]]** and the opponent hides their heel, they expose the ankle to **[[Submissions/Straight Ankle Lock|straight ankle lock]]**. When they defend the footlock by pulling toes back, they re-expose the heel. Every defensive action opens a new attacking opportunity — making the **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle|Saddle]]** dramatically more effective than isolated attacks from **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Outside Ashi-Garami|Outside Ashi-Garami]]** or **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami|Inside Ashi-Garami]]** alone.

The highest-percentage entries into Saddle Top come from **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Outside Ashi-Garami|Outside Ashi-Garami]]** via the back step, from **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami|Inside Ashi-Garami]]** through hip switches, from **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/50-50 Guard|50-50 Guard]]** through controlled elevations, and from **[[Positions/Single Leg X-Guard|Single Leg X-Guard]]** through specific angle changes. Contemporary approaches emphasize what instructors call "the hierarchy of controls" — achieving optimal perpendicular positioning, establishing specific grips in sequence, maintaining hip pressure, and clearing defensive frames before attempting finishes.

From a strategic perspective, Saddle Top functions differently depending on ruleset. In submission-only and no-gi competitions where heel hooks are legal, it becomes a primary attacking platform worthy of significant positional investment. In IBJJF gi competition, Saddle has limited utility and practitioners must use **[[Submissions/Straight Ankle Lock|straight ankle lock]]** or **[[Submissions/Toe Hold|toe hold]]** as primary finishes.

<section id="state-invariants" class="content-section">

## What Is Saddle Top in BJJ?

- You are positioned **perpendicular** to the opponent's body with their leg entangled between your legs, creating structural control of their hip, knee, and ankle joints through a figure-four or inside leg triangle configuration
- Your hips are pressuring into the opponent's trapped leg while maintaining **inside position** between their legs, preventing them from facing you directly or turning away to relieve pressure on vulnerable joints
- The opponent's heel is exposed and oriented toward your chest or armpit, enabling finishing mechanics for **[[Submissions/Inside Heel Hook|inside heel hook]]** while your grips control the foot and ankle to prevent defensive hiding or rotation


</section>

<section id="prerequisites" class="content-section">

## What Do You Need Before Using Saddle Top?

- You have achieved inside position between the opponent's legs (inside leg triangle established)
- You have established perpendicular alignment to the opponent's body — your torso at roughly 90 degrees to theirs
- You have controlled one of the opponent's legs with your leg entanglement from **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Outside Ashi-Garami|Outside Ashi-Garami]]**, **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami|Inside Ashi-Garami]]**, or **[[Positions/Single Leg X-Guard|Single Leg X-Guard]]**
- You have cleared or prevented the opponent's defensive frames from their hips and free leg


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## How Do You Control Saddle Top Effectively?

- **Perpendicular positioning** creates maximum mechanical advantage on the leg — your body at 90 degrees to theirs is non-negotiable
- **Hip pressure** prevents opponent rotation and maintains positional dominance — drive your outside shin across their hip like a wedge
- **Inside position is non-negotiable** — losing it collapses the entire control structure and allows the opponent to re-enter guard
- **Control before submission** — establish optimal leg configuration and hip pressure before gripping for heel hook; rushing the finish telegraphs intent
- **Systematic entries beat scramble-based entries** — learning back step from **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Outside Ashi-Garami|Outside Ashi-Garami]]** creates higher-percentage Saddle access than opportunistic scrambles
- **Opponent's defensive reactions should tighten your control** — every escape attempt they make should open a new submission pathway
- **Grip sequencing matters** — structural grips (ankle, belt, pants) come before finishing grips (heel cup)


</section>

## What Are Your Options from Saddle Top?

**You have achieved perpendicular positioning with inside control but opponent is framing on your hips:**
- Clear frames methodically, drive your outside shin across their hip → [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]] (Probability: 70%)
- If frames prevent advancement, step back to **[[Transitions/Inside Ashi Entry/Attacker|Inside Ashi Entry]]** → [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]] (Probability: 30%)


**Opponent's heel is fully exposed and you have established figure-four grip:**
- Execute **[[Transitions/Heel Hook/Attacker|Heel Hook]]** → [[Positions/Game Over]] (Probability: 80%)
- Execute **[[Transitions/Outside Heel Hook/Attacker|Outside Heel Hook]]** → [[Positions/Game Over]] (Probability: 20%)


**Opponent is hiding heel by rotating knee inward:**
- Execute **[[Transitions/Straight Ankle Lock Entry/Attacker|Straight Ankle Lock Entry]]** → [[Positions/Game Over]] (Probability: 60%)
- Execute **[[Transitions/Toe Hold/Attacker|Toe Hold]]** → [[Positions/Game Over]] (Probability: 40%)


**Opponent is attempting to turn into you to escape:**
- Ride the rotation while driving your outside leg across their hip to stay perpendicular → [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]] (Probability: 55%)
- Follow the rotation and take their back: **[[Transitions/Back Take from Ashi/Attacker|Back Take from Ashi]]** → [[Positions/Back Control]] (Probability: 45%)


**You have loose control with space between your bodies:**
- Re-drive hips into their trapped leg and re-establish perpendicular alignment → [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]] (Probability: 50%)
- Execute **[[Transitions/Cross Ashi Transition/Attacker|Cross Ashi Transition]]** → [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Cross Ashi-Garami]] (Probability: 35%)
- Step out to **[[Transitions/Counter-entangle to 50-50 Guard/Attacker|50-50 Guard]]** → [[Positions/Ashi Garami/50-50 Guard]] (Probability: 15%)

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid in Saddle Top?

### 1. Attempting to finish heel hook before establishing optimal control position

- ❌ **Consequence**: Opponent escapes by creating space or rotating away during your attack
- ✅ **Correction**: Follow systematic hierarchy — perpendicular alignment, hip pressure, inside position, grip establishment, clearing frames, then finish

### 2. Losing inside position by allowing opponent to turn into you

- ❌ **Consequence**: Entire position collapses as opponent achieves bilateral leg entanglement or guard recovery
- ✅ **Correction**: Maintain hip pressure and use your top leg to block opponent's hip rotation toward you

### 3. Gripping the heel immediately without establishing structural control

- ❌ **Consequence**: Telegraphs your intention and allows opponent to defend heel preemptively
- ✅ **Correction**: Establish positional grips first (ankle, pants, belt) then transition to heel grip only when ready to finish

### 4. Allowing space to develop between your body and opponent's trapped leg

- ❌ **Consequence**: Opponent uses space to create frames, rotate their body, or extract their leg
- ✅ **Correction**: Maintain constant connection with chest-to-thigh pressure while keeping your shoulder heavy on their leg

### 5. Using excessive force on submission attempts before control is optimized

- ❌ **Consequence**: Opponent escapes during your explosive effort or suffers injury from uncontrolled submission
- ✅ **Correction**: Apply submissions slowly and progressively, maintaining position throughout the finishing sequence

## How Do You Train Saddle Top?

### Saddle Entry Progression Drill

Start from **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Outside Ashi-Garami|Outside Ashi-Garami]]**, **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami|Inside Ashi-Garami]]**, **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/50-50 Guard|50-50 Guard]]**, and **[[Positions/Single Leg X-Guard|Single Leg X-Guard]]**. Practice systematic entries to Saddle with partner providing graduated resistance (none, light, moderate, full).

**Duration**: 5 minutes


### Control Hierarchy Drill

Partner gives you loose Saddle position. Systematically establish: perpendicular alignment, hip pressure, inside position, structural grips, frame clearing. Reset between each control level to reinforce the hierarchy.

**Duration**: 4 minutes


### Submission Chain Flow

Start with optimal Saddle control. Partner defends heel — attack **[[Submissions/Straight Ankle Lock|straight ankle lock]]**. Partner defends ankle — reattack heel. Flow between **[[Submissions/Inside Heel Hook|inside heel hook]]**, **[[Submissions/Straight Ankle Lock|straight ankle lock]]**, and **[[Submissions/Toe Hold|toe hold]]** based on opponent's defensive reactions.

**Duration**: 3 minutes


### Position Retention Against Escapes

Establish Saddle, partner executes specific escape sequences (hip clear, frame creation, leg extraction). Maintain position by adjusting grips, hip pressure, and alignment in real-time. When opponent turns into you, practice transitioning to **[[Positions/Back Control|Back Control]]**.

**Duration**: 5 minutes

<section id="knowledge-assessment" class="content-section">

## Test Your Knowledge: Saddle Top

**Q1**: Your opponent starts pushing your hips with their free leg while you have saddle control — what adjustment do you make?
**A**: Increase hip pressure into their trapped leg while using your top leg to pin or control their free leg. If they create significant space with their push, you may need to momentarily release some control to address the free leg, either by hooking it or by transitioning to a position where their pushing leg cannot generate effective force. The key is preventing them from using that free leg to create rotation angles.

**Q2**: What are the essential grips for maintaining saddle position before attempting submissions?
**A**: The primary structural grips include controlling the opponent's ankle with your outside arm to prevent them from rotating their knee, using your inside arm to frame on their hip or control their pants/belt, and maintaining leg configuration pressure. These positional grips differ from finishing grips — establish control grips first, only transition to heel control when ready to submit.

**Q3**: How do you shut down an opponent's primary escape of turning into you?
**A**: Maintain heavy hip pressure driving into their trapped leg while using your top leg to block their hip rotation toward you. Your shoulder should stay heavy on their thigh. If they begin rotating, ride with the rotation while adjusting your angle to stay perpendicular. The key is making their turn tighten your control rather than create escape space.

**Q4**: What is the correct grip sequence when transitioning from positional control to submission finish?
**A**: First establish structural control with leg configuration and hip pressure. Next, control the ankle with your outside arm to prevent knee rotation. Then clear any frames the opponent has on your hips. Only after these controls are established should you transition to heel grip — use your inside hand to cup the heel while your outside arm maintains ankle control. The figure-four finishing grip comes last.

**Q5**: How should you apply pressure with your hips to maximize control effectiveness?
**A**: Drive your hips into the opponent's trapped thigh at approximately a 45-degree angle toward their hip. This pressure prevents them from rotating either direction and maintains the perpendicular alignment that gives you mechanical advantage. Your hips should feel like a wedge that gets tighter when they move, not looser.

**Q6**: Your opponent begins framing on your shoulder while hiding their heel — what's your tactical response?
**A**: Address the threat hierarchy: frames before submissions. Use your free hand to strip or redirect their frame while maintaining hip pressure and leg configuration. Once the frame is cleared, recognize that their heel hiding has exposed their ankle — transition to **[[Submissions/Straight Ankle Lock|straight ankle lock]]** or **[[Submissions/Toe Hold|toe hold]]** threat. This forces them to choose between maintaining the frame or protecting the ankle, creating a new dilemma.

**Q7**: How do you manage energy expenditure when an opponent is actively resisting but not escaping?
**A**: Avoid the trap of constantly attacking submissions against strong defense. Instead, maintain positional control with minimal energy by relying on structural mechanics rather than muscular effort. Let your leg configuration and hip pressure do the work. Wait for defensive errors that open attack opportunities rather than forcing submissions against solid defense. The position should feel sustainable for extended periods.

**Q8**: What recovery protocol do you use if the opponent partially clears your hip pressure?
**A**: Immediately re-engage hip pressure before attempting to reestablish other controls. If they've created significant space, you may need to temporarily transition to **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami|Inside Ashi-Garami]]** and rebuild to Saddle. Never try to skip steps in re-establishing control — the hierarchy is perpendicular alignment, then hip pressure, then inside position, then grips. Forcing submissions from a compromised position leads to escapes.

<section id="faq" class="content-section">

## Frequently Asked Questions About Saddle Top

### What is the difference between Saddle and Honey Hole in BJJ?

**[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Honey Hole|Honey Hole]]** and **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle|Saddle]]** are different names for the exact same leg entanglement. Both describe the inside sankaku configuration — your inside leg triangles around the opponent's thigh just above the knee while your outside leg crosses their hip. The name Honey Hole is common in 10th Planet and Danaher systems; Saddle is used in other leg lock curricula. The 4/11 and Inside Sankaku labels also refer to this same position.

### Why is Saddle Top considered the most dominant leg entanglement?

Saddle Top creates a self-defending submission system. Every time the opponent defends one attack, they expose another: defending the **[[Submissions/Inside Heel Hook|inside heel hook]]** by hiding the heel exposes the ankle to **[[Submissions/Straight Ankle Lock|straight ankle lock]]**; defending the footlock by pulling toes back re-exposes the heel. No other leg entanglement in **[[Positions/Leg Entanglement|the ashi garami family]]** creates this level of layered offense.

### What are the best entries to Saddle Top in BJJ?

The four highest-percentage entries are:
1. **Back step from [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Outside Ashi-Garami|Outside Ashi-Garami]]** — rotate over the opponent's trapped leg
2. **Hip switch from [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami|Inside Ashi-Garami]]** — change the inside leg configuration
3. **Elevation from [[Positions/Ashi Garami/50-50 Guard|50-50 Guard]]** — use elevation to achieve inside position
4. **Angle change from [[Positions/Single Leg X-Guard|Single Leg X-Guard]]** — specific rotation to inside sankaku

### Is Saddle Top legal in BJJ competition?

Saddle position itself is always legal, but its primary finish — **[[Submissions/Inside Heel Hook|inside heel hook]]** — is restricted by ruleset. Inside heel hooks are legal in most no-gi submission-only events and IBJJF brown/black belt no-gi divisions. They are generally illegal in gi competition and for lower belt ranks in IBJJF no-gi. **[[Submissions/Straight Ankle Lock|Straight ankle lock]]** and **[[Submissions/Toe Hold|toe hold]]** from Saddle are legal at more competition levels. Always verify your specific ruleset.

### What does "inside position" mean in Saddle Top, and why does it matter?

Inside position means your entangling leg is on the inside between the opponent's legs, with your inside leg triangling around their thigh. Losing inside position — allowing the opponent to turn into you and face you with their chest — collapses the entire Saddle structure and allows guard recovery. Maintaining inside position is more important than any submission attempt. If you lose it, return to **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami|Inside Ashi-Garami]]** and rebuild.

### How do you avoid injuring your training partner from Saddle Top?

Apply all submissions slowly and progressively — especially **[[Submissions/Inside Heel Hook|inside heel hook]]**, which can cause ACL, MCL, and meniscus damage in under two seconds of breaking pressure. Always communicate before drilling, establish a clear tap protocol, and never apply heel hook pressure quickly in training. Kneebar and toe hold similarly require controlled application. Use a slow "squeeze-and-pause" approach to give your partner time to recognize and tap.
