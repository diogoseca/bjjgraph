<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**Ushiro Ashi-Garami Top** (reverse leg entanglement, attacking role) is the advanced no-gi position where you maintain a figure-four leg entanglement after the opponent inverts their hips to escape standard **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Outside Ashi-Garami]]** or **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]]**. Retention rate: 50%. Submission probability: 45%. Requires: outside leg crossing over opponent's knee line, inside leg underneath, and grip adaptation for the reversed hip angle.

Ushiro ashi-garami top occurs when your opponent attempts the inversion escape and you adapt your leg configuration rather than releasing the entanglement. Your outside leg crosses over their trapped leg while your inside leg stays underneath, creating heel hook and ankle lock opportunities from a reversed angle. This reversed orientation demands different grip positions, hip placement, and finishing mechanics than standard ashi-garami variations — practitioners who use standard grips without adjusting lose finishing leverage immediately.

The primary strategic objective is preventing complete escape while establishing sufficient control for submission attempts. Secondary objectives include transitioning to higher-control positions such as **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]]** or **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Honey Hole]]**, or following the opponent's continued inversion to establish **[[Positions/Back Control]]**. This position exists in dynamic tension — you must balance immediate submission commitment against readiness to follow further positional changes.

The position is most common in high-level no-gi competition, where sophisticated opponents use inversion to escape standard **[[Positions/Ashi Garami]]** positions. Attackers who haven't developed ushiro maintenance lose control entirely; those who have it can sustain offensive pressure through the opponent's entire defensive sequence. Average time in position: 10–20 seconds — this is a transitional, fast-decision position.

<section id="state-invariants" class="content-section">

## What Defines Ushiro Ashi-Garami Top?

- Attacker's legs maintain a figure-four configuration around the opponent's trapped leg with the outside leg crossing over the knee line and the inside leg underneath, adapted to the opponent's inverted hip position
- Attacker's hips are positioned to follow the opponent's inversion while maintaining leg entanglement integrity, requiring constant adjustment to the opponent's rotational movement and escape attempts
- Attacker maintains at least one controlling grip on the opponent's heel, ankle, or pants while managing hand-fighting efforts to prevent heel exposure and submission defense

<section id="prerequisites" class="content-section">

## When Does Ushiro Ashi-Garami Top Occur?

- Opponent attempted an inversion escape from a standard ashi-garami position, creating a reversed leg entanglement geometry
- Attacker successfully adapted their leg configuration during the opponent's rotation to maintain entanglement despite hip inversion
- Attacker has sufficient understanding of reversed submission mechanics to attack from an inverted opponent orientation


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## What Are the Key Offensive Principles?

- Adapt leg pressure and configuration continuously as the opponent inverts to prevent complete escape
- Adjust grip strategies for heel hooks to account for reversed hip orientation and different leverage angles
- Control the opponent's inside leg with your outside leg to prevent them from completing rotation to turtle or standing
- Use the opponent's inversion momentum to transition to higher-control positions like **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]]** or **[[Positions/Back Control]]**
- Recognize when the opponent has achieved sufficient rotation that maintaining ushiro becomes inefficient
- Balance submission attempts against position retention, prioritizing control when the opponent's escape is advanced
- Monitor the opponent's free leg position as an indicator of their next escape attempt or counter-attack opportunity


</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## What Are the Most Common Mistakes from Ushiro Ashi-Garami Top?

### 1. Maintaining standard ashi-garami grips without adjusting for opponent's inverted hip position

- ❌ **Consequence**: Heel hook finishing leverage becomes compromised as the opponent's reversed orientation changes optimal grip and hip placement requirements
- ✅ **Correction**: Adjust grip positions to account for inverted hip orientation, often requiring different hand placement on the heel and a different body angle relative to the opponent

### 2. Focusing exclusively on immediate submission without controlling opponent's continued rotation

- ❌ **Consequence**: Opponent completes escape to turtle or standing while attacker is committed to submission attempt with inadequate position control
- ✅ **Correction**: Balance submission attempts with position control, using the inside leg to prevent complete rotation while establishing finishing grips

### 3. Allowing opponent's free leg to get underneath attacker's body during inversion

- ❌ **Consequence**: Opponent uses free leg as a lever to complete rotation or enter counter-entanglement, escaping ushiro ashi-garami entirely
- ✅ **Correction**: Control the opponent's free leg with outside leg pressure, preventing them from using it as a rotational lever or posting mechanism

### 4. Releasing leg entanglement prematurely when opponent begins inverting

- ❌ **Consequence**: Allows complete escape when maintained control could have prevented rotation or created a submission opportunity during transition
- ✅ **Correction**: Maintain leg configuration through the opponent's inversion, adapting pressure and positioning rather than abandoning entanglement

### 5. Attempting to prevent inversion through pure strength rather than technical leg positioning

- ❌ **Consequence**: Exhausts energy in a failed attempt to stop inevitable rotation, leaving attacker too fatigued to capitalize on the ushiro position
- ✅ **Correction**: Allow controlled inversion while maintaining technical leg entanglement, conserving energy for submission or transition opportunities

### 6. Missing transition timing to saddle or honey hole when inside leg control is optimal

- ❌ **Consequence**: Opponent escapes from ushiro ashi-garami during the window when a transition to a higher-control position would have maintained the attack
- ✅ **Correction**: Recognize optimal transition windows based on the opponent's leg clearing progress, transitioning to **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]]** when inside leg is deeply controlled

## How Do You Train Ushiro Ashi-Garami Top?

### Inversion Following Drill

Partner starts in standard **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Outside Ashi-Garami]]** and begins an inversion escape sequence. Practice maintaining leg entanglement throughout their rotation, adjusting leg pressure and hip position to create the ushiro configuration. Focus on smooth adaptation to the opponent's movement rather than preventing inversion. Reset and repeat from various starting ashi-garami positions.

**Duration**: 5 minutes


### Ushiro Submission Development

Begin in an established ushiro ashi-garami position with a partner defending. Practice various **[[Submissions/Heel Hook from Ushiro Ashi]]** and **[[Submissions/Straight Ankle Lock]]** setups from the reversed configuration, developing grip adjustments and finishing mechanics specific to inverted opponent orientation. Partner provides realistic defensive hand-fighting and position adjustments.

**Duration**: 6 minutes


### Transition Recognition Training

Start in ushiro ashi-garami with a partner attempting various escape sequences. Practice recognizing optimal timing for transitions to **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]]**, **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Honey Hole]]**, or **[[Positions/Back Control]]** based on the partner's leg clearing progress and rotational commitment. Develop decision-making for when to pursue submission versus positional transition.

**Duration**: 5 minutes


### Flow Scrambling from Ushiro

Begin in ushiro ashi-garami with both practitioners working at 60% intensity. Defender attempts escapes while attacker maintains position and works submissions. Flow through realistic scramble sequences, developing comfort with dynamic transitions and continuous position adjustments characteristic of ushiro exchanges.

**Duration**: 4 minutes

## Test Your Knowledge

**Q1**: What leg configuration adjustment must you make when your opponent inverts from standard ashi-garami?
**A**: As the opponent inverts, adapt your figure-four by keeping your outside leg crossing over their knee line while your inside leg stays underneath. Follow their hip rotation rather than fighting it, adjusting your leg pressure to maintain entanglement integrity throughout their movement. This creates the ushiro configuration where you can still attack despite their reversed hip orientation.

**Q2**: Your opponent has begun inverting but their heel is momentarily exposed — what should you prioritize?
**A**: When the heel becomes exposed during inversion, you face a timing decision. If the exposure is clear and your grip position is favorable, immediately attack the heel hook before they complete rotation. If their inversion is progressing rapidly and your grip isn't optimal, prioritize following to maintain entanglement and transition to saddle or back control rather than a low-percentage submission attempt.

**Q3**: How does grip placement differ for heel hooks in ushiro versus standard outside ashi-garami?
**A**: In ushiro, the opponent's inverted hip position changes the optimal heel hook grip angle. You typically need to adjust your hand placement closer to your own hip line rather than reaching across their body. The reversed configuration requires a more compact finishing position with different shoulder and hip alignment relative to their trapped leg compared to standard **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Outside Ashi-Garami]]** mechanics.

**Q4**: What indicates that you should abandon the submission attempt and transition to saddle instead?
**A**: Transition to saddle when your opponent has successfully cleared your outside leg but your inside leg remains deeply controlling their thigh. Also transition when their continued rotation makes heel hook leverage progressively worse, or when their hand fighting has successfully prevented heel exposure for more than 3–4 seconds. The inside leg control creates the entry point for saddle transition.

**Q5**: Your opponent's free leg is working underneath your body during their inversion — how do you address this?
**A**: Use your outside leg to actively control their free leg by pressing it away from your center line. If their free leg gets underneath you, they gain a rotational lever to complete escape or enter counter-entanglement. Maintain outside leg pressure on their free thigh while using your inside leg to control the entangled leg, preventing them from using the free leg as a posting or pushing mechanism.

**Q6**: When is following to back control the superior option compared to maintaining ushiro ashi-garami?
**A**: Follow to **[[Positions/Back Control]]** when your opponent commits fully to turtle rotation and turns their back to escape, when your leg entanglement has degraded to the point where submission attempts have low success probability, or when they've cleared both legs but their turtle position exposes back access. The back take becomes optimal when their rotation momentum is too strong to reverse for leg attacks.

**Q7**: How do you maintain weight distribution to keep your opponent pinned while attacking from ushiro?
**A**: Keep your hips heavy and connected to the opponent's trapped leg, distributing weight through your inside leg control on their thigh. Avoid lifting your hips to reach for grips, as this creates space for escape. Angle your torso toward the trapped leg while maintaining constant downward pressure through your figure-four. Your weight should drive through your inside leg into their thigh rather than floating above them.

**Q8**: What are the key indicators that your opponent is about to attempt a counter-attack on your legs?
**A**: Watch for their free hand reaching toward your legs rather than defending their heel, hip movement that creates angle toward your lower body rather than away, attempts to square their hips back toward you during the inversion, or deliberate slowing of their escape rotation. These indicate they're shifting from escape to counter-attack, requiring you to either accelerate your finish or address their leg entanglement attempt.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Ushiro Ashi-Garami Top in BJJ?
Ushiro ashi-garami top is the attacking role in a reversed leg entanglement. It occurs when an opponent inverts their hips to escape standard ashi-garami and the attacker adapts their leg configuration to follow, maintaining a figure-four grip from a reversed angle. The position creates heel hook and ankle lock opportunities that require adjusted mechanics compared to standard leg locks.

### How does Ushiro Ashi-Garami differ from Outside Ashi-Garami?
In **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Outside Ashi-Garami]]**, the opponent's hips face toward you in a standard orientation. In ushiro ashi-garami, the opponent has inverted so their hips face away or sideways, reversing the biomechanical angles. This changes your heel hook grip position, hip placement, and finishing mechanics — standard grips from outside ashi-garami will not generate leverage from the ushiro angle.

### What submissions are available from Ushiro Ashi-Garami Top?
The primary submissions from ushiro ashi-garami top are the **[[Submissions/Heel Hook from Ushiro Ashi]]** (adjusted for inverted hip angle), the **[[Submissions/Straight Ankle Lock]]**, the **[[Submissions/Kneebar]]** via **[[Transitions/Kneebar from Ushiro]]**, and the **[[Submissions/Toe Hold]]**. Heel hooks require compact hand placement close to your hip line rather than the reach used in standard outside ashi.

### When should I transition from Ushiro Ashi-Garami to Saddle?
Transition to **[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Saddle]]** when your opponent clears your outside leg but your inside leg still controls their thigh deeply. This inside leg control is the entry point for saddle. If you wait until both legs are cleared, the opportunity is gone. Use **[[Transitions/Saddle Entry from Top]]** to execute this before the opponent finishes their escape.

### How do I stop my opponent from escaping Ushiro Ashi-Garami?
Control the opponent's free leg with your outside leg pressure to deny them a rotational lever. Keep your hips heavy and connected to their trapped leg. Do not try to prevent the inversion through strength — allow their rotation while adapting your leg configuration. Constant adjustment is more effective than resistance.

### Is Ushiro Ashi-Garami legal in competition?
Ushiro ashi-garami itself is legal across all grappling rulesets. The submissions available from it — particularly **[[Submissions/Inside Heel Hook]]** and **[[Submissions/Heel Hook from Ushiro Ashi]]** — are typically restricted to advanced divisions in IBJJF events and permitted in no-gi rulesets such as ADCC and EBI. Always verify divisional rules before competing.

### How does following to back control work from Ushiro Ashi-Garami?
Use **[[Transitions/Follow to Back Control]]** when the opponent commits to full turtle rotation. Rather than fighting the rotation, you follow it while releasing the leg entanglement and transitioning to a seat belt grip as their back becomes exposed. This works best when their rotational momentum is too strong to interrupt for a leg lock finish.

### What is the difference between Ushiro Ashi-Garami and the Honey Hole?
**[[Positions/Ashi Garami/Honey Hole]]** (also called the saddle) is a high-control inside heel hook position with both legs deeply controlling the opponent's thigh. Ushiro ashi-garami is a transitional reversed entanglement reached when the opponent inverts. Ushiro is less stable and lower control; the honey hole is a more dominant finishing position. Ushiro can transition into honey hole via saddle when inside leg control is established.
