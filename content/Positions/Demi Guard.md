<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**Demi Guard is a gi lapel guard that combines a collar grip, the opponent's lapel behind their knee, and a foot pressing on the lapel tail to resist pressure and create elevation.**

Keenan Cornelius introduces Demi Guard as an answer to a passer driving forward after a worm-wrestling attempt. The bottom player's foot has left the worm configuration, so rebuilding that guard may be difficult. Demi supplies a different connection at the knee rather than requiring the original wrap to be restored. See [Keenan's introduction](https://jiujitsux.com/courses/keenans-lapel-encyclopedia/lesson/16-1-introduction-to-the-demi-guard/).

[Legion's dictionary](https://legionsandiego.com/the-jiu-jitsu-dictionary/#demi-guard) describes the collar grip, lapel behind the passer's knee, and foot on the lapel tail. The guard player stays in front of the passer with bent legs between their bodies; the fabric supports the foot's work against pressure. The top player must deal with that connection while keeping balance.

Demi belongs with [[Positions/Lapel Guard]] and connects to [[Positions/Worm Guard]]. It is distinct from [[Positions/Half Guard]], whose defining control is trapping one opponent's leg between both legs. Half guard can be a recovery destination after releasing the lapel structure. A lapel is essential to Demi, so this position has no no-gi equivalent. Back takes and submissions are follow-ups through recovered guards, not automatic results of holding Demi.

</section>



<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Principles


- Recognize the collar, knee-line lapel and foot as a connected guard structure.

- Keep the legs between the players while the top player presses forward.

- Use the lapel connection to create room and elevation, not to twist a trapped knee.

- Distinguish a completed guard configuration from merely holding loose fabric.

- Recover another guard when the foot-on-lapel connection is lost.

- Clear the lapel and leg barrier before treating a passing attempt as complete.


</section>

<section id="bottom-summary" class="content-section">


## Playing as Bottom

[[Demi Guard/Bottom|→ Full Bottom Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Keep the collar and knee-line connections working together.

- Place the foot on the lapel tail rather than merely holding loose cloth.

- Keep bent legs between you and the passer.

- Read forward pressure before attempting elevation.

- Recover another guard promptly when the Demi connection is lost.


### Available Transitions


- **[[Transitions/Demi Guard Elevation Sweep/Attacker|Demi Guard Elevation Sweep]]** (gi only)

- **[[Transitions/Demi Guard to Lapel Guard/Attacker|Demi Guard to Lapel Guard]]** (gi only)

- **[[Transitions/Demi Guard to Half Guard/Attacker|Demi Guard to Half Guard]]** (gi only)


### Common Mistakes


- ❌ **Holding fabric without engaging the foot**
  - Consequence: The characteristic knee-line barrier is incomplete.
  - ✅ Correction: Confirm the lapel path and foot contact before relying on Demi.

- ❌ **Letting both knees drift behind the passer**
  - Consequence: The passer can approach your torso without a leg barrier.
  - ✅ Correction: Recover your legs in front before attempting another lapel feed.

- ❌ **Trying to lift a balanced opponent with the arms**
  - Consequence: The grips tire without creating a useful change of base.
  - ✅ Correction: Wait for or create a visible weight shift before elevation.

- ❌ **Hanging onto a lost lapel while being passed**
  - Consequence: The grip occupies a hand needed for frames and recovery.
  - ✅ Correction: Release the ineffective connection and recover a guard.

- ❌ **Keeping the lapel bound after coming on top**
  - Consequence: The same connection can obstruct your own stabilization.
  - ✅ Correction: Release unnecessary fabric and establish the actual landing position.


</section>

<section id="top-summary" class="content-section">


## Playing as Top

[[Demi Guard/Top|→ Full Top Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Keep balance while identifying the loaded foot and fabric.

- Reduce the lapel connection before committing forward weight.

- Move the controlled leg only when the fabric permits it.

- Recognize a remaining leg trap as half guard.

- Complete leg clearance before settling a side-control pin.


### Available Transitions


- **[[Transitions/Lapel Clear from Demi Guard/Attacker|Lapel Clear from Demi Guard]]** (gi only)

- **[[Transitions/Pass from Demi Guard/Attacker|Pass from Demi Guard]]** (gi only)


### Common Mistakes


- ❌ **Driving into the intact lapel barrier**
  - Consequence: Your weight loads the guard player's elevation structure.
  - ✅ Correction: Restore balance and address the foot and fabric first.

- ❌ **Pulling the controlled knee free by force**
  - Consequence: The remaining wrap can bind the leg during the movement.
  - ✅ Correction: Loosen the fabric before moving the controlled leg.

- ❌ **Watching only the collar-gripping hand**
  - Consequence: The foot-on-lapel connection remains active below you.
  - ✅ Correction: Track the collar, foot and knee-line connection together.

- ❌ **Treating a cleared lapel as a finished pass**
  - Consequence: The opponent's legs can still retain an ordinary guard.
  - ✅ Correction: Check whether a leg trap or open-guard frame remains.

- ❌ **Reaching with both hands while losing base**
  - Consequence: The bottom player can redirect your unsupported weight.
  - ✅ Correction: Keep a stable base throughout the grip-clearing exchange.
