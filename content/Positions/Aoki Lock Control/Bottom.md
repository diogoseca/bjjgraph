<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The bottom player is responsible for maintaining the control. The foot-lock player is seated or on the side facing the opponent's lower body. The opposing foot is turned outward and enclosed near the armpit, upper arm, and forearm. The attacker has a connection to the hip or leg that limits the opponent's free movement. The captured player must free the foot and leg control before attempting an uncontrolled turn.

Recognize the foot orientation without twisting it into position against resistance. Hold the instep and heel region securely beside the upper body rather than relying on calf compression. Use the entanglement or foot-on-hip frame to manage distance before considering a finish.

Stop the drill at a secure capture; the control can become a damaging rotation with very little movement. Keep track of your own exposed feet while concentrating on the captured foot. Open the arm enclosure and remove leg pressure immediately on a tap, sudden movement, or pain.

The captured limb is the foot and lower leg. This is distinct from an omoplata-style shoulder trap and from a bent-knee calf slicer. Either player can become the foot-lock attacker during an entanglement exchange. Rotational foot locks can injure the ankle or knee before pain provides useful warning. Use cooperative capture-and-release practice, no spinning escapes, and the event's actual division rules.

</section>
