<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**Half Butterfly Bottom** is a dynamic hybrid guard combining [[Positions/Half Guard]] (one leg trapped) with a butterfly hook inserted under the opponent's free thigh. Success rates: Beginner ~25% sweep completion, Advanced ~55%. Prerequisites: half guard control, hip mobility, and at least one upper body connection.

Half Butterfly Bottom controls one of the opponent's legs between the bottom player's legs (the half guard component) while a butterfly hook on the free side creates constant upward lifting pressure. This dual-threat structure forces the top player to simultaneously defend both the half guard escape and the butterfly hook elevation — an offensive dilemma that pure [[Positions/Half Guard]] cannot create. The position enables multiple high-percentage sweep variations, back-take opportunities, and smooth transitions to [[Positions/Deep Half Guard]], [[Positions/X-Guard]], and [[Positions/Single Leg X-Guard]].

Unlike traditional half guard, which trends defensive, Half Butterfly Bottom maintains constant attacking pressure. The position was developed and popularized by **Eddie Bravo** (the [[Transitions/Old School Sweep]] system), **Marcelo Garcia**, and **Lucas Leite**, each recognizing that the butterfly hook creates additional vectors of control and sweep mechanics that pure half guard lacks. It performs especially well in no-gi grappling and modern competitive BJJ, where hip mobility and elevation generate the attack rather than grip strength.

</section>

<section id="half-butterfly-vs-half-guard" class="content-section">

## What Is the Difference Between Half Guard and Half Butterfly Guard?

[[Positions/Half Guard]] is primarily positional and defensive — you trap one of the opponent's legs and prevent the pass. Half butterfly adds one critical modification: a foot hook inserted under the opponent's free thigh (the butterfly hook), which generates upward lifting force unavailable in standard half guard.

**Side-by-side comparison:**

| Feature | Half Guard | Half Butterfly Guard |
|---|---|---|
| Primary purpose | Defensive retention | Offensive sweeping platform |
| Sweep mechanics | Hip-based, horizontal | Butterfly hook elevation + hip combination |
| Back-take access | Limited | Direct via underhook + hook elevation |
| Single Leg X entry | Uncommon | Primary pathway |
| Opponent passing ease | Moderate | Harder — elevation creates constant threat |
| Beginner accessibility | High | Moderate-High |

The butterfly hook is the decisive addition. It forces the opponent to manage upward pressure — they cannot simply drive their weight forward as they can in pure half guard. Every attempt to flatten you creates a sweep opportunity. The [[Transitions/Old School Sweep]] (22% attempt rate) is unavailable without the hook; the [[Transitions/Basic Butterfly Sweep]] is far weaker without it.

</section>

<section id="state-invariants" class="content-section">

## What Defines the Half Butterfly Bottom Position?

- Bottom player has one leg of the opponent trapped between their own legs (half guard control), both legs wrapped around the trapped leg to prevent extraction
- Bottom player's free leg maintains a butterfly hook positioned under the opponent's free-side thigh, with foot planted on the mat and heel pulled close to their own hip — creating a loaded elevation spring
- Bottom player is on their side with shoulders turned toward the opponent, maintaining active frames with at least one underhook or overhook grip on the opponent's upper body
- Opponent must distribute pressure defensively across two simultaneous threats: the half guard escape and the butterfly hook sweep

</section>

<section id="prerequisites" class="content-section">

## What Are the Prerequisites for Half Butterfly Bottom?

- Opponent's leg successfully trapped in half guard configuration between your legs
- Sufficient space created to insert the butterfly hook on the free-leg side
- Active hip mobility — bottom player can turn toward the opponent rather than being flat
- At least one upper body connection established (underhook, overhook, or collar grip)
- Hips not completely flattened to the mat — lateral hip position is required for hook function

</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## How Do You Retain Half Butterfly Guard Against Pressure?

Guard retention in Half Butterfly Bottom depends on active movement, not passive structure. Key principles:

- **Maintain constant hook elevation**: A passive butterfly hook loses its threat — keep upward pressure active even between sweep attempts to prevent the opponent from settling their weight and establishing passing rhythm
- **Stay on your side**: Turning flat to your back eliminates hip mobility for sweeps and allows the opponent to establish crossface control; keep shoulders angled toward the opponent at all times
- **Fight for the underhook**: The underhook on the butterfly hook side maximizes sweep effectiveness and prevents flattening; losing it dramatically shifts positional control toward the top player
- **Coordinate both legs as a system**: As the butterfly hook lifts, the half guard legs simultaneously squeeze the trapped leg — creating two simultaneous threats the opponent cannot address at once
- **Use constant motion**: Static positions allow the opponent to establish grips and timing; continuous angle changes and sweep threats keep them defensive
- **Frame against crossface pressure**: When heavy crossface pressure develops, frame with your outside arm against their bicep or shoulder before shrimping laterally to recreate space
- **Have a backup plan**: When the butterfly hook is stripped, transition to [[Positions/Deep Half Guard]] by inverting under their hips, or recover [[Positions/Butterfly Guard]] by releasing the half guard and inserting the second hook before they can establish a passing grip

</section>

<section id="single-leg-x-pathway" class="content-section">

## How Does Half Butterfly Connect to Single Leg X-Guard and Leg Entanglements?

Half Butterfly Bottom is a primary entry point into [[Positions/Single Leg X-Guard]] (also called Ashi Garami or SLX). The pathway is direct:

1. **Establish active butterfly hook** under the opponent's free thigh with strong upward pressure
2. **Elevate the hooked leg** while reaching your outside arm to grab their ankle
3. **Slide your body underneath** as their leg rises, threading into Single Leg X configuration
4. **Secure SLX grips**: outside arm controls ankle, inside arm hooks behind their knee or controls hip

From [[Positions/Single Leg X-Guard]], immediate options include:
- **[[Transitions/Single Leg X Sweep]]** → mount or top control
- Entry into heel hook sequences via [[Positions/Ashi Garami]]
- Transition to full [[Positions/X-Guard]] with **[[Transitions/Butterfly Guard to X-Guard]]**

This pathway makes Half Butterfly an important gateway to the modern leg entanglement game, particularly in no-gi competition where [[Positions/Ashi Garami]] entries are high-percentage at all competitive levels. Understanding this connection also helps when the opponent blocks the traditional sweeps — the single leg entry provides a third attacking option that many top players are still learning to defend.

</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## What Are the Most Common Mistakes in Half Butterfly Guard?

### 1. Allowing the butterfly hook to become passive without active lifting pressure

- ❌ **Consequence**: Opponent can settle their weight and establish stable passing pressure, neutralizing all sweep threats
- ✅ **Correction**: Maintain constant upward pressure through the butterfly hook, actively lifting and off-balancing the opponent even when not executing a sweep

### 2. Letting the opponent flatten you completely to your back

- ❌ **Consequence**: Loses hip mobility needed for sweeps and allows the opponent to establish dominant crossface control
- ✅ **Correction**: Stay on your side with shoulders turned toward the opponent, using frames and grips to prevent being flattened while maintaining hip escape mobility

### 3. Losing half guard control while focusing on the butterfly hook

- ❌ **Consequence**: Opponent extracts the trapped leg and easily passes to side control or mount
- ✅ **Correction**: Coordinate both legs together — half guard legs squeeze the trapped leg while the butterfly hook lifts, creating dual threats the opponent must address simultaneously

### 4. Using only arm strength to execute sweeps without hip elevation

- ❌ **Consequence**: Sweeps are weak and easily defended, leading to arm fatigue and failed sweep attempts
- ✅ **Correction**: Drive sweeps primarily from hip extension and butterfly hook elevation, using grips only to guide the opponent's fall and control their upper body during the finish

### 5. Maintaining a static position without threatening constant movement

- ❌ **Consequence**: Gives the opponent time to establish grips, settle weight, and methodically work passing sequences
- ✅ **Correction**: Create constant motion through hip movement, angle changes, and sweep attempts to keep the opponent defensive and prevent them from establishing rhythm

### 6. Allowing the opponent to achieve underhook on the half guard side

- ❌ **Consequence**: Opponent can drive shoulder pressure, flatten you, and control hip movement for easier passing
- ✅ **Correction**: Fight for underhook on the trapped leg side or establish strong frames to prevent their underhook from controlling your upper body

<section id="training-drills" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Drills for Half Butterfly Guard

### Butterfly Hook Elevation Drill

Partner takes the top half butterfly position. Practice lifting and off-balancing them using only butterfly hook hip extension while maintaining half guard control. Focus on coordinating the hip lift with angle creation — the elevation should come from the leg and hip, not the arms.

**Duration**: 5 minutes per side


### Sweep Transition Flow

Start in half butterfly bottom. Partner gives 50% resistance. Flow between different sweep options — [[Transitions/Basic Butterfly Sweep]], [[Transitions/Old School Sweep]], [[Transitions/Hook Sweep]] — based on the partner's weight distribution. Emphasis on reading pressure and selecting the appropriate sweep rather than forcing one option.

**Duration**: 3 minutes per round


### Half Butterfly Retention Sparring

Bottom player maintains half butterfly while top player attempts to pass at full effort. Bottom player wins by executing a successful sweep or maintaining position for 2 minutes. Top player wins by passing. Resets on success or sweep. Develops retention and timing under live pressure.

**Duration**: 5 minutes per round


### Guard Recovery to Half Butterfly

Start with partner achieving initial passing pressure from above. Practice recovering half butterfly using hip escapes, frames, and butterfly hook insertion. Simulates realistic guard recovery scenarios where the position must be re-established rather than initially set up.

**Duration**: 10 reps per side

## Test Your Knowledge

**Q1**: What is the essential weight distribution for maintaining an effective butterfly hook in half butterfly bottom?
**A**: Position yourself on your side rather than flat on your back, with your hips angled toward your opponent. The butterfly hook foot should be planted on the mat with your heel pulled toward your own hip, creating a loaded spring position. Your weight should be distributed through your shoulder and hip on the mat side, keeping your hips mobile and ready to elevate. This positioning maximizes the hook's lifting power.

**Q2**: Your opponent starts applying heavy crossface pressure — what immediate adjustment prevents being flattened?
**A**: Frame with your inside arm against their bicep or shoulder to create space, while simultaneously shrimping your hips away from the crossface side. Use your butterfly hook to lift and disrupt their weight distribution. If the crossface is too strong, transition to [[Positions/Deep Half Guard]] by shooting your outside arm under their hips and inverting your angle. Never stay flat and absorb crossface pressure passively.

**Q3**: What are the essential grip priorities when establishing half butterfly bottom?
**A**: First priority is the underhook on the butterfly hook side — this maximizes sweep effectiveness and prevents the opponent from flattening you. Second priority is controlling their far arm or collar to prevent them from posting during sweeps. Third is maintaining active frames to manage distance. The underhook is critical because it creates the diagonal angle needed for high-percentage sweeps like the [[Transitions/Old School Sweep]].

**Q4**: How do you shut down the opponent's primary passing attempt (knee slice) from this position?
**A**: When you feel them beginning a knee slice, immediately create angle by turning toward the passing direction while driving your butterfly hook upward to disrupt their base. Use your half guard legs to prevent their knee from cutting through by squeezing and elevating slightly. If they commit to the slice, transition to [[Positions/Deep Half Guard]] by diving under. Never let them flatten you first — mobility is your primary defense.

**Q5**: How should you coordinate the butterfly hook and half guard legs to maximize sweeping power?
**A**: The legs work as a unified system — as you elevate with the butterfly hook, simultaneously tighten your half guard squeeze on the trapped leg. This creates a dual-vector off-balancing force that is extremely difficult to counter. The half guard prevents them from posting their trapped leg while the hook removes their base on the free side. Time the squeeze and lift together rather than sequentially.

**Q6**: Your opponent posts their hand to stop your butterfly sweep — what technique chain addresses this?
**A**: When they post, immediately attack the posted arm with a kimura grip while maintaining your hook elevation. If they defend the kimura, their weight shifts and opens the sweep again. Alternatively, use the post as an opportunity to transition to [[Positions/X-Guard]] by extending your hook leg under their posted side while controlling their far leg. The post creates vulnerability that you can exploit with the right follow-up.

**Q7**: How do you manage energy expenditure while maintaining an active half butterfly guard?
**A**: Use your skeletal structure rather than constant muscular tension — let your hook rest with heel on the mat between sweep attempts rather than holding it elevated. Create activity through hip movement and angle changes that require minimal energy. Time your explosive sweep attempts for when the opponent creates openings rather than forcing constantly. Rest between exchanges by maintaining frames rather than grip fighting continuously.

**Q8**: Your opponent successfully extracts their trapped leg — how do you recover guard retention?
**A**: Immediately recover full [[Positions/Butterfly Guard]] by inserting your free leg as a second hook before they can establish passing grips. If they are too fast, transition to seated guard and use your frames to manage distance while working to re-establish hooks. Never allow them to consolidate a passing position — even a scrappy guard recovery is better than accepting side control. Stay active and use hip movement to create space for hook insertion.

<section id="faq" class="content-section">

## Frequently Asked Questions About Half Butterfly Guard

### What is the half butterfly guard in BJJ?

**Half butterfly guard** is a hybrid ground position combining a traditional [[Positions/Half Guard]] (one leg trapped between both of your legs) with a butterfly hook inserted under the opponent's free thigh. The butterfly hook generates upward leverage that pure half guard cannot produce, creating constant sweep and back-take threats that force the opponent to react rather than pass at will.

### What is the difference between half guard and half butterfly guard?

[[Positions/Half Guard]] is primarily positional and defensive — you trap one leg and prevent the pass. Half butterfly adds a butterfly hook on the free side, converting the position to an offensive platform. The hook forces the opponent to manage upward pressure rather than simply driving forward, making half butterfly significantly harder to pass and far more dangerous for the bottom player.

### What are the best sweeps from half butterfly guard?

In order of attempt frequency from this position:
1. **[[Transitions/Old School Sweep]]** (22% attempt rate) — underhook + butterfly hook lift + far ankle grab
2. **[[Transitions/Basic Butterfly Sweep]]** (15%) — seated elevation with hip drive
3. **[[Transitions/Underhook Sweep from Half]]** (12%) — deep underhook drive through the opponent's base
4. **[[Transitions/Deep Half Entry]]** (12%) — not a sweep but the highest-probability position transition under pressure
5. **[[Transitions/Hook Sweep]]** (10%) — butterfly hook elevation with upper body pull

### How do you retain half butterfly guard against pressure?

Key tactics: stay on your side (not flat), maintain active butterfly hook pressure at all times, frame against crossface with your outside arm, and initiate sweeps before passes complete to force reactions. When the hook is stripped, transition immediately to [[Positions/Deep Half Guard]] or [[Positions/Butterfly Guard]] — never accept a flat pinned position.

### Who popularized half butterfly guard?

**Eddie Bravo** built the 10th Planet half butterfly system around the [[Transitions/Old School Sweep]]. **Marcelo Garcia** linked it to deep half guard transitions. **Bernardo Faria** and **Lucas Leite** refined back-take and side control connections. Today it appears across gi and no-gi competition at all levels, from white belts learning their first sweeps to world champions.

### How does half butterfly connect to Single Leg X-Guard?

When the butterfly hook elevates the opponent's leg and the outside arm grabs their ankle, the bottom player can slide under to establish [[Positions/Single Leg X-Guard]] (Ashi Garami). This makes half butterfly a direct gateway to leg entanglement entries, including heel hooks in no-gi competition. See the **How Does Half Butterfly Connect to Single Leg X-Guard** section above for step-by-step entry mechanics.

### Is half butterfly guard effective for beginners?

Yes — the half guard base is familiar and the butterfly hook adds a single new concept. Beginner sweep success rates are approximately 25%, rising to 55% at advanced levels. New players should focus on underhook mechanics and timing the hook elevation with hip extension before adding complex combinations like the [[Positions/Single Leg X-Guard]] entry or back takes.
