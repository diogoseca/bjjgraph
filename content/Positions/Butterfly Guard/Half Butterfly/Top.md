<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**Half Butterfly Top** is the top player's position when one side of the **[[Positions/Butterfly Guard]]** has been passed but the bottom player retains one butterfly hook. Pass success rate: **Knee Slice ~70%, Smash Pass ~55%, Underhook Pass ~65%**. Requires: chest-forward pressure, crossface control, wide knee base.

This is a critical juncture in modern guard passing. The top player must neutralize the remaining hook while maintaining forward pressure and advancing the pass. The position arises during **[[Transitions/Knee Slice Pass]]** attempts, **[[Transitions/Smash Pass]]** entries, or when passing aggressive butterfly guard players who fight to retain at least one hook.

Success requires understanding both pressure passing principles and the biomechanics of how the butterfly hook generates leverage. The top player must establish upper body control first through crossface or underhook, then systematically reduce the hook's effectiveness through strategic weight distribution and hip positioning.

</section>

<section id="state-invariants" class="content-section">

## What Is Half Butterfly Top in BJJ?

**[[Positions/Butterfly Guard/Half Butterfly]]** top occurs when:

- Top player has passed to one side with upper body control while bottom player maintains one butterfly hook on the opposite side
- Top player's weight is distributed forward with chest pressure on bottom player's upper body, hips driving toward the mat to reduce hook effectiveness
- Bottom player is on their back or side with one leg creating a butterfly hook under the top player's thigh while the other leg is controlled or passed
- Top player's base is established with knees wide and hips low, creating a stable platform to resist the hook's lifting mechanics

**Key difference from standard Half Guard Top:** The butterfly hook adds sweeping mechanics and elevation threat that standard **[[Positions/Half Guard]]** lacks. The top player cannot sit upright safely.

</section>

<section id="how-to-enter" class="content-section">

## How Do You Enter Half Butterfly Top?

Half butterfly top is most often entered during a guard pass attempt, not sought directly. Common entry paths:

1. **During a knee slice pass** — bottom player retains one butterfly hook as you begin cutting your knee across
2. **Passing butterfly guard** — you clear one side of **[[Positions/Butterfly Guard]]** but the bottom player fights to keep one hook
3. **Bottom player converts from half guard** — they insert a butterfly hook mid-scramble to create sweeping leverage
4. **Failed long step pass** — bottom player scoops one hook while you attempt to step over

**Who should learn this position?** Intermediate practitioners who already pass **[[Positions/Half Guard]]** and want to handle active butterfly guard players. White belts benefit from learning the fundamental pressure principles here before advancing to complex passing chains.

</section>

<section id="prerequisites" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

- Partial pass of butterfly guard with one side cleared
- Control of bottom player's upper body through crossface, underhook, or collar grips
- Bottom player retains one functional butterfly hook
- Top player has established forward pressure and weight distribution


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## How Do You Maintain Control from Half Butterfly Top?

- Keep weight distributed forward onto bottom player's chest to reduce butterfly hook's lifting effectiveness
- Control the near-side arm with underhook or overhook to prevent bottom player from creating frames
- Drive hips low and forward to flatten bottom player and reduce space for hook engagement
- Establish crossface or head control to limit bottom player's ability to turn into you
- Maintain wide base with knees to prevent being swept while creating passing angles
- Prevent bottom player from recovering full **[[Positions/Butterfly Guard]]** by controlling their free leg
- Use strategic weight shifts to bait reactions and create passing opportunities


</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Why Are You Getting Swept from Half Butterfly Top?

### 1. Standing too upright with weight off bottom player's chest

- ❌ **Consequence**: Butterfly hook becomes fully effective and bottom player can generate powerful lifting mechanics for sweeps
- ✅ **Correction**: Drive chest forward and down onto bottom player's upper body, keeping hips low to minimize hook's leverage

### 2. Allowing bottom player to establish strong underhook on passing side

- ❌ **Consequence**: Bottom player can elevate top player's weight and create sweeping opportunities or guard recovery
- ✅ **Correction**: Fight for overhook or underhook control, use crossface to prevent bottom player from turning into you

### 3. Keeping narrow base with knees too close together

- ❌ **Consequence**: Unstable platform makes top player vulnerable to sweeps and reduces ability to generate passing pressure
- ✅ **Correction**: Widen base with knees spread to create stable foundation while maintaining forward pressure

### 4. Focusing only on passing without controlling bottom player's upper body

- ❌ **Consequence**: Bottom player can frame, create angles, and either sweep or recover full guard position
- ✅ **Correction**: Establish crossface or head control first, then work passing sequences with upper body secured

### 5. Allowing bottom player to create too much space between bodies

- ❌ **Consequence**: Bottom player can insert second butterfly hook, create frames, or initiate guard recovery sequences
- ✅ **Correction**: Maintain tight chest-to-chest connection with constant forward pressure to limit bottom player's space

### 6. Committing weight too early to one passing direction

- ❌ **Consequence**: Bottom player reads commitment and can time sweeps or guard recoveries in opposite direction
- ✅ **Correction**: Use feints and weight shifts to create reactions before committing to specific passing lane

<section id="training-drills" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## How to Train Half Butterfly Top: Drills That Build Real Skills

### Pressure Maintenance Drill

Bottom player maintains one butterfly hook while top player practices maintaining forward pressure and preventing hook effectiveness. Focus on weight distribution, chest pressure, and hip positioning. Bottom player provides progressive resistance, attempting to sweep when top player's pressure weakens.

**Duration**: 3 minutes per round


### Passing Chain Flow Drill

From half butterfly top, top player flows between different passing options (**[[Transitions/Knee Slice Pass]]**, **[[Transitions/Smash Pass]]**, long step, **[[Transitions/Leg Drag Pass]]**) while bottom player provides 50% resistance and maintains one butterfly hook. Focus on smooth transitions between passing techniques and maintaining control throughout. Emphasize connection and pressure rather than speed.

**Duration**: 5 minutes continuous flow


### Hook Neutralization Drill

Bottom player has one butterfly hook inserted and attempts to maximize its effectiveness. Top player practices various methods to neutralize the hook: weight distribution, hip positioning, leg weaving, and strategic grips. Restart position after each successful neutralization or sweep. Develops understanding of hook mechanics and counters.

**Duration**: 2 minutes per round


### Reaction-Based Passing Drill

Top player maintains half butterfly top while bottom player randomly chooses between three defensive actions: sweep attempt, guard recovery, or turtle. Top player must recognize the action and execute the appropriate counter (leg weave for sweep, knee slice for recovery, back take from turtle). Develops pattern recognition and reactive passing.

**Duration**: 4 minutes per round

## Test Your Knowledge

**Q1**: What is the most critical weight distribution adjustment to neutralize the butterfly hook's lifting power?
**A**: Drive your chest forward and down onto the bottom player's upper body while keeping your hips low and heavy. This shifts the load away from the hook's optimal leverage point near the thigh and distributes your weight where the bottom player's structural support is weakest. Avoid sitting upright, which allows the hook to engage fully and generate maximum elevation.

**Q2**: Your opponent begins to elevate with their butterfly hook — what immediate adjustment prevents the sweep?
**A**: Immediately widen your base by spreading your knees outward while driving your hips forward toward the mat. Simultaneously apply heavier chest pressure and drive your shoulder across their face with a crossface. This combination removes space for the hook to generate lift and pins their upper body, killing the sweep before momentum builds.

**Q3**: What are the essential grips and control points for maintaining half butterfly top position?
**A**: The primary controls are crossface pressure controlling their head position, underhook or overhook on the near-side arm to prevent framing, and control of the far hip or leg to prevent guard recovery. The crossface is paramount as it limits their ability to turn into you and generate sweeping angles.

**Q4**: How do you shut down the bottom player's primary escape route to deep half guard?
**A**: Keep your hips heavy and your inside knee positioned to block their head from diving underneath you. When you feel them attempting to turn away and shoot their outside arm under your hips, immediately sprawl your hips back and drive weight forward to flatten them. Maintaining constant chest pressure prevents them from creating the angle needed to enter **[[Positions/Half Guard/Deep Half Guard]]**.

**Q5**: What base errors most commonly lead to being swept from half butterfly top?
**A**: The three most common base errors are: keeping knees too close together, allowing hips to rise above shoulder level, and posting weight on your hands instead of distributing through your chest and hips. Each error gives the hook maximum mechanical advantage for sweeps.

**Q6**: The bottom player gets an underhook and starts coming up — what technique chain prevents the sweep?
**A**: When they establish the underhook and begin to elevate, immediately circle toward their back while maintaining head position. Transition to the **[[Transitions/Underhook Pass/Attacker|Underhook Pass]]** by driving your overhook deep, sprawling your hips away, and using your head to drive them back down.

**Q7**: How do you manage energy expenditure while maintaining pressure in this position?
**A**: Use skeletal structure rather than muscular effort — let your bodyweight work through proper alignment rather than actively pushing. Keep your hips low so you're driving weight downward naturally. Avoid constant movement; settle into pressure and wait for their reactions to guide your passing.

**Q8**: Your opponent partially recovers butterfly guard by inserting their second hook — how do you recover control?
**A**: Immediately address the new hook by driving your hips toward the mat on that side and using your knee or shin to block the hook's elevation. Reestablish crossface pressure and work to clear one hook at a time. Do not try to pass through double hooks — retreat slightly if needed to reset with only one hook engaged.

<section id="faq" class="content-section">

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the butterfly hook and how does it work?

The butterfly hook is a foot-to-thigh insertion where the bottom player places their foot under the top player's thigh to generate upward lifting force. When the top player sits upright or allows space, the hook can lever them off-balance for powerful sweeps. The hook relies on the top player's weight being over the insertion point — forward chest pressure and low hips neutralize its effectiveness.

### How do you pass half butterfly guard?

Neutralize the butterfly hook by driving chest weight forward and keeping hips low. Most effective passes: **[[Transitions/Knee Slice Pass]]** (~70%), **[[Transitions/Underhook Pass]]** (~65%), **[[Transitions/Long Step Pass]]** (~60%), **[[Transitions/Smash Pass]]** (~55%). Crossface control of the bottom player's head is essential before committing to any passing lane.

### What is the difference between half guard and half butterfly guard?

In standard **[[Positions/Half Guard]]**, the bottom player uses two legs to trap one of the top player's legs, relying on leg entanglement. In half butterfly guard, one leg still traps while the other is inserted as a butterfly hook under the top player's thigh. The butterfly hook adds sweeping mechanics and elevation threat that standard half guard lacks, making it significantly more dangerous if the top player sits upright.

### What is the knee slice pass?

The **[[Transitions/Knee Slice Pass]]** is a guard-passing technique where the top player drives their knee across the bottom player's thigh, cutting through to **[[Positions/Side Control]]**. From half butterfly top it is the highest-percentage pass (~70% success). The passer establishes crossface, drops their hips, and slices the knee toward the mat while shifting weight toward the bottom player's hip.

### What is the smash pass in BJJ?

The **[[Transitions/Smash Pass]]** involves using heavy chest and body pressure to flatten the bottom player and drive through their guard. From half butterfly top, the passer drives weight into the bottom player's torso to eliminate the butterfly hook's effectiveness, then slides to the side to clear the leg and achieve **[[Positions/Side Control]]**.

### How do you neutralize the butterfly hook when passing?

1. Drive your chest forward and down onto the bottom player's upper body — removes your center of gravity from above the hook
2. Keep your hips low and angled toward the mat
3. Widen your knee base to create a stable platform that resists upward sweeping force
4. Establish crossface to prevent the bottom player from creating angles that maximize hook leverage

### What happens when the bottom player gets deep half guard?

**[[Positions/Half Guard/Deep Half Guard]]** occurs when the bottom player successfully dives their head and outside arm under the top player's hips from half butterfly position — a very strong sweeping and back-take position. To prevent entry, keep your inside knee blocking their head and maintain constant chest pressure. If deep half is entered, sprawl hips back and flatten the bottom player immediately.

### What is the leg weave pass in BJJ?

The leg weave pass (also called leg lace) is a technique where the top player weaves their leg around the bottom player's butterfly hook to lock it down and prevent elevation, then passes to **[[Positions/Side Control]]**. Most effective when the bottom player actively extends their hook for a sweep attempt, giving the passer the opportunity to capture and weave the leg in one motion.

### How is half butterfly top different in no-gi BJJ?

Without gi collar grips, upper body control shifts to:
- **Head control**: driving your head/chin into their chin or temple instead of crossface grip
- **Arm drag entries**: using underhooks and arm drags to control the near-side arm
- **Wrist control**: grabbing the wrist to kill the underhook before it establishes

The core principles (chest pressure, low hips, wide base) remain identical. **[[Transitions/Leg Drag Pass]]** and **[[Transitions/Knee Slice Pass]]** both translate directly to no-gi with grip adjustments.
