<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The short armbar from Dead Orchard uses the high triangle around both opposing arms to isolate a wrist and extend one elbow with controlled, immediately releasable pressure.

Begin underneath with Dead Orchard's high figure-four containing both opposing arms at the shoulder line. Control one captured wrist without letting the other arm or shoulder escape the established high triangle.

Maintain the elbow's relationship to the leg frame before attempting any extension. Adjust through a comfortable hip angle so the elbow extends along its natural line, with no wrist or shoulder twist. Take up slack gradually. The trained partner taps before the elbow is forced beyond its range; never chase a late response. Stop immediately for a hand tap, foot tap, verbal stop, or other distress. Remove the extension before opening the high legs.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

## Playing as Defender

[[Submissions/Armbar/from Dead Orchard Control/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Keep both elbows compact before the holder isolates a wrist.

- Protect the wrist before the elbow is aligned for the short armbar.

- Withdraw shoulder and elbow together only while the enclosure is open and the joint is unloaded.

- If the wrist isolation fails, recognize that the high both-arm triangle may still remain.

- If the triangle opens safely, recover posture inside the ordinary closed guard.


### Recognition Cues

<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Guide

**Injury Risks**:

| Injury | Severity | Recovery Time |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Elbow joint injury from excessive pressure | High | Variable; obtain qualified medical assessment if injured. |
| Neck or knee strain during a forced escape | High | Variable; do not train through symptoms. |


**Application Speed**: Apply gradually and release immediately at any signal.

**Tap Signals**:
- Hand tap
- Foot tap when the arms are trapped
- Verbal tap or stop


**Release Protocol**:
1. Stop adding pressure immediately.
2. Remove the joint or neck load before changing grips.
3. Free the trapped limb or neck and check the partner.


**Training Restrictions**:
- Practice under qualified supervision with a cooperative partner.
- Do not force the movement through pain or a restricted joint range.
