<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The short armbar from Dead Orchard uses the high triangle around both opposing arms to isolate a wrist and extend one elbow with controlled, immediately releasable pressure.

Begin underneath with Dead Orchard's high figure-four containing both opposing arms at the shoulder line. Control one captured wrist without letting the other arm or shoulder escape the established high triangle.

Maintain the elbow's relationship to the leg frame before attempting any extension. Adjust through a comfortable hip angle so the elbow extends along its natural line, with no wrist or shoulder twist. Take up slack gradually. The trained partner taps before the elbow is forced beyond its range; never chase a late response. Stop immediately for a hand tap, foot tap, verbal stop, or other distress. Remove the extension before opening the high legs.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Armbar from Dead Orchard Control?</p>

- Both arms inside the high triangle: Begin underneath with Dead Orchard's high figure-four containing both opposing arms at the shoulder line.
- One wrist within the enclosure: Control one captured wrist without letting the other arm or shoulder escape the established high triangle.
- Elbow retained against the leg frame: Maintain the elbow's relationship to the leg frame before attempting any extension.
- Wrist and elbow aligned for extension: Adjust through a comfortable hip angle so the elbow extends along its natural line, with no wrist or shoulder twist.
- Small elbow extension without jerking: Take up slack gradually. The trained partner taps before the elbow is forced beyond its range; never chase a late response.
- Remove extension and free the arm: Stop immediately for a hand tap, foot tap, verbal stop, or other distress. Remove the extension before opening the high legs.
