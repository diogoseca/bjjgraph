<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Keep both elbows compact before the holder isolates a wrist. Protect the wrist before the elbow is aligned for the short armbar. Withdraw shoulder and elbow together only while the enclosure is open and the joint is unloaded. If the wrist isolation fails, recognize that the high both-arm triangle may still remain. If the triangle opens safely, recover posture inside the ordinary closed guard.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Dead Orchard Control]] (Bottom)


</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Submission

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Armbar from Dead Orchard Control?</p>

- Begin underneath with Dead Orchard's high figure-four containing both opposing arms at the shoulder line.
- Control one captured wrist without letting the other arm or shoulder escape the established high triangle.
- Maintain the elbow's relationship to the leg frame before attempting any extension.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Armbar from Dead Orchard Control?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: If the triangle opens safely, recover posture inside the ordinary closed guard.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Protect the wrist before the elbow is aligned for the short armbar.
