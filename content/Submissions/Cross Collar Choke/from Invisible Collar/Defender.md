<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The attacker faces the opponent with access to both jacket collars and enough posture control to establish the grips. Invisible Collar names a high leg clinch, not a cloth grip. Releasing that clinch and arranging actual collar access is necessary; the reviewed primary lesson demonstrates mount rather than a direct Invisible Collar entry.

Fight collar access while the attacker has only a shallow connection. Keep an inside frame before the hands cross into a complete choke. Manage the distance before both collar grips are fixed; do not jerk backward against a loaded choke.

Work on a grip and neck space while the enclosure remains loose. Do not assume chin position prevents the collar from compressing the neck. Rebuild frames once both hands have opened and the cloth is slack.

The collar must be used as a controlled choking surface, never to grind the face. Release both grips promptly and do not test how long a partner can tolerate the choke.

Protect the neck by addressing the first deep grip and keeping an inside frame before the second arm crosses. If you are underneath a top attacker and the grips have opened, use framed hip movement and a short shrimp to restore knee space; an escape must first remove the collar tension.

## How to Recognize This Submission

- Place the forearms beside the neck without dragging across the jaw or bending the wrists painfully.
