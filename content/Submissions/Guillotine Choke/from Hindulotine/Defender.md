<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The attacker is in front of a lowered head, with the choking arm around the neck and a stable standing or guard connection. Hindu Control includes a raised leg in the enclosure. An ordinary guillotine requires deliberate re-gripping and leg clearance; a shared word in the names does not make the grips interchangeable.

Manage posture and inside hands before the choking arm circles the neck. Interrupt the hand connection before it becomes a closed enclosure. Do not wrench the head backward or rotate it against a fixed forearm.

Use frames and base before the attacker fixes the relationship between both torsos. A positional pass alone does not prove the choke has been removed. Move the head free only after the hand loop opens.

Use slow enclosure practice and avoid cervical extension or twisting. Open the hands and remove the neck restraint immediately on any tap.

The early escape targets the chin strap and supporting hand before they form a closed neck loop. Maintain an aligned head while making space; a positional advance around the legs does not release a captured neck.

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Guillotine Choke from Hindulotine?</p>

- Keep the head from being wrapped: Manage posture and inside hands before the choking arm circles the neck.
- Fight the support hand: Interrupt the hand connection before it becomes a closed enclosure.
- Protect neck alignment: Do not wrench the head backward or rotate it against a fixed forearm.
- Control the body angle early: Use frames and base before the attacker fixes the relationship between both torsos.
- Tap when the loop is secure: A positional pass alone does not prove the choke has been removed.
- Recover posture after release: Move the head free only after the hand loop opens.
