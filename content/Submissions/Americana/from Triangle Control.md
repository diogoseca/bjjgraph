<section id="overview" class="content-section">

Americana from Triangle Control is a distinct submission state: the bottom player retains the triangle entanglement while changing the active threat to the arm. Maintain the triangle entanglement and isolate the bent arm; only follow the external-rotation branch when the wrist and elbow position allow it. The original triangle choke remains a possible follow-up if the arm defense makes it available. The defender must protect the attacked arm while continuing to address the entanglement. The finish ends the exchange; establishing this arm attack does not itself imply a tap.

## Playing as Defender

[[Submissions/Americana/from Triangle Control/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Recognize the change from neck attack to arm attack.

- Protect the attacked joint before attempting positional recovery.

- Keep the neck protected while defending the arm.

- Recover posture only when the joint is protected.

- Tap immediately if you cannot defend safely.


### Recognition Cues


- The attacker redirects hand control to the wrist and elbow.

- The attacker aligns the arm for bent arm with external shoulder rotation.

- The leg entanglement remains in place during the arm attack.


### Escape Paths


- Protect the attacked arm, then recover posture and clear the leg entanglement.

- Extract the arm during a safe grip opening while continuing to defend the neck.

<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Guide

**Injury Risks**:

| Injury | Severity | Recovery Time |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Rotator cuff tear (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor) | High | Varies with injury severity and treatment; a clinician must assess an injury. |
| Shoulder capsule damage and labral tears | High | Varies with injury severity and treatment; a clinician must assess an injury. |
| AC joint separation or damage | Medium | Varies with injury severity and treatment; a clinician must assess an injury. |
| Glenohumeral joint dislocation | CRITICAL | Varies with injury severity and treatment; a clinician must assess an injury. |
| Biceps tendon strain or tear | Medium | Varies with injury severity and treatment; a clinician must assess an injury. |


**Application Speed**: Apply pressure slowly and progressively with a qualified instructor supervising practice. Never jerk or force the joint; release immediately at any tap, request to stop, or sign of distress.

**Tap Signals**:
- Any hand or foot tap
- A verbal tap or request to stop
- A cry of pain, distress, or loss of responsiveness; stop immediately


**Release Protocol**:
1. Immediately remove pressure from the arm and neck at any tap or sign of distress.
2. Release the arm grip and open the triangle entanglement without forcing the partner’s limb.
3. Stop the exchange and check on the partner before resuming practice.


**Training Restrictions**:
- Practice finishing mechanics under qualified supervision with a cooperative partner and minimal pressure.
- Tell the instructor and partner about prior arm, shoulder, or neck injuries before practice.
- Stop immediately if control is lost, pain occurs, or either partner wants to stop.
