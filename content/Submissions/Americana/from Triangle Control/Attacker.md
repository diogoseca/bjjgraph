<section id="overview" class="content-section">

Americana from Triangle Control is a distinct submission state: the bottom player retains the triangle entanglement while changing the active threat to the arm. Maintain the triangle entanglement and isolate the bent arm; only follow the external-rotation branch when the wrist and elbow position allow it. The original triangle choke remains a possible follow-up if the arm defense makes it available. The defender must protect the attacked arm while continuing to address the entanglement. The finish ends the exchange; establishing this arm attack does not itself imply a tap.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Triangle Control]] (Bottom)


</section>



<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Americana from Triangle Control?</p>

- Maintain the triangle entanglement and isolate the bent arm; only follow the external-rotation branch when the wrist and elbow position allow it.
- Keep the opponent’s posture controlled while changing the arm grip.
- Choose the arm attack that matches the available joint alignment.
- Maintain control before applying finishing pressure.
- Release immediately at a tap; the arm attack and choke must both be released.


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Americana from Triangle Control?</p>

- Established triangle entanglement from the bottom.
- Access to the arm being attacked.
- Control of the wrist and elbow appropriate to this attack.
- Enough posture control to adjust the grip without losing the entanglement.


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Americana from Triangle Control step by step?</p>

1. **Maintain the entanglement**: Keep control of the opponent’s posture as you change your attacking grip.
2. **Identify the available arm position**: Confirm that the arm position supports a bent arm with external shoulder rotation attack.
3. **Secure the wrist and elbow**: Maintain the triangle entanglement and isolate the bent arm; only follow the external-rotation branch when the wrist and elbow position allow it.
4. **Address the defensive grip**: If the opponent connects their hands, preserve control and address the connection before adding pressure.
5. **Apply controlled finishing pressure**: Increase pressure progressively only while the joint alignment and control remain established.
6. **Release or follow the defense**: Release immediately on a tap. If the opponent successfully protects the arm, reassess the triangle choke and other available branches.


</section>

<section id="common-counters" class="content-section">

## Opponent Defenses

<p class="section-subtitle">How might your opponent defend against Americana from Triangle Control?</p>

- **The opponent connects their hands to protect the arm** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Maintain the entanglement and address the grip or switch attacks.
- **The opponent recovers posture** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Address posture before continuing the arm finish.
- **The opponent extracts the attacked arm** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Reassess the neck attack and positional recovery.


</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Americana from Triangle Control?</p>

### 1. Abandoning posture control

- **Consequence**: The opponent can disrupt the entanglement
- **Correction**: Maintain the triangle while changing grips.

### 2. Forcing the wrong arm alignment

- **Consequence**: The intended joint lock is unavailable
- **Correction**: Switch to an attack matching the available arm position.

### 3. Pulling with the arms alone

- **Consequence**: Control and alignment deteriorate
- **Correction**: Use coordinated control of the wrist, elbow, and entanglement.

### 4. Treating entry as a completed finish

- **Consequence**: The defender still has meaningful responses
- **Correction**: Establish the attack, then choose and execute the finish.

### 5. Continuing after a tap

- **Consequence**: The partner can be injured
- **Correction**: Release both the arm attack and triangle immediately.



</section>
