<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The attacker is behind the opponent with a secure torso connection and an arm able to pass around the neck. Invisible Collar is a facing guard clinch. Reaching the back is a separate positional change; the high legs do not themselves establish rear torso access.

Connect to the torso from behind before reaching across the neck. Control the defending hands without bending individual fingers or scraping the face. Arrange the forearm and upper arm beside the neck while preserving head alignment.

Connect the arm loop with the support hand behind the head without levering the neck forward. Use connected arms and torso instead of pulling the head into a crank. Open the choking arm and supporting hand immediately on any tap or change in responsiveness.

Never squeeze through the jaw or continue after a tap. Agree on verbal signals when the hands are occupied, and open the whole arm loop immediately.

Keep the rear enclosure aligned around the neck. Pressure misplaced onto the windpipe or a tucked jaw is a reason to open and reset; dragging a trapped head sideways can turn the attempt into a neck crank rather than the intended choke.
