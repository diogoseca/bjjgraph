<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The attacker is behind the opponent with a secure torso connection and an arm able to pass around the neck. Invisible Collar is a facing guard clinch. Reaching the back is a separate positional change; the high legs do not themselves establish rear torso access.

Connect to the torso from behind before reaching across the neck. Control the defending hands without bending individual fingers or scraping the face. Arrange the forearm and upper arm beside the neck while preserving head alignment. Connect the arm loop with the support hand behind the head without levering the neck forward.

Never squeeze through the jaw or continue after a tap. Agree on verbal signals when the hands are occupied, and open the whole arm loop immediately.

Instructional sources: [Stephan Kesting — finishing the rear naked choke](https://www.grapplearts.com/how-to-finish-rear-naked-choke/); [Eddie Bravo — MTS 134 Invisible Collar in Austin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCyIwacRKu8).

## Playing as Attacker

### Key Principles


- Establish rear attachment: Connect to the torso from behind before reaching across the neck.

<section id="defender-summary" class="content-section">

## Playing as Defender

[[Submissions/Rear Naked Choke/from Invisible Collar/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Protect neck access: Keep the attacking wrist from crossing into a complete neck enclosure.

- Use both hands early: Hand fight before the supporting hand joins the choke.

- Manage rear attachment: Work on torso position only while maintaining protection against the choking arm.

- Keep the neck aligned: Do not rely on chin tucking or a violent head turn against the completed loop.

- Tap promptly: Stop before dizziness, visual changes or loss of normal awareness.

- Escape after release: Turn and recover facing position once the neck enclosure has opened.


### Recognition Cues


- The attacker is behind the opponent with a secure torso connection and an arm able to pass around the neck.

- Control the defending hands without bending individual fingers or scraping the face.

- Connect the arm loop with the support hand behind the head without levering the neck forward.


### Escape Paths


- Hand fight before the supporting hand joins the choke.

- Work on torso position only while maintaining protection against the choking arm.

<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Guide

**Injury Risks**:

| Injury | Severity | Recovery Time |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Neck or jaw injury from cranking pressure | High | Variable; stop training and seek appropriate clinical assessment after an injury or concerning symptoms. |
| Loss of consciousness or serious harm from continued neck compression | CRITICAL | Variable; stop training and seek appropriate clinical assessment after an injury or concerning symptoms. |


**Application Speed**: Never squeeze through the jaw or continue after a tap. Agree on verbal signals when the hands are occupied, and open the whole arm loop immediately. No fixed number of seconds makes a locked submission safe.

**Tap Signals**:
- Repeated hand taps anywhere the partner can feel them
- A clear verbal tap or stop, including when the hands are trapped
- Foot taps or any agreed stop signal; release immediately if normal responsiveness changes


**Release Protocol**:
1. Open the finishing grip immediately at the first signal; do not wait for repeated confirmation
2. Remove the leg, arm or body restraint without forcing the captured joint or neck through another movement
3. Check the partner can respond normally and allow a safe reset; end practice and obtain help if there is an injury or concerning symptom


**Training Restrictions**:
- Start with supervised, cooperative control-and-release drilling rather than a loaded finish
- Check the event's current rules for the specific age, experience and clothing division before live use
- Never squeeze through the jaw or continue after a tap. Agree on verbal signals when the hands are occupied, and open the whole arm loop immediately.
