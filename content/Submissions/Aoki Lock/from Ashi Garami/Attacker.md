<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The attacker is seated or on a hip facing the opponent's leg, with the opposing foot turned outward beside the armpit and the hip connection controlled. The foot enclosure creates rotation rather than a bent-knee calf compression. A foot offered on the hip during an ashi exchange can supply the capture, but the position name alone does not establish it.

Identify the turned-out foot without forcing it to rotate against resistance. Bring the instep under the upper arm and support the heel/Achilles region with the forearm. Secure the upper-body enclosure while leaving the foot unloaded during practice.

Use the legs and hip connection to limit free movement without twisting a planted knee. Recognize that a small body extension can create rotational ankle and knee pressure; do not search for a large finishing movement. Open the arms and leg connection on a tap, sudden turn, or loss of communication.

Use capture-and-release practice without finishing rotation. Pain is not a reliable warning margin for a secured rotational lock; neither partner should spin against a fixed foot.

A failed Aoki entry does not justify changing targets while the foot remains twisted. Open the first grip, identify the new foot orientation and leg control, and only then consider a separately established leg lock in a submission chain.

- Connect the hands: Secure the upper-body enclosure while leaving the foot unloaded during practice.
