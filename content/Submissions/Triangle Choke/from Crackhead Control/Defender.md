<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Keep an inside arm frame before the holder creates the one-arm-out enclosure. Prevent the high leg from closing over the shoulder while the connection is still loose. Maintain safe head and shoulder alignment while addressing the leg closure. If the triangle entry fails, recognize that the original high clinch may remain. When the legs release, recover posture inside the ordinary closed guard.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Rubber Guard/Crackhead Control]] (Bottom)


</section>

## How to Recognize This Submission

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Triangle Choke from Crackhead Control?</p>

- Start from the bottom player's reinforced high guard; do not assume the starting leg connection is already a triangle choke.
- Separate the arm configuration so the head and only one arm remain in the intended triangle enclosure.
- Bring the leg across the shoulder line while preserving a comfortable hip and knee angle.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Triangle Choke from Crackhead Control?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: When the legs release, recover posture inside the ordinary closed guard.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Prevent the high leg from closing over the shoulder while the connection is still loose.
