<section id="overview" class="content-section">

As the attacker, the Neck Crank from Twister Control is a control-first finish: you must own the opponent's torso through the truck and trapped leg before ever reaching for the head. The submission lives or dies on the foundation underneath it—boot pressure and the figure-four leg trap keep the spine rotated so that when you secure a chin strap or forehead frame, the head has nowhere to follow except into the crank. Because the cervical spine offers almost no warning before failure, every finishing rep is governed by slow, progressive pressure and instant release on the tap, making discipline as important as mechanics.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Twister Control]] (Top)


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Neck Crank?</p>

- Control precedes the crank—secure the leg trap and chest pressure before reaching for the head
- The rotation is built by the lower body; the head control simply harvests torque the twist already created
- Combine rotation and flexion rather than pulling the head straight back, which only strains muscle
- Boot pressure on the hip keeps the opponent from squaring up and aligning their spine to neutralize the lock
- Apply pressure slowly and progressively—the cervical spine gives minimal warning before catastrophic failure
- Keep the crank as one threat in a fork with the twister, calf slicer, and back take to force defensive dilemmas
- Release instantly and completely on any tap signal—never finish the rep on a neck submission


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Neck Crank?</p>

- Established Twister Control or truck with at least one of the opponent's legs trapped in a figure-four
- Constant chest-to-back pressure keeping the opponent flattened on their side and unable to square up
- Boot (foot) pressure against the hip generating spinal rotation through the opponent's torso
- Clear access to the head or jaw on the side toward which you intend to rotate and flex the neck
- Awareness of the ruleset—confirm neck cranks are legal before committing to the finish


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Neck Crank step by step?</p>

1. **Consolidate the leg trap and chest pressure**: Lock the figure-four on the trapped leg and drive your chest into the opponent's back so they cannot square their hips or align their spine, building the stable base the crank requires. (Timing: Before any head contact)
2. **Maximize spinal rotation with boot pressure**: Press your boot into the opponent's hip to twist their lower body away while their shoulders rotate toward you, pre-loading the cervical spine so the finishing grip has immediate leverage. (Timing: As control settles)
3. **Clear and thread for the head**: Reach your top arm over the far shoulder toward the chin or jawline, sliding under the head while keeping your elbow tight so the opponent cannot frame into the gap you create. (Timing: Once the torso is locked)
4. **Lock the chin strap grip**: Cup the jaw with your hand and connect your second hand behind the head with a gable or S-grip, forming a rigid frame around the skull that turns your whole upper body into the cranking lever. (Timing: Immediately after threading)
5. **Set the finishing angle**: Drive your shoulders and hips to widen the gap between the rotated torso and the head, aligning the lever so that small movements translate into large, controllable force on the cervical spine. (Timing: Before applying pressure)
6. **Initiate the crank slowly**: Pull the head toward your chest while combining rotation with forward flexion, increasing pressure gradually over several seconds and reading the opponent for the tap at every increment. (Timing: Slow and progressive)
7. **Add compression with the bridge**: Elevate your hips or arch slightly to feed additional, measured compression into the lock once the grip and angle are set, escalating only as fast as the opponent can recognize and respond. (Timing: Only if no tap yet)
8. **Release completely on the tap**: The instant any tap, verbal signal, or sign of distress appears, release all pressure, return the head to neutral alignment, and check on your partner before disengaging the entanglement. (Timing: Immediate)


</section>

<section id="common-counters" class="content-section">

## Opponent Defenses

<p class="section-subtitle">How might your opponent defend against Neck Crank?</p>

- **Aggressive chin tuck to deny the chin-strap thread** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Switch to the forehead-push crank, attacking flexion instead of rotation since that vector works regardless of jaw position → Leads to [[Positions/Twister Control]]
- **Two-on-one hand fight to peel the cranking arm** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Pin the wrist, re-thread, or abandon the crank and climb to traditional back control to keep the dominant position → Leads to [[Positions/Back Control]]
- **Granby roll toward you to unwind the spinal rotation** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Follow the roll and re-insert hooks or chase the back rather than fighting the momentum, accepting guard-top if the position resets → Leads to [[Positions/Open Guard]]
- **Bridging into you to flatten out and reduce torque** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Re-establish boot pressure and tighten the leg trap to restore rotation before re-attacking the head → Leads to [[Positions/Twister Control]]


</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Neck Crank?</p>

### 1. Applying the crank explosively or with a jerking motion

- **Consequence**: Severe risk of permanent cervical injury to your partner and likely disqualification under most rulesets
- **Correction**: Always increase pressure slowly and progressively, giving the defender time to recognize danger and tap

### 2. Reaching for the head before the leg trap and chest pressure are secured

- **Consequence**: The opponent squares their hips and escapes the position entirely during your reach
- **Correction**: Finish the foundation first—control the torso and trapped leg, then attack the head last

### 3. Pulling the head straight back without rotation or flexion

- **Consequence**: You strain the neck muscles without loading the spine, producing a low-percentage attempt the opponent rides out
- **Correction**: Combine rotation and flexion off the twist the lower body already created for true cervical leverage

### 4. Rising off the opponent's back and losing chest pressure

- **Consequence**: Space opens for the opponent to turn, breathe, and begin a roll or hip escape
- **Correction**: Stay heavy with constant chest-to-back connection throughout the entire finishing sequence

### 5. Fixating only on the neck crank and ignoring the fork

- **Consequence**: A committed defender stalls the single threat while you burn energy on an unavailable angle
- **Correction**: Chain the crank with the twister, calf slicer, and back take so defending one attack opens another

### 6. Letting the boot pressure lapse during the finish

- **Consequence**: Spinal rotation collapses, the opponent aligns their spine, and the lock loses all its leverage
- **Correction**: Maintain boot pressure on the hip the entire time—it is the engine of the rotation the crank depends on



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Neck Crank (Attacker)?</p>

**Phase 1: Mechanical familiarization** - Head control and lever geometry
Drill threading the chin strap and setting the finishing angle on a fully compliant partner, with zero pressure, until the grip and body alignment are automatic.

**Phase 2: Controlled pressure calibration** - Progressive application and tap timing
With a compliant partner, practice introducing slow, incremental pressure and releasing instantly on the tap, calibrating how little movement produces a response on the cervical spine.

**Phase 3: Positional sparring** - Securing the finish against resistance
From established Twister Control, work to establish the crank against a defending partner who is allowed to hand-fight and reduce rotation, but never apply fast finishing force.

**Phase 4: Live integration** - Chaining attacks under resistance
Integrate the crank into live rounds as part of the twister, calf slicer, and back-take fork, maintaining strict safety discipline and immediate release at all times.
