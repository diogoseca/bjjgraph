<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The attacker starts underneath in Hindu Control and must clear the raised leg to arrange an arm around the head and the opponent's arm across the neck. Hindu Control supplies a leg-in guillotine enclosure. An Arm Triangle requires a newly positioned head-and-arm loop; the high rubber-guard leg is not its defining choking surface.

Recover the arm before it crosses the neck and becomes enclosed. Maintain an early frame between the shoulder and head while the loop is still open. An upper arm beside the neck plus your own shoulder on the other side is the key threat.

Move the hips and shoulders together while space remains; do not turn the head alone. Stop before dizziness or impaired awareness instead of treating top position as a prerequisite for danger. Rebuild a safe arm position once the head-and-arm loop is open.

Use positional closure rather than a neck crank, practice the control without pressure, and release the shoulder and neck at any tap.

Protect the neck by keeping an early frame between the trapped shoulder and the closing arm. If that enclosure opens and the attacker remains on top, continue the escape with supported hip movement and a short shrimp to return a knee inside; do not bridge against a secured neck.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Hindulotine]] (Bottom)

## How to Recognize This Submission

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Arm Triangle from Hindulotine?</p>

- The attacker starts underneath in Hindu Control and must clear the raised leg to arrange an arm around the head and the opponent's arm across the neck.
- The attacker places the attacking upper arm beside one side of your neck without levering your head backward.
- The attacker closes the arm loop without changing your neck into a twisting position.


</section>

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Arm Triangle from Hindulotine?</p>

- Keep the elbow inside: Recover the arm before it crosses the neck and becomes enclosed.
- Preserve neck space: Maintain an early frame between the shoulder and head while the loop is still open.
- Track the attacking arm: An upper arm beside the neck plus your own shoulder on the other side is the key threat.
- Prevent the final angle: Move the hips and shoulders together while space remains; do not turn the head alone.
- Tap to a secured choke: Stop before dizziness or impaired awareness instead of treating top position as a prerequisite for danger.
- Restore frames after release: Rebuild a safe arm position once the head-and-arm loop is open.
