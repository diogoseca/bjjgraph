<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Keep the elbow close before the leg crosses into shoulder isolation. Control the hip distance while the holder opens the reinforcing leg. Clear the wrist before the shoulder is loaded by the new enclosure. If the isolation fails, the opponent may retain Crackhead's high clinch. If the high legs open, maintain a stable base inside the remaining closed guard.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Rubber Guard/Crackhead Control]] (Bottom)


</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Submission

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Omoplata from Crackhead Control?</p>

- Begin as the bottom holder of the high two-leg clinch; the shoulder must still be separately isolated.
- Open the reinforcing leg while retaining enough upper-body connection to organize the shoulder entry.
- Bring the leg into the omoplata shoulder enclosure while keeping the opponent's wrist and upper arm controlled.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Omoplata from Crackhead Control?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: If the high legs open, maintain a stable base inside the remaining closed guard.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Control the hip distance while the holder opens the reinforcing leg.
