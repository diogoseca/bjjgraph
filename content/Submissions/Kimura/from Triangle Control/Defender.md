<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The triangle entanglement remains in place while the attacker changes the active threat to the arm. Protect the joint first, keep the neck defended, and recover posture only after the immediate arm threat is addressed.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Triangle Control]] (Bottom)


</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Submission

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Kimura from Triangle Control?</p>

- The attacker redirects hand control to the wrist and elbow.
- The attacker aligns the arm for bent arm with internal shoulder rotation.
- The leg entanglement remains in place during the arm attack.


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Kimura from Triangle Control?</p>

- Recognize the change from neck attack to arm attack.
- Protect the attacked joint before attempting positional recovery.
- Keep the neck protected while defending the arm.
- Recover posture only when the joint is protected.
- Tap immediately if you cannot defend safely.


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Kimura from Triangle Control?</p>

### 1. Connect the hands to protect the arm

- **When to use**: Before the attacker separates and aligns the attacked arm
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Triangle Control]]
- **If successful**: The immediate arm finish is blocked while the triangle remains.
- **Risk**: The neck attack remains available.

### 2. Recover posture while keeping the arm protected

- **When to use**: After protecting the joint and creating space
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Closed Guard]]
- **If successful**: The triangle entanglement is cleared and you remain on top.
- **Risk**: Reaching or posturing without arm protection can expose the finish.

### 3. Extract the arm when the attacking grip loosens

- **When to use**: When a grip change creates an opening without loading the joint
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Triangle Control]]
- **If successful**: The arm attack is interrupted.
- **Risk**: The attacker can return to the neck attack.



</section>

<section id="escape-paths" class="content-section">

## Escape Paths

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you escape Kimura from Triangle Control?</p>

- Protect the attacked arm, then recover posture and clear the leg entanglement.
- Extract the arm during a safe grip opening while continuing to defend the neck.


</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Kimura from Triangle Control?</p>

### 1. Ignoring the arm attack while defending the choke

- **Consequence**: The attacker can finish the joint lock
- **Correction**: Protect the attacked joint while continuing to defend the neck.

### 2. Jerking the arm free against a fixed grip

- **Consequence**: Sudden movement can injure the joint
- **Correction**: Use controlled defensive movement; tap if the joint is trapped.

### 3. Recovering posture before protecting the arm

- **Consequence**: The arm may remain exposed as the body moves
- **Correction**: Protect the joint before attempting positional recovery.



</section>
