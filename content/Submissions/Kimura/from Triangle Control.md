<section id="overview" class="content-section">

Kimura from Triangle Control is a distinct submission state: the bottom player retains the triangle entanglement while changing the active threat to the arm. Keep the elbow bent and control the wrist while using the triangle entanglement to limit the opponent’s posture. The original triangle choke remains a possible follow-up if the arm defense makes it available. The defender must protect the attacked arm while continuing to address the entanglement. The finish ends the exchange; establishing this arm attack does not itself imply a tap.

## Playing as Attacker

[[Submissions/Kimura/from Triangle Control/Attacker|→ Full Attacker Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Keep the elbow bent and control the wrist while using the triangle entanglement to limit the opponent’s posture.

- Keep the opponent’s posture controlled while changing the arm grip.

- Choose the arm attack that matches the available joint alignment.

- Maintain control before applying finishing pressure.

- Release immediately at a tap; the arm attack and choke must both be released.

### Common Mistakes


- **Abandoning posture control**
  - Consequence: The opponent can disrupt the entanglement
  - Correction: Maintain the triangle while changing grips.

- **Forcing the wrong arm alignment**
  - Consequence: The intended joint lock is unavailable
  - Correction: Switch to an attack matching the available arm position.

- **Pulling with the arms alone**
  - Consequence: Control and alignment deteriorate
  - Correction: Use coordinated control of the wrist, elbow, and entanglement.

<section id="defender-summary" class="content-section">

## Playing as Defender

[[Submissions/Kimura/from Triangle Control/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Recognize the change from neck attack to arm attack.

- Protect the attacked joint before attempting positional recovery.

- Keep the neck protected while defending the arm.

- Recover posture only when the joint is protected.

- Tap immediately if you cannot defend safely.


### Recognition Cues


- The attacker redirects hand control to the wrist and elbow.

- The attacker aligns the arm for bent arm with internal shoulder rotation.

- The leg entanglement remains in place during the arm attack.


> Tap early and often. [Injury risks ↓](#safety-considerations)

### Escape Paths


- Protect the attacked arm, then recover posture and clear the leg entanglement.

- Extract the arm during a safe grip opening while continuing to defend the neck.
