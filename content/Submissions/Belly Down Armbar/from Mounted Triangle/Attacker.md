<section id="overview" class="content-section">

Attacking with the belly down armbar from mounted triangle requires mastering the rotational transition from an upright mounted triangle position to a prone finishing position while maintaining unbroken control of the trapped arm. The mounted triangle provides superior arm isolation compared to other belly down armbar setups because the triangle legs physically prevent elbow retraction during rotation. Success hinges on wrist control timing, hip placement during the roll, and progressive extension pressure once belly down. The attacker must read whether the defender is protecting the neck or the arm to select the optimal moment for the belly down transition.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Mount/Mounted Triangle]] (Top)


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Belly Down Armbar from Mounted Triangle?</p>

- Secure the wrist before initiating rotation - the entire technique depends on maintaining control of the trapped arm throughout the transition
- Use the triangle leg lock as a guide rail during rotation to prevent the defender from extracting the elbow
- Place your hips directly over the elbow joint once belly down to create the primary fulcrum for extension
- Keep your knees pinched together around the upper arm to prevent any lateral escape of the limb
- Apply extension through controlled hip pressure and wrist lift rather than explosive jerking motions
- Maintain heavy chest pressure on the mat to anchor your position and prevent the defender from rolling you
- Time the rotation when the defender commits to defending the triangle choke rather than protecting the arm


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Belly Down Armbar from Mounted Triangle?</p>

- Established mounted triangle with legs locked in figure-four configuration over one shoulder
- The defender's arm is trapped inside the triangle between your legs with clear access to the wrist
- Wrist control secured on the trapped arm using a two-on-one or C-grip before initiating any rotation
- The defender is actively defending the triangle choke, creating opportunity to redirect to the arm attack
- Your base is stable enough to initiate rotation without losing top position during the transition


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Belly Down Armbar from Mounted Triangle step by step?</p>

1. **Secure wrist control**: While maintaining the mounted triangle, grip the trapped arm's wrist with both hands using a C-grip or monkey grip. Pull the wrist toward your chest to straighten the arm slightly and confirm control before proceeding with any rotation. (Timing: 2-3 seconds to establish firm grip)
2. **Unlock triangle and control upper arm**: Uncross your ankles from the figure-four triangle configuration while keeping both legs clamped tightly around the upper arm and shoulder. Your legs transition from choking structure to arm isolation structure. Squeeze your knees together to maintain upper arm control during this critical phase. (Timing: 1-2 seconds for smooth unlock)
3. **Initiate rotation over the trapped arm**: Begin rotating your body over the trapped arm by leading with your head and shoulders toward the mat on the far side of the arm. Keep the wrist pulled tight to your chest throughout the rotation. Your legs guide and control the upper arm as you transition from upright to prone orientation. (Timing: 2-3 seconds for controlled rotation)
4. **Establish belly-down position with hip placement**: Complete the rotation until your chest and stomach are flat on the mat. Position your hips directly over the defender's elbow joint so that your pelvis creates the primary fulcrum for hyperextension. The arm should be sandwiched between your thighs with the thumb pointing upward toward the ceiling. (Timing: 1-2 seconds to settle hips)
5. **Pinch knees and anchor position**: Squeeze your knees tightly together around the upper arm just above the elbow to prevent any lateral movement or rotation of the limb. Press your chest into the mat to create a heavy base that prevents the defender from rolling or bridging you off. Post toes on the mat for additional stability. (Timing: 1 second to consolidate control)
6. **Apply progressive extension pressure**: Lift the wrist slowly upward toward the ceiling while driving your hips down into the elbow joint. The extension should be gradual and controlled, creating steady pressure that allows the defender time to recognize the submission and tap. Monitor continuously for any tap signal and release immediately upon recognition. (Timing: 3-5 seconds of progressive application)

<section id="common-counters" class="content-section">

## Opponent Defenses

<p class="section-subtitle">How might your opponent defend against Belly Down Armbar from Mounted Triangle?</p>

- **Defender retracts elbow and curls arm tight to body before rotation completes** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Abandon the belly down transition and return to mounted triangle. Re-establish the triangle choke threat to force the defender to address the neck, then reattempt the arm when they extend to defend the choke. → Leads to [[Positions/Mount/Mounted Triangle]]
- **Defender bridges explosively during the rotation phase to disrupt attacker's base** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Post your free hand on the mat during rotation to maintain base. If the bridge is strong enough to disrupt position, abort the belly down attempt and re-establish mounted triangle or transition to standard armbar control. → Leads to [[Positions/Mount/Mounted Triangle]]
- **Defender turns into the attacker and recovers guard during or after the rotation** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: If you lose hip position but still have wrist control, immediately transition to a standard armbar finish from guard or release and work to pass the guard back to a dominant position. → Leads to [[Positions/Closed Guard]]
- **Defender grips own wrist or interlocks hands to prevent arm extension once belly down** (Effectiveness: Low) - Your Response: Peel the grip by wedging your forearm between their clasped hands while maintaining hip pressure on the elbow. Alternatively, apply slow constant pressure through the grip, which typically forces separation within seconds. → Leads to [[Positions/Mount/Mounted Triangle]]

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Belly Down Armbar from Mounted Triangle?</p>

### 1. Initiating rotation before securing firm wrist control on the trapped arm

- **Consequence**: The defender retracts the arm during rotation, leaving the attacker in a compromised position without the submission or the mounted triangle
- **Correction**: Always confirm a strong C-grip or monkey grip on the wrist with both hands before beginning any rotational movement. Test the grip by pulling slightly before committing to the roll.

### 2. Placing hips too high on the bicep instead of directly over the elbow joint

- **Consequence**: Reduced mechanical advantage on the arm, allowing the defender to bend the elbow and resist extension, potentially escaping the submission entirely
- **Correction**: After completing the rotation, scoot your hips down until you feel the elbow crease directly under your pelvis. The arm should be controlled just above the elbow by your knees.

### 3. Rushing the rotation and losing leg control of the upper arm during transition

- **Consequence**: The defender extracts the arm during the gap in control, escaping the submission and potentially recovering guard
- **Correction**: Maintain constant knee squeeze throughout the entire rotation. Think of your legs as a vise around the upper arm that never opens, even momentarily, during the transition from mounted triangle to belly-down.

### 4. Failing to keep chest heavy on the mat once belly down

- **Consequence**: The defender bridges or rolls the attacker, creating space to extract the arm or reverse the position entirely
- **Correction**: Once prone, drive your sternum into the mat and spread your base with toes posted wide. Your body weight should feel like dead weight anchoring the position.

### 5. Jerking or spiking the arm extension rather than applying progressive pressure

- **Consequence**: Serious risk of elbow injury to training partner, potential ligament or joint damage before they can tap
- **Correction**: Always apply extension slowly and steadily. Lift the wrist gradually while sinking hips. Allow 3-5 seconds of progressive pressure so the defender has time to recognize and signal the tap.

### 6. Leaving the thumb rotated downward instead of ensuring it points toward the ceiling

- **Consequence**: Poor alignment reduces hyperextension pressure and allows the defender to rotate the forearm to relieve pressure on the elbow
- **Correction**: Ensure the defender's thumb points upward (supinated forearm) before applying extension. This aligns the elbow joint for maximum hyperextension vulnerability.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Belly Down Armbar from Mounted Triangle (Attacker)?</p>

**Phase 1: Static Mechanics** - Hip placement and arm positioning
Partner lies prone while you practice final belly-down position. Focus on placing hips directly over elbow, pinching knees on upper arm, and understanding proper wrist lift angle. No resistance. Repeat 20 reps per side.

**Phase 2: Rotation Drill** - Smooth transition from mounted triangle to belly-down
Start in mounted triangle and practice the full rotation to belly-down with a cooperative partner. Focus on maintaining wrist control and knee squeeze throughout the rotation. No finishing pressure. Build fluidity over 10 reps per side.

**Phase 3: Light Resistance** - Handling common defensive reactions during transition
Partner provides 50% resistance by attempting arm retraction and bridging during the rotation. Practice adjusting grip, maintaining control through disruption, and completing the transition despite defensive movement. Apply only light finishing pressure.

**Phase 4: Live Situational Sparring** - Full-speed application with progressive resistance
Begin in mounted triangle and attempt to finish the belly down armbar against progressively increasing resistance. Partner defends with full technique but controlled intensity. Practice reading when to commit versus returning to other mounted triangle attacks.
