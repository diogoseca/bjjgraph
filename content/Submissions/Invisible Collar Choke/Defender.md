<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Recognize the high enclosure before pressure develops. Protect head and shoulder alignment while an early frame is available. Do not force a neck twist to try to escape a loaded choke. Tap and wait for complete pressure release before changing direction. Confirm whether the second leg remains or the position has returned to Chill Dog.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Rubber Guard/Invisible Collar]] (Bottom)

## How to Recognize This Submission

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Invisible Collar Choke?</p>

- Keep both raised knees supported through the hip angle while maintaining the enclosure.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Invisible Collar Choke?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: Confirm whether the second leg remains or the position has returned to Chill Dog.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Protect head and shoulder alignment while an early frame is available.
