<section id="overview" class="content-section">

From Side Control, the D'Arce becomes available when the opponent's turn or frame opens a path under an armpit and around the neck. Thread the choking arm through that opening, connect a figure-four grip on your own upper arm, and bring the trapped shoulder toward the neck as you adjust the finishing angle.

Marce is a named D'Arce-family finish: move close to the opponent's hip and roll or dive onto the shoulder while keeping the head and arm contained. The choking structure remains a head-and-arm connection. It does not use a lapel as a ligature. It can be practiced in gi and no-gi, and is more commonly taught in no-gi.

The same family is taught from front headlock and half-guard exchanges. This entry's canonical start remains Side Control/Top; the alternative origins shown in companion lessons do not add graph origins or change this entry's outcome table.

## Playing as Attacker

[[Submissions/Darce Choke/from Side Control/Attacker|→ Full Attacker Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Secure the head-and-arm connection before adding pressure

- Thread under the armpit and around the neck without forcing the cervical spine

- Close the figure-four with your own upper arm and keep the elbows connected

- Retain the opponent's shoulder beside the neck as you change angle

- Use a controlled shoulder roll for the Marce finish while maintaining hip proximity

- Release pressure immediately when the partner taps or shows distress

### Common Mistakes


- **Threading without trapping the arm**
  - Consequence: The shoulder cannot complete the head-and-arm structure
  - Correction: Contain the arm beside the neck before closing the grip

- **Squeezing before closing the space**
  - Consequence: The arms fatigue while the opponent can recover posture
  - Correction: Adjust the shoulder and figure-four connection before adding pressure

- **Treating Marce as a lapel choke**
  - Consequence: The grip sequence no longer matches the named variation
  - Correction: Use the D'Arce arm thread and shoulder-roll finish in either ruleset

<section id="defender-summary" class="content-section">

## Playing as Defender

[[Submissions/Darce Choke/from Side Control/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Recognize the under-armpit thread before the figure-four closes

- Preserve space around the trapped shoulder and neck

- Address the connecting grip before attempting a large positional movement

- Follow the changing angle without forcing the neck against the grip

- Recover guard or frames only after the choke connection is disrupted

- Tap early when the structure is secured and pressure increases


### Recognition Cues


- An arm travels beneath your armpit toward the far side of your neck

- The attacker reaches toward their own upper arm to close a figure-four

- Your trapped shoulder is drawn toward the side of your neck

- The attacker brings their hips close and drops toward a shoulder-roll angle


### Escape Paths


- Deny the early thread or extract the trapped arm, then rebuild Side Control frames

- Disrupt the grip before the roll and recover guard or the top side as the actual exchange permits

<section id="variations" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Variations


**Side Control D'Arce**: Use the opening made by a turn or frame to thread under the armpit and around the neck, connect the figure-four, and adjust your angle while containing the head and arm. (When to use: A Side Control exchange exposes the armpit-to-neck channel)


**Marce Choke (Rolling D'Arce)**: Keep the D'Arce head-and-arm connection, move hip to hip, and roll onto the shoulder to finish with the grip close to your torso and the elbows closing. (When to use: The shoulder-roll angle is available without losing the trapped arm; usable in gi and more common in no-gi)


**Turn-In Entry**: Follow an opponent turning toward you while keeping their shoulder and head contained, then thread the choking arm through the available opening. (When to use: The opponent turns toward the passer during Side Control recovery)

<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Guide

**Injury Risks**:

| Injury | Severity | Recovery Time |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Loss of consciousness from neck compression | CRITICAL | Stop training; obtain medical assessment if consciousness is lost |
| Neck or shoulder strain from forced rotation | High | Varies with injury; persistent pain requires assessment |


**Application Speed**: Slow, progressive pressure with a clear opportunity to tap

**Tap Signals**:
- Physical hand tap
- Verbal tap or stop
- Foot tap
- Any distress or loss of responsiveness


**Release Protocol**:
1. Open the figure-four immediately on a tap or distress
2. Release neck and shoulder pressure before changing position
3. Stop the drill and check the partner before continuing


**Training Restrictions**:
- Practice the shoulder roll cooperatively before adding resistance
- Do not wrench the neck or force a roll through a trapped shoulder
- Keep a clear tap signal available throughout the movement
