<section id="overview" class="content-section">

Contain the opponent's head and arm before committing to the finish. The choking arm travels under the armpit and around the neck; your other arm closes the figure-four. Keep the trapped shoulder connected as you change angle. For the Marce variation, bring the hips close and roll onto the shoulder without letting the arm escape.

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Darce Choke from Side Control?</p>

- Secure the head-and-arm connection before adding pressure
- Thread under the armpit and around the neck without forcing the cervical spine
- Close the figure-four with your own upper arm and keep the elbows connected
- Retain the opponent's shoulder beside the neck as you change angle
- Use a controlled shoulder roll for the Marce finish while maintaining hip proximity
- Release pressure immediately when the partner taps or shows distress


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Darce Choke from Side Control?</p>

- Side Control/Top with access to the opponent's head and near upper body
- An opening under the armpit and around the neck
- The opponent's arm contained beside their head rather than free to withdraw
- Enough room to adjust the finishing angle without forcing a trapped joint


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Darce Choke from Side Control step by step?</p>

1. **Recognize the opening**: Follow the opponent's turn or frame from Side Control while keeping the head and shoulder within reach.
2. **Thread the choking arm**: Pass the arm under the armpit and around the neck, keeping the opponent's arm inside the head-and-arm loop.
3. **Connect the figure-four**: Reach your choking hand to your own opposite upper arm and place the supporting hand to keep the structure closed.
4. **Contain the trapped shoulder**: Bring the opponent's shoulder toward the side of the neck while closing unnecessary space in the loop.
5. **Choose the finishing angle**: Maintain the connection as you adjust your body angle. For Marce, move hip to hip and roll onto the shoulder without pulling the neck through the turn.
6. **Apply and release progressively**: Close the elbows and add gradual pressure through the connected head-and-arm structure. Open the grip immediately on the partner's tap or distress.


</section>

<section id="common-counters" class="content-section">

## Opponent Defenses

<p class="section-subtitle">How might your opponent defend against Darce Choke from Side Control?</p>

- **Opponent extracts the trapped arm before the grip closes** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Recover Side Control and rebuild the opening without chasing an empty neck grip → Leads to [[Positions/Side Control]]
- **Opponent frames and creates space during the angle change** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Follow the shoulder connection or release the attempt and stabilize Side Control → Leads to [[Positions/Side Control]]
- **Opponent disrupts the roll and reverses the exchange** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Release the neck as the connection fails and establish defensive frames from the resulting guard position → Leads to [[Positions/Half Guard]]


</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Darce Choke from Side Control?</p>

### 1. Threading without trapping the arm

- **Consequence**: The shoulder cannot complete the head-and-arm structure
- **Correction**: Contain the arm beside the neck before closing the grip

### 2. Squeezing before closing the space

- **Consequence**: The arms fatigue while the opponent can recover posture
- **Correction**: Adjust the shoulder and figure-four connection before adding pressure

### 3. Treating Marce as a lapel choke

- **Consequence**: The grip sequence no longer matches the named variation
- **Correction**: Use the D'Arce arm thread and shoulder-roll finish in either ruleset

### 4. Rolling while the hip connection is loose

- **Consequence**: The opponent can withdraw the trapped arm or reverse the exchange
- **Correction**: Bring the hips close and preserve the loop through a slow roll

### 5. Forcing the head through the finishing angle

- **Consequence**: The action loads the neck instead of improving the choke structure
- **Correction**: Reduce pressure, correct the angle, and reset if the connection is unavailable



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Darce Choke from Side Control (Attacker)?</p>

**Phase 1: Threading** - Head-and-arm identification
With a cooperative partner, find the armpit-to-neck channel from Side Control and close the figure-four without pressure.

**Phase 2: Connection** - Shoulder containment
Practice preserving the trapped shoulder as the partner slowly changes their frame and posture; reset when the arm escapes.

**Phase 3: Marce Roll** - Controlled shoulder movement
Move hip to hip and rehearse the shoulder roll slowly with no finishing pressure, keeping the neck and shoulder free of forced rotation.

**Phase 4: Choice and Release** - Angle selection and tap response
Alternate the standard angle and Marce option against light positional resistance, adding pressure only after agreement and releasing on the first signal.
