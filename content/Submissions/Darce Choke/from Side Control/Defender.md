<section id="overview" class="content-section">

Respond as the arm threads under your armpit toward the neck. Keep room around the trapped shoulder and address the connecting grip before the attacker changes angle. A shoulder roll does not make a secured choke harmless; use the tap if pressure closes before you can restore space.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Side Control]] (Top)


</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Submission

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Darce Choke from Side Control?</p>

- An arm travels beneath your armpit toward the far side of your neck
- The attacker reaches toward their own upper arm to close a figure-four
- Your trapped shoulder is drawn toward the side of your neck
- The attacker brings their hips close and drops toward a shoulder-roll angle


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Darce Choke from Side Control?</p>

- Recognize the under-armpit thread before the figure-four closes
- Preserve space around the trapped shoulder and neck
- Address the connecting grip before attempting a large positional movement
- Follow the changing angle without forcing the neck against the grip
- Recover guard or frames only after the choke connection is disrupted
- Tap early when the structure is secured and pressure increases


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Darce Choke from Side Control?</p>

### 1. Block the threading arm and recover space around your shoulder

- **When to use**: Before the figure-four connection is complete
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Side Control]]
- **If successful**: The choke attempt ends while the attacker remains in Side Control/Top
- **Risk**: Waiting until the shoulder is compressed leaves less room to move

### 2. Address the connecting grip while recovering your trapped arm

- **When to use**: The attacker is closing the grip but the arm can still be withdrawn
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Side Control]]
- **If successful**: The head-and-arm loop opens and positional defense can resume
- **Risk**: Pulling the neck against an already secured loop can worsen pressure

### 3. Use the disrupted rolling exchange to come on top after freeing the neck

- **When to use**: The Marce roll loses its head-and-arm connection
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **If successful**: The attacker lands in Half Guard/Bottom and you recover the top side
- **Risk**: Attempting a reversal before the neck is free can tighten the choke



</section>

<section id="escape-paths" class="content-section">

## Escape Paths

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you escape Darce Choke from Side Control?</p>

- Deny the early thread or extract the trapped arm, then rebuild Side Control frames
- Disrupt the grip before the roll and recover guard or the top side as the actual exchange permits


</section>

<section id="favorable-outcomes" class="content-section">

## Best-Case Outcomes for Defender

<p class="section-subtitle">What is the best outcome when defending Darce Choke from Side Control?</p>

### → [[Positions/Side Control]]

Remove the submission connection; this table records the attacker's remaining top position, after which normal Side Control defense resumes

### → [[Positions/Half Guard]]

Free the head-and-arm connection during a failed roll and reverse the exchange, leaving the attacker on the bottom side of Half Guard



</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Darce Choke from Side Control?</p>

### 1. Ignoring the arm thread while trying to bridge

- **Consequence**: The attacker can close the figure-four around the moving head and shoulder
- **Correction**: Address the choke connection before continuing the positional escape

### 2. Assuming a rolling finish requires a lapel

- **Consequence**: The defender misses the Marce head-and-arm threat in either ruleset
- **Correction**: Recognize the D'Arce grip and shoulder-roll angle

### 3. Forcing a turn after pressure has closed

- **Consequence**: The trapped head and shoulder can take more load
- **Correction**: Tap when the structure is secured rather than forcing movement through it



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train defense against Darce Choke from Side Control?</p>

**Phase 1: Recognition** - Early thread cues
The partner alternates ordinary Side Control grips with a slow D'Arce thread; identify the attempt before the figure-four closes.

**Phase 2: Space Recovery** - Arm and grip defense
Use a cooperative open grip to rehearse preserving shoulder space and withdrawing the trapped arm, resetting before pressure is applied.

**Phase 3: Rolling Exchange** - Marce recognition and release
Rehearse a slow shoulder roll without squeeze, identifying when the connection remains intact and when it breaks; practice a clear tap and immediate release.
