<section id="overview" class="content-section">

You oppose the performer of this action. Recognize the upper-arm clamp before the wrist is fixed. Keep the elbow near the body while the shoulder connection is still forming. Clear the wrist before trying to rotate the shoulder through the enclosure. If the finish is interrupted, do not assume the Carni shoulder control has also disappeared. When the holder releases safely, restore posture in the facing closed guard.

Practice cooperatively with controlled movement. Stop at any tap, verbal signal, or distress; release all joint or neck pressure before changing direction. Do not force a trapped knee, shoulder, or neck to preserve the route.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Rubber Guard/Carni]] (Bottom)


</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Submission

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Carni Shoulder Lock?</p>

- Begin in Carni/Bottom with the opposing shoulder and upper arm controlled; this is an upper-body lock.
- Keep control of the triceps so the opponent cannot freely withdraw the upper arm.
- Secure the wrist while keeping the shoulder and elbow within a comfortable, controlled range.

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Carni Shoulder Lock?</p>

### 1. Acting after the joint or neck is already loaded

- **Consequence**: The attempted movement may increase pressure.
- **Correction**: Tap and release before moving.

### 2. Assuming a cleared grip completes the escape

- **Consequence**: The remaining control can still prevent the intended landing.
- **Correction**: When the holder releases safely, restore posture in the facing closed guard.

### 3. Losing a stable base during the response

- **Consequence**: You may concede the next connection.
- **Correction**: Keep the elbow near the body while the shoulder connection is still forming.
