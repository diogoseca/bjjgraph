<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The attacker controls the head and one arm from in front of the opponent, with room to establish the arm enclosure and a safe rolling angle. Dead Orchard is a bottom-player high leg triangle containing both arms. Its leg enclosure does not supply an Anaconda grip; a separate front-headlock exchange must occur first.

Contain the opponent's head and one arm while keeping a balanced base. Pass the choking arm beneath the neck and toward the far armpit, enclosing one shoulder. Join to the supporting arm without pulling the neck into a twist. Use the taught shoulder-led angle only when both partners can roll safely; do not dive onto the neck.

Introduce the arm loop without pressure and rehearse any roll cooperatively. Never drive onto the cervical spine or continue squeezing after a tap.

Instructional sources: [Stephan Kesting and Denis Kang — the Anaconda choke](https://www.grapplearts.com/the-anaconda-choke/); [Nathan Orchard — Dead Orchard, part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XoiB1D2WJwE).

## Playing as Attacker

[[Submissions/Anaconda Choke/from Dead Orchard/Attacker|→ Full Attacker Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Establish front headlock: Contain the opponent's head and one arm while keeping a balanced base.

- Thread under the neck: Pass the choking arm beneath the neck and toward the far armpit, enclosing one shoulder.

- Connect the arm loop: Join to the supporting arm without pulling the neck into a twist.

- Choose a controlled angle: Use the taught shoulder-led angle only when both partners can roll safely; do not dive onto the neck.

- Close the shoulder gap: Bring the trapped shoulder toward the neck with gradual whole-body connection rather than a violent head pull.

- Release the loop: Open both arms immediately on a tap or any loss of normal responsiveness.

<section id="defender-summary" class="content-section">

## Playing as Defender

[[Submissions/Anaconda Choke/from Dead Orchard/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Recognize the thread: An arm passing beneath the neck toward the armpit signals the Anaconda enclosure.

- Keep an early frame: Preserve space between the shoulder and neck before the attacker closes the arm loop.

- Protect head alignment: Do not fight a secured loop by twisting the neck or planting the head against a roll.

- Address the grip: Hand fight before the enclosure becomes tight, while keeping the trapped elbow connected to the torso.

- Tap before symptoms: Stop if the neck enclosure is secure rather than relying on a last-second roll.

- Rebuild base after release: Recover posture and guard or turtle only when the head and shoulder can move freely.


### Recognition Cues


- The attacker controls the head and one arm from in front of the opponent, with room to establish the arm enclosure and a safe rolling angle.

- Pass the choking arm beneath the neck and toward the far armpit, enclosing one shoulder.

- Use the taught shoulder-led angle only when both partners can roll safely; do not dive onto the neck.


### Escape Paths


- Preserve space between the shoulder and neck before the attacker closes the arm loop.

- Do not fight a secured loop by twisting the neck or planting the head against a roll.

<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Guide

**Injury Risks**:

| Injury | Severity | Recovery Time |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Neck injury during a forced roll or head twist | High | Variable; stop training and seek appropriate clinical assessment after an injury or concerning symptoms. |
| Loss of consciousness or serious harm from a sustained choke | CRITICAL | Variable; stop training and seek appropriate clinical assessment after an injury or concerning symptoms. |


**Application Speed**: Introduce the arm loop without pressure and rehearse any roll cooperatively. Never drive onto the cervical spine or continue squeezing after a tap. No fixed number of seconds makes a locked submission safe.

**Tap Signals**:
- Repeated hand taps anywhere the partner can feel them
- A clear verbal tap or stop, including when the hands are trapped
- Foot taps or any agreed stop signal; release immediately if normal responsiveness changes


**Release Protocol**:
1. Open the finishing grip immediately at the first signal; do not wait for repeated confirmation
2. Remove the leg, arm or body restraint without forcing the captured joint or neck through another movement
3. Check the partner can respond normally and allow a safe reset; end practice and obtain help if there is an injury or concerning symptom


**Training Restrictions**:
- Start with supervised, cooperative control-and-release drilling rather than a loaded finish
- Check the event's current rules for the specific age, experience and clothing division before live use
- Introduce the arm loop without pressure and rehearse any roll cooperatively. Never drive onto the cervical spine or continue squeezing after a tap.
