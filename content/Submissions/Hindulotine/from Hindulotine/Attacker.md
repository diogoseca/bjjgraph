<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The bottom guard player keeps a raised knee near the torso and loops an elbow over the foot, bringing the hand around the opponent's shoulder. Hindu Control is the setup; Hindulotine is the choke. Ben Eddy's early reverse-gable demonstration and later S-grip explanation are teaching variants, not a license to force any hand grip through a closed gap.

Turn onto the hip and keep the knee close so the foot is reached through body position. Bring the elbow above the raised foot and the hand around the shoulder into the head-control space. Retain the leg-and-arm enclosure without adding neck pressure while locating the second hand.

Use the demonstrated S-grip or the earlier reverse-gable variation without wrenching the wrist or knee. Lift the connected elbows only enough to close space; stop immediately if the pressure becomes a neck twist. Release the whole enclosure at the first tap or change in responsiveness, including if the opponent has passed the lower leg.

Drill the arm path with no choke pressure first. Protect the high knee, avoid cervical twisting, and release both the grip and raised leg immediately on any stop signal.

This neck enclosure can load the sides of the neck as a vascular choke, and misplaced forearm pressure can also compress the windpipe. Neither pressure type is a training target: rehearse the grip without squeezing, and release before the partner needs to tolerate throat or neck pressure.

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Hindulotine from Hindulotine?</p>

- Support the high knee: Turn onto the hip and keep the knee close so the foot is reached through body position.
- Loop over the foot: Bring the elbow above the raised foot and the hand around the shoulder into the head-control space.
- Establish Hindu Control: Retain the leg-and-arm enclosure without adding neck pressure while locating the second hand.
- Connect the finishing grip: Use the demonstrated S-grip or the earlier reverse-gable variation without wrenching the wrist or knee.
- Remove slack gradually: Lift the connected elbows only enough to close space; stop immediately if the pressure becomes a neck twist.
- Open hands and leg: Release the whole enclosure at the first tap or change in responsiveness, including if the opponent has passed the lower leg.
