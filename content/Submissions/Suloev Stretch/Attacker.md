<section id="overview" class="content-section">

As the attacker you are converting leg control into a finishing knee compression, and the entire submission lives or dies on the quality of your figure-four and the angle of your hips. Your job is to trap the opponent's lower leg so their heel is pinned against their own backside, then use bodyweight and hip extension — not arm strength — to fold the calf into the hamstring and load the knee joint. Because the finishing structures are dense and the tap arrives suddenly, you must win the positional battle first: kill their ability to straighten the leg or rotate their hip away, and only then apply pressure. Rushing the finish before the leg is isolated lets the opponent kick free and reverse the entanglement; patient, layered control followed by slow extension is what produces the consistent tap.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Half Guard]] (Top)


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Suloev Stretch?</p>

- Isolate the leg before you compress — the heel must be trapped against the opponent's own buttock with no path to straighten the knee before you apply any finishing pressure.
- Generate the finish from hip extension and bodyweight, never from pulling with the arms; the arms only maintain the figure-four while your hips drive the compression.
- Control the opponent's hip and far side so they cannot rotate away or invert to relieve the angle — a mobile hip defeats the submission.
- Keep the knee line and the compression vector aligned; the heel should travel toward the buttock and slightly toward the centerline to stack calf against hamstring.
- Stay chest-heavy and connected so the opponent cannot create the space needed to kick the leg free or scramble to a counter-entanglement.
- Apply pressure progressively and read the tap early — the dense knee and calf structures fail fast, so smooth incremental loading replaces any explosive motion.


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Suloev Stretch?</p>

- Top control over one of the opponent's legs, typically from half guard top, a stalled knee-shield pass, or a turtle leg-ride.
- A secured figure-four (or equivalent ankle/foot trap) folding the opponent's lower leg so the heel points toward their own buttock.
- Control of the opponent's hip on the attacking side to prevent them rotating away from the compression.
- Your bodyweight stacked over the trapped leg so hip extension translates directly into compression rather than the opponent absorbing it as slack.
- Awareness of the opponent's far-side framing and free leg so you can shut down the kick-out and reversal before finishing.


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Suloev Stretch step by step?</p>

1. **Establish leg control**: From top half guard or a stalled pass, pin and isolate the opponent's near leg, clearing their framing so their lower leg is exposed and you can begin folding it. (Timing: Entry)
2. **Fold the lower leg and trap the foot**: Bend the opponent's knee and bring their heel toward their own buttock, trapping the foot with a figure-four of your legs or by clamping the instep against your hip so the knee cannot straighten. (Timing: Control phase)
3. **Kill the hip**: Pin or hook the opponent's hip on the attacking side so they cannot rotate away or invert; without hip mobility the opponent loses the primary escape and the compression angle stays intact. (Timing: Control phase)
4. **Stack your weight over the trapped leg**: Drop your chest and shift your bodyweight directly over the folded leg so that any hip extension you produce loads straight into the calf and knee rather than feeding slack into the entanglement. (Timing: Pre-finish)
5. **Drive the heel toward the buttock**: Using your hips and bodyweight, press the trapped heel down and toward the opponent's backside, beginning to crush the calf muscle into the hamstring while the bent knee absorbs the compressive load. (Timing: Finish)
6. **Extend the hips to finish**: Slowly extend your hips and lever the compression on, increasing the fold of the knee and the calf-to-hamstring crush in a smooth progressive motion until the opponent taps. (Timing: Finish)
7. **Read the tap and release**: Watch for verbal or physical taps and any sudden pain reaction; the moment a tap registers, immediately stop hip extension and let the leg straighten naturally without forcing. (Timing: Release)


</section>

<section id="common-counters" class="content-section">

## Opponent Defenses

<p class="section-subtitle">How might your opponent defend against Suloev Stretch?</p>

- **Opponent straightens the trapped leg before the figure-four is locked** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Re-fold the knee immediately and clamp the instep; if the leg is fully extended, switch to a kneebar or ankle attack rather than forcing a failed compression. → Leads to [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **Opponent rotates their hip away from the compression to unload the angle** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Pin the hip and follow the rotation with your chest, re-stacking your weight so the heel-to-buttock vector is restored before re-applying pressure. → Leads to [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **Opponent kicks the free leg through and reverses into a counter-entanglement** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Maintain inside position and hand-fight for your own leg control; if you are losing the entanglement, retreat to top half guard rather than chasing the lost leg. → Leads to [[Positions/Half Guard]]
- **Opponent grips your near leg and bridges to create scrambling space** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Re-establish chest pressure and re-pin the hip, killing the bridge before it generates the space needed to free the trapped leg. → Leads to [[Positions/Half Guard]]


</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Suloev Stretch?</p>

### 1. Applying compression before the leg is fully isolated

- **Consequence**: The opponent straightens the knee and kicks free, often reversing into a dominant leg entanglement
- **Correction**: Confirm the heel is trapped against the buttock and the knee cannot straighten before adding any finishing pressure

### 2. Pulling with the arms instead of driving with the hips

- **Consequence**: The finish stalls and your arms fatigue while the opponent works their escape
- **Correction**: Use the figure-four only to maintain the trap and generate all compression from hip extension and bodyweight

### 3. Letting the opponent's hip stay mobile

- **Consequence**: The opponent rotates away, unloads the compression angle, and recovers the leg or scrambles to a counter
- **Correction**: Pin or hook the attacking-side hip and follow any rotation with your chest before re-applying pressure

### 4. Floating your weight off the trapped leg

- **Consequence**: Hip extension feeds slack into the entanglement rather than compression, and the submission produces no finishing pressure
- **Correction**: Stay chest-heavy and stacked directly over the folded leg so every degree of hip extension loads the knee and calf

### 5. Cranking explosively for a fast tap

- **Consequence**: Dense knee and calf structures can be injured before the opponent can physically tap, creating serious training and competition risk
- **Correction**: Apply slow, progressive pressure and read the tap early; the compression finishes reliably without any sudden or jerking motion

### 6. Ignoring the opponent's free leg and far-side frames

- **Consequence**: The opponent bridges or frames into space, kicks the free leg through, and reverses the position
- **Correction**: Monitor and control the free leg and far-side frames so the opponent cannot generate the space needed to escape

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Suloev Stretch (Attacker)?</p>

**Phase 1: Isolation drilling** - Trapping and folding the leg
With a compliant partner, drill entering from top half guard and folding the lower leg into the figure-four trap, confirming the heel is pinned to the buttock without applying compression.

**Phase 2: Positional control** - Killing the hip and stacking weight
Add the hip pin and weight stack against light resistance, focusing on preventing your partner from rotating away or straightening the leg while you maintain the trap.

**Phase 3: Slow finish calibration** - Progressive compression and tap recognition
Introduce extremely slow finishing pressure with constant communication, learning to feel the compression threshold and releasing immediately on the tap to keep the drill safe.

**Phase 4: Live entry sparring** - Catching the submission in scrambles
From positional sparring in half guard top and leg-ride situations, hunt the entry against full resistance while keeping all finishes controlled and progressive.

**Phase 5: Chaining and competition application** - Integrating counters and follow-ups
Drill flowing between the Suloev Stretch and kneebars, ankle locks, and heel hooks when the primary attack is defended, building a connected leg-attack system for live rounds.
