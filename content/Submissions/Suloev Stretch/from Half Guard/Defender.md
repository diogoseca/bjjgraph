<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The attacker is attached behind an opponent who has raised the hips into a tripod, with torso control and access to one ankle. Half guard is not the demonstrated finishing configuration. Sterling and Gonzalez distinguish the straight-leg stretch from sideways knee torque; obtaining the back is a separate prerequisite.

A hand reaching from your back toward the ankle is an early cue to protect the leg. Use hand fighting before the ankle and torso are controlled together. Maintain a stable base and address the rear attachment before raising the hips with a leg available.

Keep the captured leg aligned instead of rolling sideways against the ankle grip. Stop before a forced hamstring stretch or knee rotation develops. Recover the base only after the attacker has opened the ankle and torso controls.

Use a cooperative entry and stop at the aligned capture. Never drop onto the stretch, bounce the leg, or roll sideways while the ankle is fixed.

The early escape denies the rear hand access to the ankle and reduces the exposed tripod while the leg is still free. If the ankle and torso are already captured together, tap and let both controls open before rebuilding your base.

Before an ankle is captured, rebuilding base brings the elbows beside the ribs and the knees back beneath the body. Keep that elbow-knee connection while checking the rear attacker's hands: rising into a tripod still exposes the ankle, so the base change alone does not remove the submission threat.

## How to Recognize This Submission

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Suloev Stretch from Half Guard?</p>

- The attacker is attached behind an opponent who has raised the hips into a tripod, with torso control and access to one ankle.
- Your raised hips expose an ankle that the rear attacker can reach while retaining balance.
- The attacker arranges your leg so it extends in its natural line rather than rotating sideways at the knee.
