<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The attacker is attached behind an opponent who has raised the hips into a tripod, with torso control and access to one ankle. Half guard is not the demonstrated finishing configuration. Sterling and Gonzalez distinguish the straight-leg stretch from sideways knee torque; obtaining the back is a separate prerequisite.

Control the torso from behind before reaching below the hips. Work only when the opponent's raised hips expose an ankle without sacrificing your balance. Reach down with the opposite hand and secure the lower leg while maintaining rear torso control. Arrange the leg so it extends in its natural line rather than rotating sideways at the knee.

Use a cooperative entry and stop at the aligned capture. Never drop onto the stretch, bounce the leg, or roll sideways while the ankle is fixed.

Instructional sources: [Aljamain Sterling and James Gonzalez — UFC 228 submission breakdown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iHhOQwl1src); [ADCC — competition rules](https://adcombat.com/adcc-rules-regulations/).

## Playing as Defender

[[Submissions/Suloev Stretch/from Half Guard/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Recognize ankle access: A hand reaching from your back toward the ankle is an early cue to protect the leg.

- Deny the grip early: Use hand fighting before the ankle and torso are controlled together.

- Avoid an exposed tripod: Maintain a stable base and address the rear attachment before raising the hips with a leg available.

- Do not twist away: Keep the captured leg aligned instead of rolling sideways against the ankle grip.

- Tap to the capture: Stop before a forced hamstring stretch or knee rotation develops.

- Move after the release: Recover the base only after the attacker has opened the ankle and torso controls.


### Recognition Cues


- The attacker is attached behind an opponent who has raised the hips into a tripod, with torso control and access to one ankle.

- Work only when the opponent's raised hips expose an ankle without sacrificing your balance.

- Arrange the leg so it extends in its natural line rather than rotating sideways at the knee.


### Escape Paths


- Use hand fighting before the ankle and torso are controlled together.

- Maintain a stable base and address the rear attachment before raising the hips with a leg available.

## Safety Guide

**Injury Risks**:

| Injury | Severity | Recovery Time |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Hamstring or tendon injury from forced extension | High | Variable; stop training and seek appropriate clinical assessment after an injury or concerning symptoms. |
| Knee injury from sideways rotation of a captured leg | High | Variable; stop training and seek appropriate clinical assessment after an injury or concerning symptoms. |


**Application Speed**: Use a cooperative entry and stop at the aligned capture. Never drop onto the stretch, bounce the leg, or roll sideways while the ankle is fixed. No fixed number of seconds makes a locked submission safe.

**Tap Signals**:
- Repeated hand taps anywhere the partner can feel them
- A clear verbal tap or stop, including when the hands are trapped
- Foot taps or any agreed stop signal; release immediately if normal responsiveness changes


**Release Protocol**:
1. Open the finishing grip immediately at the first signal; do not wait for repeated confirmation
2. Remove the leg, arm or body restraint without forcing the captured joint or neck through another movement
3. Check the partner can respond normally and allow a safe reset; end practice and obtain help if there is an injury or concerning symptom


**Training Restrictions**:
- Start with supervised, cooperative control-and-release drilling rather than a loaded finish
- Check the event's current rules for the specific age, experience and clothing division before live use
- Use a cooperative entry and stop at the aligned capture. Never drop onto the stretch, bounce the leg, or roll sideways while the ankle is fixed.
