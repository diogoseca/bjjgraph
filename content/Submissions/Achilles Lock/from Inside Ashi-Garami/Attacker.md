<section id="overview" class="content-section">

Your finish is decided by the order you do things in, not by how hard you extend. Capture the foot above your hip line, close your knees to kill the escape lane, seat the blade on the tendon rather than the calf, and only then extend. Every practitioner who fails this submission fails it by reversing that order — extending first and then hunting for the blade, which turns a joint attack into a strength contest and warns the opponent while you still have nothing. Because the Achilles tendon gives almost no warning before it fails, the tap window is short: the ramp must be slow and deliberate every single time, including in competition.

**From Position**: [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]] (Top)


</section>



<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Attacking Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for executing Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami?</p>

- Placement before power — the blade sits on the tendon before any hip extension begins
- Capture the foot ABOVE your own hip line; below it, extension shortens the lever instead of loading it
- Close your knees to remove the escape lane before you commit to the finish
- Extend through the hips and shoulders together, not by pulling with the arms
- Compression, not rotation — this is the straight-ankle mechanism, and rotating turns it into a different and more dangerous attack
- Ramp slowly on every repetition; the tendon fails faster than a partner can tap


</section>

<section id="setup-requirements" class="content-section">

## Prerequisites

<p class="section-subtitle">What do you need before attempting Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami?</p>

- Ashi garami established with the opponent's leg threaded and their foot trapped against your ribs or armpit
- The captured foot sitting above your own hip line, not slipping down toward your belt
- Your inside knee positioned across their thigh to prevent them rotating free
- A free arm able to reach under the heel and present the blade of the forearm to the tendon


</section>

<section id="execution-steps" class="content-section">

## Execution Steps

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you execute Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami step by step?</p>

1. **Confirm the entanglement**: Check that the opponent's leg is genuinely threaded and that their foot is trapped high against your ribs or in your armpit. A foot that has slipped below your hip line cannot be finished by extension and must be re-captured first.
2. **Close the knees**: Pinch your knees together across their thigh so their leg cannot rotate or slide out. This removes the primary escape lane and is what makes the finish a submission rather than a race.
3. **Reach under the heel**: Thread your finishing arm under their Achilles so your forearm crosses the back of the ankle and your hand cups the heel. Keep the elbow tight to your ribs so the arm cannot be stripped.
4. **Seat the blade on the tendon**: Rotate the forearm so the sharp radial edge sits directly on the Achilles tendon rather than in the soft belly of the calf. This is the single detail that separates a finish from a squeeze, and it must be confirmed before any extension.
5. **Lock the structure**: Close a figure four or grip your own biceps to fix the blade in place, then bring your shoulders back over your hips so the arm cannot slide during the extension.
6. **Extend slowly to the tap**: Drive the hips forward and the shoulders back as one unit over three to five seconds, arching only as far as the tap requires. Stop the instant resistance changes character, and release the extension before the grip.

<section id="common-counters" class="content-section">

## Opponent Defenses

<p class="section-subtitle">How might your opponent defend against Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami?</p>

- **Rotating the knee inward to turn the heel away from the blade** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Follow the rotation with your hips and switch to a toe hold, or re-pinch the knees before they complete the turn. → Leads to [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]]
- **Boot-and-roll — driving the free foot into your hip and rolling toward the trapped side** (Effectiveness: High) - Your Response: Sit up into them and drop the outside leg to block the roll, keeping the blade seated throughout. → Leads to [[Positions/Closed Guard]]
- **Sitting up and hand-fighting the finishing arm before it locks** (Effectiveness: Medium) - Your Response: Break their posture back down with your outside leg across their hip, then rebuild the grip from a lying position. → Leads to [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]]

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Attacking Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when executing Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami?</p>

### 1. Extending before the blade is seated

- **Consequence**: The pressure lands on the calf, the opponent is warned, and the attack becomes a leg-strength contest a heavier opponent wins.
- **Correction**: Confirm the radial edge is on the tendon and only then begin the extension — placement always precedes power.

### 2. Letting the captured foot slip below your hip line

- **Consequence**: Extension no longer loads the ankle, and the opponent extracts the leg during the arch.
- **Correction**: Re-capture the foot high against the ribs or armpit before attempting the finish again.

### 3. Leaving the knees open

- **Consequence**: The opponent rotates the knee and turns the heel out of the blade, escaping or reversing into their own entanglement.
- **Correction**: Pinch the knees across their thigh as a distinct step before the arm ever moves.

### 4. Pulling with the arms instead of extending the hips

- **Consequence**: Arm strength cannot generate enough compression, the grip fatigues, and the finish stalls.
- **Correction**: Fix the arm structure and generate everything from the hips and shoulders moving as one unit.

### 5. Applying explosively or 'catching' the finish in a scramble

- **Consequence**: The Achilles tendon can rupture before a partner is able to tap, which is a career-length injury.
- **Correction**: Never finish a moving opponent — establish the position first, then ramp over three to five seconds.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami (Attacker)?</p>

**Isolation** - Blade placement
With a static partner, seat the blade on the tendon and have them confirm out loud whether it is on the tendon or the calf. Do not extend at all during this phase.

**Positional** - Capture height and knee pinch
Partner works only to slide the foot below your hip line and to rotate the knee. Your task is to keep the capture high and the knees closed for a full minute.

**Resistance** - The slow ramp
Live application against a defending partner with the explicit rule that the extension takes at least three seconds. A faster finish counts as a failed repetition regardless of the tap.

**Integration** - Chaining
From live ashi garami, attack the Achilles and switch to a toe hold or re-enter the entanglement when the knee rotates, so the finish sits inside a chain rather than standing alone.
