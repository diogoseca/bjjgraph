<section id="overview" class="content-section">

Your defence lives entirely before the blade is seated. Once the forearm is on the tendon, your knees are pinched and the extension starts, the tap window is measured in a second or two and the correct action is to tap — the Achilles tendon does not warn you and it does not forgive. So the whole game is recognition and early action: feel the foot being drawn above their hip line, feel the knees closing, and act on either one immediately. The two reliable escapes are rotating the knee to turn the heel out of the blade and driving the free foot into their hip to boot-and-roll, and both are far easier before the structure locks than after.

**Opponent's Starting Position**: [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]] (Top)


</section>



<section id="recognition-cues" class="content-section">

## How to Recognize This Submission

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you know when someone is attempting Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami?</p>

- Your foot is being drawn upward toward their ribs or armpit rather than staying at their belt line
- Their knees pinch together across your thigh, which removes your rotation escape
- Their arm threads under your heel and the forearm turns edge-on against the back of your ankle
- Their shoulders begin travelling back over their hips — the extension is starting and the window is closing


</section>

<section id="key-principles" class="content-section">

## Key Defensive Principles

<p class="section-subtitle">What are the key principles for defending Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami?</p>

- Act on the capture, not on the pain — by the time it hurts the tap window is nearly closed
- Rotating the knee inward turns the heel away from the blade and is the highest-percentage answer
- Keep your heel low and your foot below their hip line; the capture height is what makes their extension work
- Your free leg is your best tool — it boots their hip, blocks the roll, and creates the space to extract
- Sit up early: a defender lying flat has no leverage and no access to their own trapped foot
- Tap early and without ego on this one — a ruptured Achilles is measured in months, not weeks


</section>

<section id="defensive-options" class="content-section">

## Defensive Options

<p class="section-subtitle">What can you do to defend against Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami?</p>

### 1. Rotate the knee inward to turn the heel out of the blade

- **When to use**: As soon as the arm threads under the heel, before the knees pinch fully.
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]]
- **If successful**: The blade loses the tendon and you keep the leg, returning to a contested entanglement.
- **Risk**: Rotating into a heel hook grip instead is far more dangerous — confirm which attack you are in first.

### 2. Boot-and-roll — drive the free foot into their hip and roll toward the trapped side

- **When to use**: When the capture is set but the extension has not begun.
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Closed Guard]]
- **If successful**: You unwind the entanglement and come out on top, often inside your own closed guard position.
- **Risk**: Rolling the wrong way winds the ankle tighter and finishes the submission for them.

### 3. Sit up and hand-fight the finishing arm

- **When to use**: Early, while their arm structure is still being built and the figure four is open.
- **Targets**: [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]]
- **If successful**: You strip the grip before it locks and can begin extracting the leg.
- **Risk**: Sitting up late exposes your back and gives them the angle to finish anyway.

### 4. Tap

- **When to use**: The blade is seated, the knees are pinched, and the extension has begun.
- **Targets**: [[game-over]]
- **If successful**: You train again tomorrow.
- **Risk**: None. Waiting is the risk.



</section>

<section id="escape-paths" class="content-section">

## Escape Paths

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you escape Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami?</p>

- Knee rotation — turn the knee inward so the heel leaves the blade, then extract the leg over the top of their thigh
- Boot-and-roll — free foot to their hip, roll toward the trapped side, and unwind the entanglement as you come up
- Sit-up hand fight — post up, strip the finishing arm before the figure four closes, and back out of the entanglement


</section>

<section id="favorable-outcomes" class="content-section">

## Best-Case Outcomes for Defender

<p class="section-subtitle">What is the best outcome when defending Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami?</p>

### → [[Positions/Closed Guard]]

Boot their hip with the free foot, roll toward the trapped side, and come up as the entanglement unwinds, recovering guard on them as they follow. This is the counter outcome: the finish fails and you win the exchange.

### → [[Positions/Ashi Garami/Inside Ashi-Garami]]

Rotate the knee out of the blade early enough that the extension never loads. The finish fails but the entanglement continues, so you are still defending — you have bought time, not safety.



</section>

<section id="common-mistakes" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Common Defensive Mistakes

<p class="section-subtitle">What mistakes should you avoid when defending Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami?</p>

### 1. Waiting for pain before defending

- **Consequence**: The Achilles tendon gives almost no warning, so by the time it hurts the rupture window is already open.
- **Correction**: Defend the capture and the knee pinch, which are visible and felt early, rather than the pressure.

### 2. Rolling away from the trapped side

- **Consequence**: The rotation winds the ankle tighter into the blade and finishes the submission for them.
- **Correction**: Always roll toward the trapped side so the entanglement unwinds rather than tightens.

### 3. Lying flat and pulling with the trapped leg

- **Consequence**: A flat defender has no leverage, no access to their own foot, and simply loses a tug of war against their hips.
- **Correction**: Sit up first — every reliable escape from this position starts with posture.

### 4. Treating it like a heel hook and rotating hard

- **Consequence**: Rotating against a compression attack does nothing useful and can walk you into an actual heel hook grip.
- **Correction**: Identify the attack first: a blade across the back of the ankle is a compression finish, and the answer is knee rotation plus extraction, not counter-rotation.



</section>

<section id="training-progressions" class="content-section hide-minimal">

## Training Progressions

<p class="section-subtitle">How do you train defense against Achilles Lock from Inside Ashi-Garami?</p>

**Isolation** - Recognition
Partner sets the capture at three different heights without finishing. Call out loud whether your foot is above or below their hip line each time.

**Positional** - Early knee rotation
Start with their arm threaded but their knees still open. Rotate the knee and extract before they close the pinch, resetting on each failure.

**Resistance** - The roll
Start with the capture fully set and the knees pinched. Boot-and-roll toward the trapped side against live resistance, and tap immediately if the extension begins.
