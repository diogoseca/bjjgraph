<section id="overview" class="content-section">

The attacker has rear torso access, a secure jacket-collar grip and control of the opponent's lower body. Invisible Collar is a facing rubber-guard clinch without a required jacket grip. Rear control and a real collar grip must be established separately; this finish does not have a clothing-free equivalent with the same grip.

Keep the torso connected from behind before reaching for the collar. Place the hand securely in the jacket collar without dragging the cloth across the face. Use the available leg or trouser control to manage the opponent's hips. Turn the body gradually to create opposing collar and lower-body tension.

Use a sound gi and gradual tension. Do not substitute face pressure, jerk the collar, or treat a chin tuck as reliable protection from a secured choke.

Instructional sources: [Stephan Kesting and Emily Kwok — bow-and-arrow choke and escape](https://www.grapplearts.com/bow-arrow-choke-escape/); [Eddie Bravo — MTS 134 Invisible Collar in Austin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KCyIwacRKu8).

## Playing as Attacker

[[Submissions/Bow and Arrow Choke/from Invisible Collar/Attacker|→ Full Attacker Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Establish rear control: Keep the torso connected from behind before reaching for the collar.

- Take the collar grip: Place the hand securely in the jacket collar without dragging the cloth across the face.

- Control the lower body: Use the available leg or trouser control to manage the opponent's hips.

- Change the angle: Turn the body gradually to create opposing collar and lower-body tension.

- Remove slack slowly: Use the connection to the torso rather than a sudden arm pull or neck twist.

- Open both controls: Release the collar and lower-body restraint immediately on any stop signal.

## Playing as Defender

[[Submissions/Bow and Arrow Choke/from Invisible Collar/Defender|→ Full Defender Guide]]

### Key Principles


- Protect the collar early: Hand fight before the attacker obtains a deep grip from behind.

- Keep the bodies aligned: Avoid giving the attacker a large sideways angle while the collar remains fixed.

- Slide before tightening: Use an early downward body adjustment only while the neck has space and the grip is not loaded.

- Control the attacking elbow: Work at the elbow lever while protecting the neck instead of merely tugging at the sleeve.

- Tap before neck loading: Stop when the choke is established; do not attempt a violent turn against a fixed collar.

- Turn after the grip opens: Recover facing position only when the neck and collar have been released.


### Recognition Cues


- The attacker has rear torso access, a secure jacket-collar grip and control of the opponent's lower body.

- Place the hand securely in the jacket collar without dragging the cloth across the face.

- Turn the body gradually to create opposing collar and lower-body tension.


### Escape Paths


- Avoid giving the attacker a large sideways angle while the collar remains fixed.

- Use an early downward body adjustment only while the neck has space and the grip is not loaded.

<section id="safety-considerations" class="content-section">

## Safety Guide

**Injury Risks**:

| Injury | Severity | Recovery Time |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Neck injury from twisting against a fixed collar | High | Variable; stop training and seek appropriate clinical assessment after an injury or concerning symptoms. |
| Loss of consciousness or serious harm from sustained collar pressure | CRITICAL | Variable; stop training and seek appropriate clinical assessment after an injury or concerning symptoms. |


**Application Speed**: Use a sound gi and gradual tension. Do not substitute face pressure, jerk the collar, or treat a chin tuck as reliable protection from a secured choke. No fixed number of seconds makes a locked submission safe.

**Tap Signals**:
- Repeated hand taps anywhere the partner can feel them
- A clear verbal tap or stop, including when the hands are trapped
- Foot taps or any agreed stop signal; release immediately if normal responsiveness changes


**Release Protocol**:
1. Open the finishing grip immediately at the first signal; do not wait for repeated confirmation
2. Remove the leg, arm or body restraint without forcing the captured joint or neck through another movement
3. Check the partner can respond normally and allow a safe reset; end practice and obtain help if there is an injury or concerning symptom


**Training Restrictions**:
- Start with supervised, cooperative control-and-release drilling rather than a loaded finish
- Check the event's current rules for the specific age, experience and clothing division before live use
- Use a sound gi and gradual tension. Do not substitute face pressure, jerk the collar, or treat a chin tuck as reliable protection from a secured choke.
