<div class="learning-reader" data-content-type="learning">
<p class="learning-category">Strategy</p>
<section id="overview" class="learning-section">
<p class="learning-summary">Funneling means using control to make some opponent choices difficult while keeping a useful response to the choices that remain.</p>
<p>Pick an option you want to limit, such as a guard player turning to face you. Then identify the grip, angle, or body position that actually limits it; wanting a back take does not make the opponent turn away.</p>
</section>


<section id="key-takeaways" class="learning-section">
<h2>Key takeaways</h2>
<ul><li>Name the option your control removes. A grip is useful because of what it changes.</li><li>Keep your preferred follow-up reachable from the controls you already have.</li><li>Expect more than one answer, including the opponent clearing your control or choosing not to move.</li></ul>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more takeaway</summary><ul><li>Distinguish limiting a route from provoking a reaction: a funnel shapes the available choices before you read the response.</li></ul></details>

</section>



<section id="applications" class="learning-section">
<h2>In practice</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>You have side control and want to follow a turn away</h3><p>Use upper-body control that limits their turn toward you. If they turn away, stay connected and work toward back control; if they remain flat, keep a suitable pin and work from there.</p><p class="learning-result">Both staying and turning leave you with a task you have prepared.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>A seated guard player keeps moving away from your passing entry</h3><p>Establish a connection that lets you follow before committing your weight. Use your angle to approach one side while limiting their easy retreat.</p><p class="learning-result">You reduce the distance they can create without treating a grip as a completed pass.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more example</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>A passer repeatedly circles past your unconnected feet</h3><p>Use a leg connection and an upper-body grip suited to your guard to reduce free circling. Off-balance from those connections rather than reaching after the passer with both hands.</p><p class="learning-result">The passer must address a connection before returning to unrestricted movement.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="mistakes" class="learning-section">
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Assuming the remaining reaction is guaranteed</h3><p>You launch a follow-up against a response that never happened.</p><p>Wait for the position cue and retain an option for a partner who stays still.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Removing an escape by giving up your own base</h3><p>The opponent reverses you while you concentrate on blocking one route.</p><p>Check your balance and the next likely threat whenever you change a control.</p></div>

</section>



<section id="exercises" class="learning-section">
<h2>Try it in training</h2>
<div class="learning-exercise"><h3>One route blocked</h3><p>Start in side control. Top chooses one movement to limit: turning in or moving the hips away. Bottom tries several escapes at manageable resistance. Reset on guard recovery, reversal, a new stable pin, or a tap; compare which control actually limited the chosen route.</p><p class="learning-focus">Connect a control to a specific restriction.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more exercise</summary><div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Two acceptable outcomes</h3><p>Start in butterfly guard with upper-body contact. Bottom draws the partner forward; top may stay close or withdraw. Bottom tries to off-balance a close partner or follows a withdrawal to rebuild seated contact. Reset on a sweep, pass, or lost connection.</p><p class="learning-focus">Prepare for the choices that remain instead of demanding one reaction.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="self-assessment" class="learning-section">
<h2>Self-assessment</h2>
<details class="learning-question"><summary>How do you know a funnel is working?</summary><p>A useful opponent option becomes harder while you retain balance and a response to what they actually do.</p></details>
</section>
