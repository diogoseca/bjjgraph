<p class="learning-category">Strategy</p>
<section id="overview" class="learning-section">
<p class="learning-summary">A useful passing checklist is to remove obstructing connections, get beyond the legs, and establish control that survives the first escape.</p>
<p>You may win these jobs together or revisit one several times. Use the checklist to diagnose a stalled pass, especially when getting around the feet keeps ending in another guard.</p>
</section>


<section id="key-takeaways" class="learning-section">
<h2>Key takeaways</h2>
<ul><li>Identify the grip or hook that actually prevents your next step; clearing every contact is rarely necessary.</li><li>As you move past the legs, keep track of the hips and the knee that can return inside.</li><li>Getting your chest beyond the feet is progress, but it does not by itself control the torso.</li></ul>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 2 more takeaways</summary><ul><li>Finish with a stable pin or another deliberate position before chasing a submission.</li><li>If the guard returns, name what was missing: access, control of the recovery, or the final pin.</li></ul></details>

</section>



<section id="applications" class="learning-section">
<h2>In practice</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>You stand in a De La Riva guard with your posture pulled forward</h3><p>Address the grip bending your posture and the hook governing your lead leg before committing to a passing direction. Keep your balance while changing those connections.</p><p class="learning-result">Your passing step no longer depends on pulling through a control you have ignored.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>You move around the feet but the near knee keeps returning</h3><p>Check the space between your body and the guard player’s hips. Control that recovery route while bringing your torso into position, rather than sprinting farther around their head.</p><p class="learning-result">The knee has less room to re-enter while you complete the pass.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more example</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>You reach side control and immediately lose it</h3><p>Settle your base and a suitable upper-body control, then answer the first frame or hip escape. Delay the next attack until you can stay on top through that movement.</p><p class="learning-result">You can tell whether the pass produced a usable pin.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="mistakes" class="learning-section">
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Dropping weight before your balance and position are ready</h3><p>The guard player can redirect you or recover around the space you leave.</p><p>Win a stable angle and control the relevant leg or hip as you close distance.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Treating the checklist as three mandatory pauses</h3><p>A useful opening disappears while you stop to complete a stage that was already handled.</p><p>Keep moving when the next job is available; return to an earlier job when a new grip or hook stops you.</p></div>

</section>



<section id="exercises" class="learning-section">
<h2>Try it in training</h2>
<div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Pass and hold through one escape</h3><p>Start in a familiar open guard. Top passes and must stay on top through bottom’s first deliberate escape attempt before resetting. Bottom tries to recover or reverse. Stop on a tap; switch roles after each result.</p><p class="learning-focus">Include consolidation in every passing repetition.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more exercise</summary><div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Start where the pass fails</h3><p>Choose a recurring failure: trapped lead leg, returning knee, or lost side control. Start just before it with agreed grips. Spar until a pass settles, guard returns, or a reversal occurs, then restart at the same checkpoint.</p><p class="learning-focus">Repair the missing job without replaying the whole entry.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="self-assessment" class="learning-section">
<h2>Self-assessment</h2>
<details class="learning-question"><summary>You cleared the legs but got re-guarded immediately. What should you review?</summary><p>How you controlled the hips and returning knee, and whether your upper body ever settled into a usable pin.</p></details><details class="learning-question"><summary>Do you have to strip every grip before passing?</summary><p>No. Remove or neutralize the connections that obstruct the chosen pass, while accounting for any grips that still threaten your balance.</p></details>
</section>
