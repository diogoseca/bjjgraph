<section id="overview" class="learning-section">
<p class="learning-summary">Choose training intensity by the job of the round: learning a movement, solving a position, or rehearsing a match.</p>
<p>A partner can resist strongly while moving slowly, or move quickly without trying to stop you. Agree on what each person will do before relying on a vague request to go light.</p>
</section>


<section id="key-takeaways" class="learning-section">
<h2>Key takeaways</h2>
<ul><li>Separate speed, resistance, and available attacks. Change one at a time when you are trying to understand a problem.</li><li>For a new movement, start with a partner who allows the setup; then give them a specific way to resist.</li><li>Describe the round in actions: defend the pass, keep the same starting grips, or reset after a sweep.</li></ul>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 2 more takeaways</summary><ul><li>Competition practice needs an agreed ruleset and pace. It does not require every training round to feel like a match.</li><li>Pause and renegotiate if either partner cannot follow the task or control the exchange.</li></ul></details>

</section>



<section id="applications" class="learning-section">
<h2>In practice</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>A pass works in drilling and falls apart as soon as your partner resists.</h3><p>Keep the starting position fixed. Ask your partner to recover their knee, without adding grips or counterattacks yet. Work on that problem before opening the round.</p><p class="learning-result">You can identify the missing control instead of attributing every failure to a lack of effort.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>You asked for a light round, but your partner keeps accelerating through escapes.</h3><p>Stop briefly and make the request concrete: slow transitions, no explosive escapes, and a reset when either person loses control of the pace.</p><p class="learning-result">Both people now have an observable agreement to follow.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more example</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>You want to prepare for a match.</h3><p>With your coach and a willing partner, use the event duration and rules, keep score, and agree which starts or exchanges you will rehearse. Review the decisions as well as the result.</p><p class="learning-result">The round tests your plan under match constraints without turning the whole session into a competition.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="mistakes" class="learning-section">
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Calling every difficult round a fitness problem.</h3><p>You may overlook prolonged squeezing, an unfamiliar position, or a pace mismatch.</p><p>Describe where the task broke down before choosing what to change.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Using a percentage such as 80% as the whole agreement.</h3><p>Partners can attach very different behavior to the same number.</p><p>Name the allowed resistance, pace, and reset condition.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more mistake</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>Increasing speed to rescue a movement you cannot yet coordinate.</h3><p>The failed repetitions become harder to inspect.</p><p>Slow the exchange, find the missing connection, and add resistance again.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="exercises" class="learning-section">
<h2>Try it in training</h2>
<div class="learning-exercise"><h3>One new obstacle</h3><p>Start in half guard top with a familiar pass. First rehearse the setup cooperatively; then let the bottom partner try only to recover their knee shield. Reset after a pass or a recovered shield. Add another defensive option only when both partners can describe what changed.</p><p class="learning-focus">Adjust resistance without changing the entire problem.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more exercise</summary><div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Agree, roll, review</h3><p>Before a positional round, each partner states a goal and the allowed pace. Start from that position and reset after a pass, sweep, or submission. Stop immediately on a tap or request to pause. After the round, compare whether the actual pace matched the agreement.</p><p class="learning-focus">Make intensity a shared decision.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="self-assessment" class="learning-section">
<h2>Self-assessment</h2>
<details class="learning-question"><summary>How can a slow round still be difficult?</summary><p>The partner can deny your grips, keep their balance, and defend the position without moving at maximum speed.</p></details>
</section>
