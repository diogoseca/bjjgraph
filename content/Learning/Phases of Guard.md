<section id="overview" class="learning-section">
<p class="learning-summary">Guard play shifts between making connections, using them to attack, and recovering them when a pass threatens.</p>
<p>The engagement, maintenance, and retention framework appears in Rob Biernacki and Stephan Kesting’s BJJ Formula curriculum. Use it to choose your next job; a live exchange can move between these priorities within a few seconds.</p>
</section>


<section id="key-takeaways" class="learning-section">
<h2>Key takeaways</h2>
<ul><li>Engagement: make a useful connection without letting the passer move straight around your legs.</li><li>Maintenance: use grips and hooks to affect posture or balance while keeping a way to follow the passer.</li><li>Retention: when the passer clears your legs or wins an angle, protect the space around your torso and recover a barrier.</li></ul>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 2 more takeaways</summary><ul><li>Change priorities when the position changes. A lost grip matters less than a passer who has already reached your hip.</li><li>Offense can help retention when it restores control or balance; abandon an attack that leaves you unable to follow the pass.</li></ul></details>

</section>



<section id="applications" class="learning-section">
<h2>In practice</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Seated, facing a standing passer without grips</h3><p>Stay facing the passer and seek a connection you can use, such as wrist control with a foot or shin managing distance. Do not lie back expecting an absent grip to hold them nearby.</p><p class="learning-result">You have a starting point for a guard rather than a named position with no control.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Your De La Riva hook and grips are established</h3><p>Use your connections to move the passer’s weight and test a sweep. Follow their stance changes; the hook alone does not keep the guard intact.</p><p class="learning-result">The passer has to address your balance threat while you check which connections remain useful.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more example</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>The passer clears your feet and reaches the side of your hips</h3><p>Release an attacking grip if it prevents you from framing. Face the passer, keep your elbows near your knees, and make room to bring a leg back between you.</p><p class="learning-result">Recovering any workable guard gives you another chance to establish your preferred one.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="mistakes" class="learning-section">
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Insisting on the original guard after its grips and angle are gone</h3><p>You keep searching for an attack while the passer reaches your torso.</p><p>Recover a frame or leg first; choose the guard once you have space.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Treating the phases as a fixed sequence</h3><p>You pause to complete a label even when a sweep or recovery is already available.</p><p>Use the labels after the round to explain a decision, not as commands that override the position.</p></div>

</section>



<section id="exercises" class="learning-section">
<h2>Try it in training</h2>
<div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Three starting points</h3><p>Start one exchange disconnected, one in an established open guard, and one with the passer beside the legs but without a chest pin. Bottom tries to sweep or recover; top tries to pass. Reset on a sweep, a settled pin, or a tap, then change the starting point.</p><p class="learning-focus">Recognize the job imposed by the starting position.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more exercise</summary><div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Replay the lost connection</h3><p>After a pass, agree on the first grip, hook, or angle that bottom lost. Restart just before that moment at manageable resistance. Try a recovery instead of continuing the original attack; reset when guard returns or the pin settles.</p><p class="learning-focus">Notice the cue to switch from attacking to recovering.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="self-assessment" class="learning-section">
<h2>Self-assessment</h2>
<details class="learning-question"><summary>Your attacking grip remains, but the passer has cleared both legs. What changes?</summary><p>The urgent job is protecting your torso and recovering a barrier. Keep the grip only if it helps that job.</p></details><details class="learning-question"><summary>Does retention always mean abandoning offense?</summary><p>No. A balance threat may help you recover, but the attack must leave a workable answer to the pass.</p></details>
</section>
