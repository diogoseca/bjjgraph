<p class="learning-category">Strategy</p>
<section id="overview" class="learning-section">
<p class="learning-summary">A useful specialty includes a position you can reach, an attack you trust, and an answer when the opponent denies it.</p>
<p>Your favorite move may be strong once established and hard to reach from anywhere else. Spend some of your extra practice on that access problem before adding another finish.</p>
</section>


<section id="key-takeaways" class="learning-section">
<h2>Key takeaways</h2>
<ul><li>Choose a strength from repeated live experience, interest, and access to useful coaching; body type alone does not choose your game.</li><li>Develop the route into your preferred position as carefully as the attack from it.</li><li>Practice the opponent’s most common denial, not just the sequence that already works.</li></ul>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 2 more takeaways</summary><ul><li>Keep practicing escapes and unfamiliar positions so your specialty does not become your only usable option.</li><li>Reassess your focus when training partners, rules, or the problems you encounter change.</li></ul></details>

</section>



<section id="applications" class="learning-section">
<h2>In practice</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Your half guard sweep works, but you rarely reach the underhook it needs.</h3><p>Start extra rounds before the underhook is won. Practice the entry and a response to being flattened, instead of beginning every drill with your best grips supplied.</p><p class="learning-result">Your strongest sweep becomes connected to the positions you actually encounter.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>You finish well from mount but lose balance while arriving there.</h3><p>Link your pass to the mount entry, then let your partner immediately try their familiar escape. Work on landing with usable base before adding a submission.</p><p class="learning-result">The practice connects the finish to stable arrival.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more example</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>You have a competition coming up and keep adding techniques to the plan.</h3><p>Write one route you already use, the most likely place it gets stopped, and a familiar alternative. Rehearse those branches under the event constraints.</p><p class="learning-result">Your preparation has a manageable set of decisions to test.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="mistakes" class="learning-section">
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Treating a favorite position as a reason to skip everything else.</h3><p>A single denied entry can leave you without a workable next step.</p><p>Maintain an escape and a recovery route from the positions where your plan tends to fail.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Choosing a game from a body-type label.</h3><p>You may abandon a position you enjoy before learning how to adjust it.</p><p>Test entries, control, and recovery in live practice before deciding whether the position suits you.</p></div>

</section>



<section id="exercises" class="learning-section">
<h2>Try it in training</h2>
<div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Start outside your specialty</h3><p>Choose your preferred guard or top position. Begin one exchange away from it and ask your partner to deny your entry. Reset when you establish the position, lose the route, or reach a tap. After several attempts, drill the most common denial before repeating.</p><p class="learning-focus">Build access to the game you already trust.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more exercise</summary><div class="learning-exercise"><h3>One route and one detour</h3><p>Start from a familiar position and rehearse your main sequence. Let your partner choose either the usual response or one agreed denial. Follow your main route or its practiced detour; reset when the next position is established.</p><p class="learning-focus">Keep specialization adaptable.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="self-assessment" class="learning-section">
<h2>Self-assessment</h2>
<details class="learning-question"><summary>What should you practice if your favorite attack works only from perfect grips?</summary><p>The route to those grips and a usable response when the opponent denies them.</p></details>
</section>
