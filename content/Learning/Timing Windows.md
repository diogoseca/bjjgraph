<p class="learning-category">Strategy</p>
<section id="overview" class="learning-section">
<p class="learning-summary">A timing window is a temporary change in balance, contact, or position that makes a particular action available.</p>
<p>The useful cue is specific: a knee lifts, a hand leaves its post, or pressure briefly changes direction. Learn what your chosen action needs so you can recognize both an opening and the moment it has already closed.</p>
</section>


<section id="key-takeaways" class="learning-section">
<h2>Key takeaways</h2>
<ul><li>Pair the timing cue with one action; “move during transitions” is too broad to guide a decision.</li><li>Secure the position the action needs before waiting for its cue.</li><li>Act while the relevant support or contact is changing, not simply because the opponent is moving.</li></ul>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 2 more takeaways</summary><ul><li>Create a usable change with an off-balance or threat when waiting gives the opponent more control.</li><li>If the opening closes, rebuild. Speed does not replace a missing grip, angle, or safe route.</li></ul></details>

</section>



<section id="applications" class="learning-section">
<h2>In practice</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>A mounted opponent shifts weight to adjust their hands</h3><p>Feel whether the shift actually lightens the leg you need to recover. If it does, use your frames and hip movement to bring your knee and elbow closer; if not, keep defending the pin.</p><p class="learning-result">Your escape attempt is connected to a relevant weight change rather than any upper-body motion.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>A guard player removes a foot while changing guards</h3><p>Use an existing balanced stance and leg control to advance through the opening if their other leg cannot immediately block you. Be ready to stop when the new hook is established.</p><p class="learning-result">You pass during a contact change without assuming that every guard switch is vulnerable.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more example</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>A passer settles weight onto your butterfly hook</h3><p>With suitable upper-body control, use that loading to begin an off-balance. If they withdraw the weight before you can act, follow to rebuild contact rather than lifting an empty space.</p><p class="learning-result">The attempt depends on the actual load and connection.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="mistakes" class="learning-section">
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Waiting for an opening while the opponent improves their position</h3><p>Your remaining controls disappear before the imagined perfect moment.</p><p>Create a manageable reaction or improve your own frame and posture first.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Treating breathing or a facial expression as a dependable cue</h3><p>Neither tells you whether the required balance or support has changed.</p><p>Use observable contact, weight, and position relevant to the action you want.</p></div>

</section>



<section id="exercises" class="learning-section">
<h2>Try it in training</h2>
<div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Open and close the window</h3><p>Start in butterfly guard with the upper-body control used in a sweep you have learned. At low resistance, top alternates between loading the hook and withdrawing their weight. Bottom attempts the sweep only while the load is available. Reset on the sweep, lost connection, or a tap.</p><p class="learning-focus">Learn what the opening feels like and when it has ended.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more exercise</summary><div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Create the cue</h3><p>Start from the same grips, but let the partner keep their support. Use a controlled off-balance to make them adjust, then try the action if the cue appears. Reset on a sweep, pass, lost connection, or tap.</p><p class="learning-focus">Develop timing through interaction rather than passive waiting.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="self-assessment" class="learning-section">
<h2>Self-assessment</h2>
<details class="learning-question"><summary>Is every transition a useful opening?</summary><p>No. The change must remove or weaken something that blocks your chosen action, while you still have the connections to use it.</p></details><details class="learning-question"><summary>You recognized the cue too late. What is the useful response?</summary><p>Keep or rebuild your position and try to create the opening again instead of forcing the action after its conditions have gone.</p></details>
</section>
