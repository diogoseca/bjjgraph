<p class="learning-category">Strategy</p>
<section id="overview" class="learning-section">
<p class="learning-summary">A useful technique chain connects an attack to a follow-up through a specific defensive cue and controls you can carry into the next action.</p>
<p>Start with one transition between two moves you already know. The difficult part is often the handoff: which grip stays, which one changes, and whether the opening still exists when you arrive.</p>
</section>


<section id="key-takeaways" class="learning-section">
<h2>Key takeaways</h2>
<ul><li>Choose a first attack you can actually finish; otherwise the defense you want may never appear.</li><li>Name the cue for the second action and the connections it needs.</li><li>Practice the transition between the moves, including the moment you change a grip or body angle.</li></ul>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 2 more takeaways</summary><ul><li>Include a stopping point: if the controls are gone, recover a workable position.</li><li>Keep the first version short enough to test against resistance before adding more branches.</li></ul></details>

</section>



<section id="applications" class="learning-section">
<h2>In practice</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>A closed-guard hip-bump attempt draws forward pressure</h3><p>If the partner drives back in with an accessible arm, a kimura or overhook entry may follow. Preserve control of the arm and distance; do not fall back expecting a grip you have not secured.</p><p class="learning-result">The defense suggests a continuation, while the available connection determines which one fits.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>You sweep from butterfly guard and arrive on top</h3><p>Keep track of the arm and legs that can stop your progress. Establish balance and begin a familiar pass if the opponent’s legs remain between you.</p><p class="learning-result">The sweep flows into the actual landing position instead of ending with an unnecessary release.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more example</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>A passing attempt succeeds and the bottom player turns away</h3><p>Stay connected to their upper body and hips before attempting a back take. If they return flat, settle the pin rather than chasing behind a turn that has ended.</p><p class="learning-result">Your next action follows the escape that is present.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="mistakes" class="learning-section">
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Memorizing a long sequence with a cooperative partner</h3><p>The chain depends on defenses that may never happen in a round.</p><p>Test two actions with a partner who can choose whether and how to defend.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Releasing every grip between moves</h3><p>The opponent can recover before the second attack starts.</p><p>Identify one useful connection to retain and practice changing the others around it.</p></div>

</section>



<section id="exercises" class="learning-section">
<h2>Try it in training</h2>
<div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Train the handoff</h3><p>Choose two familiar actions and start just before the first meets its expected defense. Move into the second at a controlled pace, keeping the required connection. Reset when the second position is secured, the connection is lost, or either partner taps.</p><p class="learning-focus">Find the grip and angle change that links the moves.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more exercise</summary><div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Make the branch real</h3><p>Start earlier in the same exchange. The defender may allow the first attack, use the expected defense, or disengage. Continue, switch, or recover accordingly; reset after a clear positional result or tap.</p><p class="learning-focus">Use a chain as a decision, not a script.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="self-assessment" class="learning-section">
<h2>Self-assessment</h2>
<details class="learning-question"><summary>What information should you know before adding a second attack?</summary><p>The defense that makes it available, the controls it needs, and where you will recover if those controls disappear.</p></details>
</section>
