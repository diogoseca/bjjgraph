<p class="learning-category">Strategy</p>
<section id="overview" class="learning-section">
<p class="learning-summary">Useful defense addresses the immediate threat and gives you a reachable next step toward more space, a guard, or a reversal.</p>
<p>Sometimes the next step is small: free an elbow, turn onto a side, or stop a hand reaching your neck. Surviving while you rebuild those options is progress; you do not need to force a complete escape in one movement.</p>
</section>


<section id="key-takeaways" class="learning-section">
<h2>Key takeaways</h2>
<ul><li>Address a submission threat before attempting a positional escape; tap when you cannot defend safely.</li><li>Choose a nearby improvement you can describe, such as getting your elbow inside or recovering half guard.</li><li>Use the space a frame creates. If you never move into it, the top player has time to change the pin.</li></ul>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 2 more takeaways</summary><ul><li>Keep the improvement you have earned before reaching for the next one.</li><li>An escape can be partial. Half guard with working frames may be a useful result from mount.</li></ul></details>

</section>



<section id="applications" class="learning-section">
<h2>In practice</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>You are under side control with your near elbow outside</h3><p>First make room to bring the elbow inside without extending both arms. Once you have a usable frame, move your hips and try to insert a knee.</p><p class="learning-result">The first success is an inside frame; guard recovery is the next job.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>You are mounted and the top player follows every hip escape</h3><p>Protect your neck and keep your elbows near your body. Use a controlled bridge to make them adjust their base, then work your elbow and knee toward recovering a leg.</p><p class="learning-result">You connect the bridge to a specific recovery instead of repeating a movement with no destination.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more example</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>You have recovered half guard but are still flat underneath</h3><p>Keep the trapped leg while rebuilding a frame and room to turn toward the opponent. Do not release the leg just to chase closed guard that is not yet available.</p><p class="learning-result">You retain the progress of the escape while improving a still-difficult position.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="mistakes" class="learning-section">
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Trying to escape through an already secured submission</h3><p>The positional movement may tighten the finish or remove your remaining defense.</p><p>Defend the actual submission first and tap before injury; restart to practice an earlier decision.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Rejecting any result short of full guard or top position</h3><p>You discard useful intermediate control and return to the pin.</p><p>Choose and secure one attainable improvement at a time.</p></div>

</section>



<section id="exercises" class="learning-section">
<h2>Try it in training</h2>
<div class="learning-exercise"><h3>One useful improvement</h3><p>Start under side control with no submission applied. Bottom chooses a small goal, such as an inside elbow or a knee between the bodies; top holds the pin with agreed resistance. Reset on that improvement, a position change, or a tap.</p><p class="learning-focus">Recognize and keep partial progress.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more exercise</summary><div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Escape in two parts</h3><p>Start under mount. Bottom first tries to recover half guard, then works from there toward an open guard; top tries to re-establish mount or pass. Reset after the second recovery, a settled pin, or a tap.</p><p class="learning-focus">Connect an escape to the work required in its landing position.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="self-assessment" class="learning-section">
<h2>Self-assessment</h2>
<details class="learning-question"><summary>Is holding a defensive position always passive?</summary><p>No. Preventing a submission and rebuilding an inside frame can be the necessary work before any escape is available.</p></details><details class="learning-question"><summary>You escaped mount to half guard. Is the escape wasted?</summary><p>No. You recovered a leg barrier; now preserve it and improve your posture and frames.</p></details>
</section>
