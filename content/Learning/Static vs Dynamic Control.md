<p class="learning-category">Strategy</p>
<section id="overview" class="learning-section">
<p class="learning-summary">Top control combines settling a useful pin with changing grips or position when the opponent’s escape makes that pin difficult to keep.</p>
<p>Even a settled pin needs small adjustments. Movement is useful when it preserves an advantage or creates a new one; circling for its own sake can give the bottom player the space they need.</p>
</section>


<section id="key-takeaways" class="learning-section">
<h2>Key takeaways</h2>
<ul><li>Settle when your base and controls prevent the opponent from making useful space.</li><li>Move when a grip change, hip switch, or new pin answers the escape better than squeezing the old hold.</li><li>Keep a connection during transitions so the opponent cannot turn freely underneath you.</li></ul>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 2 more takeaways</summary><ul><li>Re-establish balance and control after moving; reaching mount is not the same as holding it.</li><li>Choose from the position you see rather than assigning static or mobile styles by body size.</li></ul></details>

</section>



<section id="applications" class="learning-section">
<h2>In practice</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>You have side control and the bottom player cannot recover an inside frame</h3><p>Maintain a stable base and adjust to their bridge without unnecessarily opening a gap. Work toward your next attack from that control.</p><p class="learning-result">You use the pin you earned instead of giving it away through needless movement.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Strong inside frames start lifting your torso away</h3><p>Change your grip, hip angle, or pin while tracking their hips. Do not insist on driving straight into frames that are now supporting your weight.</p><p class="learning-result">You answer the new structure before a knee returns inside.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more example</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>You move from side control toward mount and feel a leg being trapped</h3><p>Check your balance and clear the leg only if you can do so under control. If half guard is established, treat it as another passing problem rather than claiming a finished mount.</p><p class="learning-result">You recognize the position you actually landed in.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="mistakes" class="learning-section">
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Holding tighter after the useful controls are gone</h3><p>You spend effort on a grip that no longer prevents the escape.</p><p>Identify what changed and choose a new control that addresses it.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Moving to the next pin before controlling the current transition</h3><p>The bottom player turns into the gap or gets their knees back inside.</p><p>Maintain contact with the hips or upper body and settle before changing again.</p></div>

</section>



<section id="exercises" class="learning-section">
<h2>Try it in training</h2>
<div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Follow one escape</h3><p>Start in side control. Bottom uses a controlled bridge and hip escape; top may adjust the pin or move to another pin. Reset on guard recovery, reversal, a stable new pin, or a tap.</p><p class="learning-focus">Change only as much as the escape requires.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more exercise</summary><div class="learning-exercise"><h3>Arrive and settle</h3><p>Start beside the legs after a pass, with neither player fully settled. Top tries to establish side control or mount while bottom recovers guard. Reset when either position is clearly established.</p><p class="learning-focus">Make control after movement part of the transition.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="self-assessment" class="learning-section">
<h2>Self-assessment</h2>
<details class="learning-question"><summary>Is constant movement evidence of good dynamic control?</summary><p>Only if it preserves an advantage. If the opponent gains space, turns freely, or recovers guard, the movement did not maintain control.</p></details>
</section>
