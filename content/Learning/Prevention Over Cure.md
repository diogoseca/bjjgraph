<p class="learning-category">Strategy</p>
<section id="overview" class="learning-section">
<p class="learning-summary">Early defense addresses the grip, angle, or connection that enables a threat before the opponent completes it.</p>
<p>When you review an escape failure, look a little earlier than the pin. There may have been a moment when your elbow could stay inside or your knee could return without first moving the opponent’s settled weight.</p>
</section>


<section id="key-takeaways" class="learning-section">
<h2>Key takeaways</h2>
<ul><li>Learn the earliest cue you can reliably recognize, such as a cleared knee or a hand entering a collar.</li><li>Prevent the next meaningful control, rather than fighting every touch as though it were equally urgent.</li><li>Keep the threatened space occupied with an appropriate frame or leg barrier while you recover your position.</li></ul>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 2 more takeaways</summary><ul><li>If the early defense fails, acknowledge the new position and switch to a taught escape.</li><li>An immediate submission threat takes priority over stopping a positional advance.</li></ul></details>

</section>



<section id="applications" class="learning-section">
<h2>In practice</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>A passer clears your knee shield but has not yet settled chest-to-chest.</h3><p>Use the remaining space to restore a leg barrier or recover an inside frame. Once they settle the pin, switch to the escape that fits the controls they have established.</p><p class="learning-result">You distinguish the chance to recover guard from the later task of escaping a pin.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>A partner begins feeding a collar grip toward your neck.</h3><p>Address the grip and posture before continuing your own attack. Notice what keeps the attacking hand from reaching its next useful control.</p><p class="learning-result">You respond to the setup while it is still changing.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more example</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>An opponent circles behind you during a passing scramble.</h3><p>Track the loss of your ability to face them. Recover that alignment while space remains; if they establish back control, protect against the immediate attack and use your practiced back defense.</p><p class="learning-result">You avoid treating an established back attack as though it were still only a passing problem.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="mistakes" class="learning-section">
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<div class="learning-example"><h3>Trying to stop every grip with the same urgency.</h3><p>You can surrender useful position while chasing a harmless connection.</p><p>Ask what the grip enables and whether a more immediate threat is developing.</p></div><div class="learning-example"><h3>Continuing the early defense after the opponent has already settled the pin.</h3><p>You may reach or twist without the space that made the original response available.</p><p>Recognize the changed position, rebuild your defense, and choose the appropriate escape.</p></div>

<details class="learning-more"><summary>Show 1 more mistake</summary><div class="learning-example"><h3>Practicing only from completely established bad positions.</h3><p>You miss the decisions that led into those positions.</p><p>Include starts just before the decisive grip or barrier is lost.</p></div></details>

</section>



<section id="exercises" class="learning-section">
<h2>Try it in training</h2>
<div class="learning-exercise"><h3>One step before the pin</h3><p>Start with a partner passing your half guard, just before the knee shield is cleared. The passer tries to settle side control; the defender tries to restore a leg barrier. Reset on either result or a tap. Repeat from a slightly later start to identify when recovery becomes a pin-escape problem.</p><p class="learning-focus">Recognize the boundary between prevention and escape.</p></div>

</section>



<section id="self-assessment" class="learning-section">
<h2>Self-assessment</h2>
<details class="learning-question"><summary>What changes when the early defensive opportunity has passed?</summary><p>The available space and controls have changed, so switch to defending and escaping the position that now exists.</p></details>
</section>
