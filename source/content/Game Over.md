<section id="overview" class="content-section">

**Game Over** is the terminal state in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu where a match ends through submission. This node represents the ultimate objective of submission grappling: forcing your opponent to acknowledge defeat through a tap (verbal or physical) due to a joint lock or choke. Every submission in the BJJ Graph converges to this single endpoint.

In the state machine model of BJJ, this is a **sink node** - once reached, the match resets. Unlike point-based victories, a submission represents complete technical dominance where the opponent has no option but to concede.

</section>

<section id="paths-to-game-over" class="content-section">

## Paths to Game Over

All submissions lead here. The BJJ Graph tracks the following submission categories:

### Chokes (Blood and Air)

Blood chokes target the carotid arteries to restrict blood flow to the brain, while air chokes compress the trachea. Blood chokes are generally preferred for their efficiency and safety profile.

- [[Rear Naked Choke]] - The highest-percentage submission in grappling
- [[Triangle Choke]] - Leg-based blood choke from guard positions
- [[Guillotine Choke]] - Front headlock choke with multiple variations
- [[Arm Triangle]] - Shoulder-assisted blood choke
- [[Darce Choke]] - No-gi arm-in head and arm choke
- [[Anaconda Choke]] - Opposite arm configuration to the D'arce
- [[Ezekiel Choke]] - Sleeve or wrist-assisted choke
- [[Bow and Arrow Choke]] - High-control gi choke from back
- [[Cross Collar Choke]] - Fundamental gi lapel choke
- [[North-South Choke]] - Shoulder pressure blood choke
- [[Clock Choke]] - Gi choke from turtle position
- [[Loop Choke]] - Lapel choke from various positions
- [[Baseball Bat Choke]] - Grip-specific collar choke
- [[Peruvian Necktie]] - Front headlock variation
- [[Japanese Necktie]] - Rotational headlock choke
- [[Von Flue Choke]] - Counter to guillotine defense
- [[Brabo Choke]] - D'arce variant with different grip
- [[Buggy Choke]] - Bottom position arm trap choke
- [[Breadcutter Choke]] - Lapel and collar control choke

### Joint Locks (Arms)

Arm locks hyperextend the elbow or rotate the shoulder beyond safe range of motion.

- [[Armbar from Mount]] - Classic attack from dominant position
- [[Armbar from Guard]] - Fundamental closed guard submission
- [[Armbar from Back]] - Attack from rear control
- [[Armbar from Side Control]] - Near or far side attacks
- [[Kimura]] - Double wristlock shoulder lock
- [[Americana]] - Reverse figure-four shoulder lock
- [[Omoplata]] - Leg-based shoulder lock
- [[Straight Armbar]] - Extended arm attack
- [[Belly Down Armbar]] - Face-down finish position
- [[Flying Armbar]] - Aerial armbar entry
- [[Reverse Armbar]] - Inverted grip armbar
- [[Mir Lock]] - Shoulder lock from guard

### Joint Locks (Legs)

Leg locks attack the knee, ankle, or hip joints. These require heightened safety awareness due to injury potential.

- [[Kneebar]] - Hyperextension of the knee
- [[Heel Hook Variations]] - Rotational attack on knee ligaments
- [[Inside Heel Hook]] - High-danger medial knee attack
- [[Achilles Lock]] - Ankle and Achilles tendon compression
- [[Estima Lock]] - Foot lock from various positions
- [[Aoki Lock]] - Calf slicer variation
- [[Calf Slicer]] - Compression lock on calf muscle

### Compression Locks

Compression locks cause pain through muscle or tendon compression rather than joint manipulation.

- [[Bicep Slicer]] - Arm compression lock
- [[Calf Slicer]] - Leg compression lock
- [[Can Opener]] - Neck compression (use with extreme caution)

<section id="safety" class="content-section">

## Safety Protocols

Every path to Game Over requires strict adherence to safety protocols:

1. **Tap Early, Tap Often** - In training, tap before pain or danger
2. **Release Immediately** - Honor all tap signals instantly
3. **Progressive Pressure** - Apply submissions gradually (3-5 seconds minimum)
4. **Know the Signals** - Recognize verbal, hand, and foot taps
5. **Partner Check** - Ensure partner is okay after every submission

For detailed safety information, see individual submission pages.

</section>
