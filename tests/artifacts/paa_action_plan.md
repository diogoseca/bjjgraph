Ashi Garami positions involve significant injury risk, particularly from heel hooks

- Practice with controlled resistance, never explosive force

of these leg control positions.

### What is the difference between Ashi Garami and 50-50?
50-50 Guard is a specific type of Ashi Garami featuring bilateral leg entanglement with
symmetrical control where both players have similar offensive and defensive positions.
Ashi Garami is the broader family of leg entanglement positions; 50-50 is one variation
within it, alongside Outside Ashi, Inside Ashi, Cross Ashi, and Saddle.

### What is Saddle position vs Ashi Garami?
Saddle (formerly called "Honey Hole") is the apex position within the Ashi Garami family,
offering maximum leg control and heel exposure. It's the most dominant variation of
Ashi Garami, not a separate position system. Think of it as the "mount" of leg
entanglements.

### Is Ashi Garami good for beginners?
Beginners should approach Ashi Garami cautiously. Start with Outside Ashi and straight
ankle locks before progressing to heel hooks. Success rates for beginners are 40%
retention and 15% submission probability. Understanding leg lock safety protocols and
tap-early mentality is essential. Avoid heel hooks until blue/purple belt with
experienced instruction.
