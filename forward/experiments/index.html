The 2.5D graph EXPERIMENT — NOT DEPLOYED

One map, two floors. Every dual state — Mount Top over Mount Bottom, every attacker over its defender — becomes a pair of nodes at the same equidistant vertical offset, everywhere. Stand on any technique and you already know where its other half lives: a few pixels straight down. The graph stays flat, and reads deep.

Flat worlds that read as deep — CivilizationAge of Empires IIStarCraftDungeon Keeper

Those games never rendered 3D; they rendered rhythm — a uniform projection the eye learns once and then stops seeing. That is the whole trick here: because the offset never varies, the two layers stop being 1,170 pairs to decode and become one rule. The current feel — planets, neurons, the glow — carries over untouched; the layers live inside it.

RULE 1One map, two layers, equidistant.

The offset is global and vertical. Learn it once at any node, and you know it at all 1,170 pairs. Any per-pair variation breaks the projection — that is why the organic layout below is rejected.

RULE 2Slot 0 is the upper layer.

The data has known its layers all along: every strength pair stores top/attacker first. Upper = the side with initiative; each orb wears its own side's color (sv), already computed by the calibration work.

RULE 3Neutral lives on the midline.

Standing, Clinch, and the 297 symmetric singles sit between the layers. Their neutrality stops being a footnote and becomes visible geography.

Evidence

The fixed prototype is the ancestor of the paradigm: uniform offset, wrong angle. Rotate its 25° grain to vertical and the two layers appear. These are real renders of the 2,637-node build (1,170 pairs + 297 singles), live behind ?dual=fixed.

The rejected alternative: force (organic per-pair axes) — kept for the record

Each pair finding its own axis reads beautifully in isolation and destroys the layer illusion in aggregate: 1,170 orientations is 1,170 things to decode, where 2.5D's whole power is decoding one.

The ten questions, re-answered by the paradigm

Three dissolve outright; the rest get a recommendation stated first, with the alternatives and their costs one click below. Every verdict follows from the three rules above.

Q4 · Layout strategyDISSOLVED

Fixed, rotated vertical. "Equidistant" is the paradigm — there is nothing left to choose.

Force dies by Rule 1. The one refinement over the prototype: the offset axis becomes 90° (screen-down = the bottom player, a pun the sport hands us for free), and the bottom layer takes a subtle depth cue — see N1.

Q3 · Pair tie stylingDISSOLVED

No line. Verticality is the tie. A shared elliptical site-glow holds the two orbs as one place.

alternatives considered
  • Ordinary link line (today): reads as a playable edge — a lie; you cannot "transition" from Mount Top to Mount Bottom by drawing on the map.
  • Heavier/shorter tie: still a line, still a claimed edge, plus 1,170 new strokes of clutter.
  • Chosen — site-glow: zero new lines, keeps the planet/neuron feel (a nebula holding two bodies), brightens on focus.
Q6 · Symmetric techniquesDISSOLVED

Singles stay single, on the midline. The layer geometry gives "no role split" a place instead of a footnote.

alternatives considered
  • Fabricate mirror pairs: claims a role split the data does not author — the exact sin the role-correctness work spent v1.103.0 undoing.
  • Chosen — midline placement: neutral positions (Standing, Clinch) already behave this way as single nodes; the 297 symmetric techniques join them and the map becomes self-explaining.
Q1 · Old share links & stored listsRECOMMENDED

A hub ordinal lights the whole site — both orbs — anchored on the upper.

why, and the alternative

An old link meant "this technique, as a place." Under 2.5D the place is the pair; lighting one floor of it would silently reinterpret a coach's class. Primary-only was the alternative — rejected because the sender never chose a side, and fidelity to what was shared is the share system's first law. New captures are side-precise (Q10), so ambiguity ends with the legacy codes. The successors map ships hub → {upper, lower} and the camera frames the site.

Q2 · Defender edgesRECOMMENDED

The static web belongs to the upper layer. The lower layer borrows edges only when you stand on it.

why, and the alternatives

2.5D games draw the roads once — units at different heights share them. Mirroring every edge doubles the web and buries both layers. Fully edge-light forever (today's prototype) makes the bottom layer feel dead. The middle path: at rest the lower orbs ride their sites quietly; when a bottom node takes focus, its real options — escapes, sweeps, the dealt hand — light from it, which is exactly when a player needs them and never sooner.

Q5 · Partner under focusRECOMMENDED

The halo encloses the site; the partner stays co-lit and dimmer, wearing a side kicker (TOP / BOTTOM), never a repeated name.

why, and the alternatives

Excluding the partner from the halo hides the floor below you — the one thing 2.5D exists to show. Full co-light blurs which side you are on. Dim co-presence is the basement with its light on: visible, unambiguous, and clicking it becomes the most natural perspective-flip control the app has ever had (see N2). The halo grows taller, not rounder — it holds a site now, not a dot.

Q7 · Focus sets — Systems & listsRECOMMENDED

Light exactly the captured sides. The site-glow already shows the geography without diluting the selection.

why

A System teaches techniques from a side; a coach's class is sides. Co-lighting partners would answer "where is this" at the cost of "what did the coach pick" — and the share system's canon (qualified names, exact fidelity) has always chosen the second. The unlit partner inside a lit site is not a gap; it is information.

Q8 · PayloadRECOMMENDED

Compact pair encoding — a lower orb is derivable, so it is never shipped whole. Target ≤ 15KB gzip added, not +47.

why this is now cheap

Under Rule 1 a lower member is its twin plus a constant: same position + the global offset, role-flipped strength (sv is the other slot of a pair the wire already carries), id derivable by suffix. The v1.108.0 wire diet built exactly this muscle — compact on the wire, expanded at the top of ingest() — so pairs ride the same pattern. The naive +47KB was the cost of pretending each floor is a stranger.

Q9 · First impressionRECOMMENDED

Keep the draw math untouched; land the camera on the drawn floor.

why

The weighted start already picks a position by real traffic and a side by coin flip — one RNG draw each, and every rigged replay depends on that shape. The paradigm needs no new math: the same coin flip now has an address, and the opening flight descending onto the upper or lower orb makes the side you were dealt legible for the first time. Zero replay risk, pure gain.

Q10 · Capture semanticsRECOMMENDED

Capture the side. Never normalize to the pair.

why

"We worked half guard bottom" is the class. Normalizing a coach's + to the pair would destroy precisely what the qualified-names canon protects — what was actually taught. The recipient still sees the geography: their teammate's side arrives lit inside its site, partner dim beside it. Legacy pair-level ids keep resolving per Q1.

What the paradigm newly asks — two small calls, answered

N1 · How strong is the depth cue?RECOMMENDED

Barely there: lower orbs at ~0.95 scale and one shade dimmer. The "half" in 2.5D is a whisper.

AoE2's far tiles are not small — they are slightly small. Anything stronger reads as importance ranking ("bottom players matter less"), which the sport — where sweeps and half of all submissions live underneath — flatly contradicts.

N2 · Clicking the partnerRECOMMENDED

It switches sides — the same confirmed roll-from-here flow, seeded on the twin.

The most requested mental move in the sport is "now think of it from the bottom." Under 2.5D that is one click, one floor down, through a seam that already exists (confirmPlayFrom asks first, so a live roll is never discarded by accident).
