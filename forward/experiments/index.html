The 2.5D graph PROJECTION C — TRUE ISOMETRIC, CHOSENNOT DEPLOYED

The map's x, y is the ground plane. z is the player axis — top above, bottom below, straight up off the ground. The world is viewed in true isometric: all three axes at 120° on screen, x running toward the lower-right corner, y toward the lower-left, z pointing up. The owner's call, rendered below over the real 2,637 nodes.

Flat worlds that read as deep — CivilizationAge of Empires IIStarCraftDungeon Keeper

Orthographic, like all of them: nothing shrinks with distance, so world→screen is one fixed matrix — not a 3D engine. Baked once at emit time, which is why the camera, hit-testing and every renderer downstream run unchanged:

sx = (x − y) · cos 30°    sy = (x + y) · sin 30° − z·H

RULE 1One ground plane. z is the player axis.

The offset is global: +H up for slot 0, −H down for slot 1, everywhere. Learn it once at any node and you know it at all 1,170 pairs — the uniformity IS the projection.

RULE 2Slot 0 is the sky side.

The data has known its layers all along: every strength pair stores top/attacker first. Above the ground = the side with initiative; each orb wears its own side's color (sv), and the underworld runs a shade darker.

RULE 3Neutral sits on the ground.

Standing, Clinch, and the 297 symmetric singles live at z=0, on the plane itself. Their neutrality stops being a footnote and becomes terrain.

Projection C, rendered over the real graph

These are real frames of the 2,637-node build (1,170 pairs + 297 singles) through the iso matrix, live behind ?dual=iso. Pairs stack vertically — in the mid frame, Closed Guard reads as two orbs, the upper one red because being on top inside someone's closed guard is genuinely a bad place to be. Ground grid, shadows and underworld tone are the next step (the site-rendering pass); this is the geometry, honest.

For the record: the paths not taken — flat-era strategies and projections A/B

Projection A (tilt-only, no rotation) kept the learned geography exactly but reads as a squashed version of today, not a new world — safe, and rejected for it. Projection B (classic 2:1) is the same rotation family as C with a harder squash; C's 120° symmetry is cleaner. The two flat-era strategies below predate the paradigm: fixed (25° screen offset) was its ancestor; force (organic per-pair axes) dies by Rule 1 — 1,170 orientations is 1,170 things to decode, where 2.5D's whole power is decoding one.

The ten questions, answered under the paradigm

Three dissolve; the rest carry a recommendation stated first, alternatives one click below. Every verdict follows from the three rules.

Q4 · Layout strategyDISSOLVED

Projection C — chosen by the owner. Ground plane through the iso matrix, pairs split ±H along z. Nothing left to choose.

One consequence to hold: the learned map rotates 45° and compresses. That is the deliberate price of a new world — the overview above shows what it buys.

Q3 · Pair tie stylingDISSOLVED

No line. The ground shadow at z=0 is the tie. A floating orb over its shadow is the universal height cue.

alternatives considered
  • Ordinary link line (flat era): reads as a playable edge — a lie; you cannot "transition" from Mount Top to Mount Bottom by drawing on the map.
  • Heavier/shorter tie: still a claimed edge, plus 1,170 strokes of clutter.
  • Chosen — shadow + shaft: the shared shadow ellipse on the plane, a faint vertical shimmer on focus. Civ never draws a rope from a hilltop to its foot.
Q6 · Symmetric techniquesDISSOLVED

Singles stay single, ON the ground. z=0 gives "no role split" a place instead of a footnote.

alternatives considered
  • Fabricate mirror pairs: claims a role split the data does not author — the sin the role-correctness work spent v1.103.0 undoing.
  • Chosen — ground placement: neutral positions already behave this way; the 297 symmetric techniques join them and the terrain explains itself.
Q1 · Old share links & stored listsRECOMMENDED

A hub ordinal lights the whole site — both orbs — anchored on the sky side.

why, and the alternative

An old link meant "this technique, as a place." Under 2.5D the place is the site; lighting one floor would silently reinterpret a coach's class. Primary-only was rejected because the sender never chose a side, and fidelity to what was shared is the share system's first law. New captures are side-precise (Q10), so the ambiguity ends with the legacy codes. The successors map ships hub → {upper, lower}.

Q2 · Defender edgesRECOMMENDED

The static web is drawn at ground level, owned by the sky side. The underworld borrows edges only when you stand in it.

why, and the alternatives

Iso games draw the roads once — units at different heights share them. Mirroring every edge doubles the web and buries both layers. Permanently edge-light (the flat prototype) makes the underworld feel dead. The middle path: at rest the lower orbs ride their sites quietly; when a bottom node takes focus, its real options — escapes, sweeps, the dealt hand — light from it, exactly when a player needs them and never sooner.

Q5 · Partner under focusRECOMMENDED

The halo holds the whole site; the partner stays co-lit and dimmer, wearing a side kicker (TOP / BOTTOM), never a repeated name.

why, and the alternatives

Excluding the partner hides the floor 2.5D exists to show. Full co-light blurs which side you are on. Dim co-presence is the basement with its light on — and clicking it is the most natural side-switch the app has ever had (N2). The halo grows taller, not rounder: it holds a site now, not a dot.

Q7 · Focus sets — Systems & listsRECOMMENDED

Light exactly the captured sides. The site's shadow already shows the geography without diluting the selection.

why

A System teaches techniques from a side; a coach's class is sides. Co-lighting partners answers "where is this" at the cost of "what did the coach pick" — and the share canon (qualified names, exact fidelity) has always chosen the second. The unlit twin inside a lit site is not a gap; it is information.

Q8 · PayloadRECOMMENDED

Compact pair wire — a lower orb is derivable, so it is never shipped whole. Target ≤ 15KB gzip added, not +47.

why this is now cheap

Under Rule 1 a lower member is its twin plus constants: the same ground point through the same matrix, −2H on screen-y, role-flipped strength (sv is the other slot of a pair the wire already carries), id by suffix. The v1.108.0 wire diet built exactly this muscle — compact on the wire, expanded at the top of ingest() — so pairs ride the same pattern.

Q9 · First impressionRECOMMENDED

Draw math untouched; the camera lands on the drawn floor.

why

The weighted start already picks a position by real traffic and a side by coin flip — one RNG draw each, and every rigged replay depends on that shape. The paradigm needs no new math: the same coin flip now has an address, and the opening flight descending onto the sky orb or down through the plane to the underworld makes the dealt side legible for the first time. Zero replay risk, pure gain.

Q10 · Capture semanticsRECOMMENDED

Capture the side. Never normalize to the pair.

why

"We worked half guard bottom" is the class. Normalizing a coach's + to the site would destroy precisely what the qualified-names canon protects — what was actually taught. The recipient still sees the geography: the taught side arrives lit inside its site, twin dim beside it. Legacy pair-level ids keep resolving per Q1.

What the paradigm newly asks — two small calls, answered

N1 · The underworld's toneRECOMMENDED

Below z=0 runs one shade darker and slightly cooler — never smaller.

Dungeon Keeper's underground is a place, not a lesser copy. Scale attenuation would read as importance ranking ("bottom players matter less"), which the sport — where sweeps and half of all submissions live underneath — flatly contradicts. Tone says beneath; size says worse. We want beneath.

N2 · Clicking the partnerRECOMMENDED

It switches sides — the same confirmed roll-from-here flow, seeded on the twin.

The most requested mental move in the sport is "now think of it from the bottom." Under 2.5D that is one click, straight down through the plane, via a seam that already exists (confirmPlayFrom asks first, so a live roll is never discarded by accident).

The build sequence

  1. Geometry — DONE.

    The iso variant is emitted (scripts/prototype_dual_pair_layout.py) and rendered above; ?dual=iso is live in the prototype build.

  2. Site rendering.

    Ground shadows, the faint iso grid, underworld tone below z=0, side kickers, halo-holds-the-site, partner click = side switch, starfield parallax (background only — node layers never shear).

  3. Compact pair wire.

    Emit sky members + derivation constants; synthesize the underworld at ingest (the v1.108.0 pattern). Measure against the ≤15KB gzip target.

  4. Ordinal cutover.

    Mint member ordinals, retire hub ordinals behind the committed successors map — every WhatsApp link ever posted keeps resolving (Q1 behavior).

  5. Sign-off gate.

    Everything stays behind ?dual until the owner plays it and calls it. Then the flag flips to default and the flat layout retires.

Playing it now — two commands: npm run prototype:dual (writes the layout payloads), then restart the dev server with npm run serve — a RESTART is required, because the route that serves these payloads lives in dev-serve.mjs and a server already running predates it. Then open /?dual=iso (also fixed, force).

The payloads are 2.2MB each and deliberately never enter the shipped tree — the dev server maps the URL straight to tests/artifacts/dualpair/payloads/, so the deployed artifact stays byte-identical and the payload budget is untouched. If they are missing, the flag degrades to the standard graph and says so in the console; a prototype flag never breaks the app. Engineering record: docs/DualPairMigration.md §9–11.
